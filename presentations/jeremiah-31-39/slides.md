# Hear. Act. Hope.

<p class="subtitle">What do we do when God's word is hard to accept?</p>

<p v-click class="sample-answer"><strong>One possible answer:</strong> We can hear the words carefully, seek help to act, and trust the Lord’s promise of forgiveness and renewal.</p>

<p class="eyebrow">Jeremiah 36:1–3</p>

# Why write<br>the warning?

The Lord hopes the people will hear, turn, and be forgiven.

<p class="question">What purpose do you find in verse 3?</p>

<p v-click class="sample-answer"><strong>One possible answer:</strong> The warning gives the people a chance to turn and receive forgiveness (Jeremiah 36:3).</p>

# The king burns<br>the scroll.

Some officials urge him to stop. He keeps burning it.

<p class="question">What can the king destroy? What remains?</p>

<p v-click class="sample-answer"><strong>One possible answer:</strong> Jehoiakim burns a copy of the scroll. The Lord’s word is spoken and written again (Jeremiah 36:27–32).</p>

<p class="eyebrow">Jeremiah 36:27–32</p>

# “Take thee again<br>another roll.”

Baruch writes the words again, with more added.

<p class="question">What does faithful persistence look like here?</p>

<p v-click class="sample-answer"><strong>One possible answer:</strong> Jeremiah obeys the Lord’s direction, and Baruch writes the words again (Jeremiah 36:28, 32).</p>

<p class="eyebrow">Jeremiah 37:17; 38:14–20</p>

# “Is there any word<br>from the Lord?”

Zedekiah asks. Jeremiah answers. The king names his fear.

<p class="question">What holds Zedekiah back?</p>

<p v-click class="sample-answer"><strong>One possible answer:</strong> Yes. Jeremiah warns of Babylon's victory; Zedekiah says he fears the Judeans who have gone over to the Chaldeans (Jeremiah 37:17; 38:19).</p>

<p class="eyebrow">Jeremiah 38:7–13</p>

# Rags. Ropes. Courage.

Ebed-melech speaks up, gathers helpers, and pulls Jeremiah from the cistern.

<p class="question">What does he do with the help he has?</p>

<p v-click class="sample-answer"><strong>One possible answer:</strong> He speaks to the king, gathers helpers, and uses rags and ropes to lift Jeremiah out (Jeremiah 38:7–13).</p>

# A covenant<br>on the heart.

The Lord promises to write His law within His people and forgive them.

<p v-click class="sample-answer"><strong>One possible answer:</strong> The Lord promises to write His law within His people and forgive their iniquity (Jeremiah 31:33–34).</p>

The Lord promises a future shaped by righteousness.

<p class="eyebrow">Lamentations 1:1–2; 3:21–26</p>

# Grief speaks.<br>Hope remembers.

The city is mourned. The Lord's mercies remain.

<p class="question">What does hope sound like inside a lament?</p>

<p v-click class="sample-answer"><strong>One possible answer:</strong> Hope remembers the Lord’s mercy and faithfulness while still telling the truth about grief (Lamentations 3:21–23).</p>

# Competing voices.<br>A later choice.

<p class="small">Jeremiah 27–28: Hananiah and the yoke.<br>Jeremiah 40–43: the later journey to Egypt.</p>

<p class="question">How does each story test willingness to hear?</p>

<p v-click class="sample-answer"><strong>One possible answer:</strong> Both stories ask whether people will accept the Lord’s word when it conflicts with the outcome they want.</p>
