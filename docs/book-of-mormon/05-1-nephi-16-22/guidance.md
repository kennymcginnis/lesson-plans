# 1 Nephi 16 - A New Bow and a Willing Response

## Hard words and a hard journey

The chapter opens with disagreement about Nephi's teaching. Read 1 Nephi 16:1-5 and notice the invitation to keep the commandments. A truthful correction can be uncomfortable, but discomfort alone does not prove an accusation is true. Our own counsel still needs accuracy, humility, and care.

[Read 1 Nephi 16:1-10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/1-ne/16?lang=eng&id=p1-p10#p1)

The family receives a ball of curious workmanship at Lehi's tent. Guidance comes as a gift, but the record does not describe an effortless journey. Marriage, work, travel, and the need for food remain part of their lives.

## When the bow breaks

Nephi's steel bow breaks, and the other bows have lost their spring. Hunger and fatigue affect the whole household. Even Lehi murmurs. Nephi responds by making a wooden bow and an arrow and asking his father where he should go for food.

[Read 1 Nephi 16:18-32](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/1-ne/16?lang=eng&id=p18-p32#p18)

He does not merely wait for equipment to appear. Nor does he assume that his own resourcefulness makes guidance unnecessary. Making the bow and inquiring of the Lord belong together. His question also makes room for Lehi to turn back to the Lord rather than permanently defining him by his discouragement.

## Small means

> "And thus we see that by small means the Lord can bring about great things."

1 Nephi 16:29, excerpt.

Verse 28 associates the ball's operation with faith, diligence, and heed. Alma 37:38-47 later identifies the instrument as the Liahona and explicitly applies its guidance to attending to Christ's words.

[Read Alma 37:38-47](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/37?lang=eng&id=p38-p47#p38)

Small acts of attention can matter: asking for help, making a repair, taking counsel, or resuming prayer. They are not a formula guaranteeing that every difficulty will be quickly solved.

## Grief is also part of the account

Ishmael's death and his daughters' mourning follow in verses 34-39. Read their loss with compassion. The threats and rebellion that follow are not the same thing as grieving. Do not use the story to suggest that sorrow itself is disobedience.

## Questions to sit with

- How could Nephi's response apply to a present problem without pretending it is easy?
- Is there something within your control that could be your way of making a new bow?
- How might you include the Lord and other people in dealing with the challenge?
- What small source of guidance have you been overlooking?
