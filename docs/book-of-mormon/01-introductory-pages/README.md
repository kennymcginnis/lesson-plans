# Another Testament of Jesus Christ

Week 1 - Introductory Pages of the Book of Mormon

> What would it mean to read this book in order to come closer to Jesus Christ?

## Lesson notes

The title page names the book's audience and purposes. The introduction directs us toward the Savior and invites us to read, ponder, and pray. The testimonies of the witnesses describe what they saw and the responsibility they felt to declare it.

Read these pages as invitations to thoughtful study, not merely material to finish before the narrative begins. Notice the difference between a witness's report, a promise to readers, and the response each of us is invited to make.

## A 25-minute class

1. **Find the purpose, 4 minutes:** Read the title page's final paragraph. Identify what the book says about Christ, covenants, and remembering God's dealings.
2. **Read to come closer, 7 minutes:** Discuss the introduction and one small way to live what we learn. Connect prayerful reading with pondering rather than a reading quota alone.
3. **Listen to the witnesses, 8 minutes:** Compare the Three and Eight Witnesses' statements. Ask what each reports and why the differences matter.
4. **Begin a pattern, 6 minutes:** Read Moroni 10:3-5, choose a realistic study practice, and close with testimony. Give time for questions and a private response.

## Scriptures to emphasize

Prioritize the title page, the introduction, the testimonies of the Three and Eight Witnesses, and Moroni 10:3-5. Joseph Smith's testimony and the brief explanation help readers understand the account of the book's coming forth and its record structure.

## How the modern teachings connect

The book's own introductory texts are the foundation. Modern teachings are identified by source and relationship to the passage. A testimony of the book need not be presented as an answer to every historical or textual question, and a sincere question should not be treated as proof that someone has refused to believe.
