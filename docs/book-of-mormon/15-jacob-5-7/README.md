# The Lord Labors with Us

Week 15 - Jacob 5-7

> What do the Lord's repeated efforts in the vineyard teach us about His desire to save?

## Lesson notes

Jacob quotes Zenos's allegory of the olive trees, then invites his listeners to repent and cleave to God. The image is first about Israel and the Lord's covenant work. It can also help us reflect personally on receiving His care and joining His labor.

The Lord nourishes, grieves, acts again, and works alongside His servants. Good fruit matters, but the account is not a method for ranking people by their circumstances or explaining every hardship as pruning sent by God.

## A 25-minute class

1. **Identify the image, 4 minutes:** Read Jacob 5:3 and introduce the symbol table. Begin with Israel before moving to personal application.
2. **See the Lord's heart, 8 minutes:** Read Jacob 5:41, 47, 51. Discuss what His grief and continued efforts reveal about His purpose.
3. **Join the work, 7 minutes:** Read Jacob 5:61-72. Find what the servants do and notice that the Lord labors with them.
4. **Cleave unto God, 6 minutes:** Read Jacob 6:4-5 and briefly connect Jacob 7's witness of Christ. Choose one response and close with testimony.

## Scriptures to emphasize

Prioritize Jacob 5:3, 41, 47, 51, 72, 75 and 6:4-5. Read Jacob 7:10-12, 23 as the chapter's witness of Christ and its description of renewed peace and scripture study.

## How the modern teachings connect

The allegory and Jacob's explanation are the primary sources. Distinguish a symbol explicitly identified in the text from a useful devotional comparison. The vineyard's geography and cultivation do not establish a simple formula connecting a person's circumstances, righteousness, and visible success.
