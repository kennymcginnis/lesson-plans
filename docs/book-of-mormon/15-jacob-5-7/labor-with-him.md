# Jacob 5 - The Lord's Labor and Grief

## Find the repeated actions

Scan Jacob 5:4-7, 11, 47, 61-72. Notice the verbs: nourish, dig, graft, prune, dung, and burn. These are agricultural actions in an allegory, not a literal program for how we should treat people.

[Read Jacob 5:4-11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/jacob/5?lang=eng&id=p4-p11#p4)

[Read Jacob 5:61-72](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/jacob/5?lang=eng&id=p61-p72#p61)

What do the repeated efforts suggest about the Lord's involvement? The trees are not abandoned after one intervention. Nourishing and changing conditions belong to a long work of preservation.

## It grieveth me

Read verses 7, 11, 32, 46-47, 51, and 66 for the recurring expression of grief over losing the trees. The Lord's question is especially moving:

> 41. "What could I have done more for my vineyard?"

Jacob 5:41, excerpt.

[Read Jacob 5:41-51](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/jacob/5?lang=eng&id=p41-p51#p41)

The passage portrays concern and sorrow, not pleasure in destruction. The servant asks for more time, and the work continues. Mercy is not indifference to what the vineyard produces; it is part of the Lord's persistent effort to save.

Do not infer that every painful event in a person's life is a particular act of pruning chosen by God. The image teaches His care and purposes; it does not explain every injury or excuse another person's harmful choices.

## The Lord works alongside the servants

> 72. "The Lord of the vineyard labored also with them."

Jacob 5:72, excerpt.

The servants labor with their might and follow His direction. They do not replace Him, and the work does not rest entirely on any one servant. Verse 75 connects their labor with shared joy in the fruit.

[Read Jacob 5:70-75](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/jacob/5?lang=eng&id=p70-p75#p70)

For us, participation may include teaching, inviting, repairing relationships, welcoming people, or temple and family history service. Invite rather than coerce. The people we serve are not projects or evidence of our success.

## Questions to sit with

- Which action in the vineyard helps you understand a way the Lord cares for His people?
- What difference does the Lord's grief make to the way you read the warnings?
- How does verse 72 change the feeling that everything depends on you?
- What is one small part of the work you could join with others this week?
