# Alma 9-10 - Remember and Listen

## Who are you, and who is God?

The people question Alma's authority and ask why they should believe one witness. Alma's response calls them to remember their ancestors, deliverance, revelation, and the blessings they have received.

[Read Alma 9:1-10, 20-23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/9?lang=eng&id=p1-p10,p20-p23#p1)

This audience is described as a people who have received much. Do not assume that everyone who asks about God has the same background or is deliberately forgetting. As a personal reflection, however, we can ask whether help has been present in our lives in ways we did not recognize at the time.

## I would not hear

Amulek identifies himself as a man with family ties, reputation, and resources. Then he corrects his own account of not knowing God's power:

> 6. "I was called many times and I would not hear; therefore I knew concerning these things, yet I would not know."

Alma 10:6, excerpt.

[Read Alma 10:1-11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/10?lang=eng&id=p1-p11#p1)

His honesty matters. He does not present himself as someone who has always listened perfectly. He remembers a turning point when he obeyed the angel's direction, returned home, and received Alma.

The account dates that experience to the fourth day of the seventh month in the tenth year of the judges. It does not identify the day with a modern calendar holiday. The point for the lesson is his remembered response, not an assumed calendar equivalence.

## The prayers of the righteous

Amulek teaches that the people's preservation is connected with the prayers of the righteous. Read Alma 10:22-23 alongside Alma's prayer in 8:10.

[Read Alma 10:22-23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/10?lang=eng&id=p22-p23#p22)

The relationship invites us to consider that prayer may matter in ways not immediately visible. The text does not specify that Alma alone caused the city's preservation or let us calculate how many prayers produce an outcome.

Pray for people without using prayer as a substitute for appropriate help or as a way to claim ownership of their choices. An unanswered question about an outcome is not proof that prayer was worthless.

## Questions to sit with

- What does remembering add to a discussion of who God is?
- How can we acknowledge past resistance without giving up on future change?
- What can Amulek's plain statement "I obeyed" teach us about a turning point?
- How can we pray faithfully while remaining humble about what we cannot see?
