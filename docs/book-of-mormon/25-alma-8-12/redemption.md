# Alma 11-12 - The Plan of Redemption

## Teaching amid confrontation

Alma 11 places Amulek's testimony within a dispute involving lawyers, money, and attempts to entangle him. His refusal of an inducement to deny God is part of the setting, not the whole lesson. Listen for what he teaches about Jesus Christ.

[Read Alma 11:20-46](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/11?lang=eng&id=p20-p46#p20)

Amulek teaches that Christ does not save people in their sins. Redemption is not a declaration that wrongdoing does not matter. The same teaching promises resurrection to all, distinguishing the universal restoration of body and spirit from a person's response to the gospel and judgment.

## A complete restoration of body and spirit

Verses 42-44 describe the reunion of spirit and body and judgment before God. The passage includes old and young, bond and free, male and female, wicked and righteous. No group is beyond the reach of Christ's victory over death.

This hope should deepen respect for mortal bodies and for people facing illness or disability. It does not justify treating their present lives as less valuable or claiming to know every detail of their future experience.

## Receive the portion given

In Alma 12, Alma explains that understanding grows with heed and diligence. A hardened heart receives less; a receptive heart can receive more.

[Read Alma 12:9-11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/12?lang=eng&id=p9-p11#p9)

The invitation is to receive and live the word, not to seek secret status through hidden information. Nor does an intellectual question or mental-health struggle prove a hard heart. Listen carefully to a person's actual concern.

## Time to prepare

> 24. "This life became a probationary state; a time to prepare to meet God."

Alma 12:24, excerpt.

[Read Alma 12:22-37](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/12?lang=eng&id=p22-p37#p22)

The plan includes teaching, repentance, and the invitation to receive mercy through the Only Begotten. Mortality is not merely a waiting room before resurrection. Our response to God's word can begin in the responsibilities and relationships of today.

## Questions to sit with

- How is being saved from sin different from having sin declared harmless?
- What difference does the universality of resurrection make to the way we value people?
- What could receiving more light look like through living what we already understand?
- What is one way to prepare to meet God that is both practical and hopeful?
