# God's Intent Is to Bring You Home

Week 32 - Alma 39-42

> How does knowing God's intent change the way we receive correction and the invitation to repent?

## Lesson notes

Alma speaks to Corianton about serious wrongdoing, resurrection, restoration, justice, and mercy. The conversation does not end with rejection. It ends with an invitation to preach truthfully so that others may receive the great plan of mercy.

Read the correction within that whole movement. God's plan does not excuse harm, but neither is it designed to make return impossible. Jesus Christ makes repentance, resurrection, and reconciliation possible.

## A 25-minute class

1. **Name God's intent, 4 minutes:** Read Alma 42:27 and the brief teaching from Patrick Kearon. Ask what it means to be invited rather than kept out.
2. **Correction for our good, 7 minutes:** Read Alma 39:7-13. Discuss accountability and practical change without asking anyone to disclose personal sins.
3. **Justice and mercy, 8 minutes:** Read Alma 42:15, 22-25. Keep the Atonement at the center rather than presenting mercy as ignoring the truth.
4. **A future still open, 6 minutes:** Read Alma 42:29-31. Choose one response of repentance or service and close with testimony. Use Alma 40's resurrection discussion for preparation or follow-up.

## Scriptures to emphasize

Prioritize Alma 39:7-13; 40:5, 11; 41:10; and 42:15, 23, 27, 29-31. These passages name both responsibility and the Savior's provision.

## How the modern teachings connect

Patrick Kearon's April 2024 address is verified and directly develops the plan's intent with references to Alma 42. Illustrative parent-child dialogue is not presented as a real conversation or as Alma's words. The studies preserve the source's hopeful direction while distinguishing the seriousness of wrongdoing from a person's enduring worth.
