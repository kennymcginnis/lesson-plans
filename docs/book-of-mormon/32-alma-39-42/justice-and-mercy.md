# Alma 41-42 - Justice, Mercy, and God's Intent

## Restoration is not an escape from responsibility

Alma 41 connects restoration with works and desires. It warns against assuming that remaining in wrongdoing can simply be transformed into happiness at the end.

> "Wickedness never was happiness."

Alma 41:10, excerpt.

[Read Alma 41:3-15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/41?lang=eng&id=p3-p15#p3)

This is a teaching about the direction of a life, not a claim that every unhappy person is wicked or that every pleasant feeling proves a choice is right. Mental illness, grief, and injustice cannot be diagnosed by reversing this sentence.

## The names of the plan

Find the descriptions in Alma 42:8, 13, 15-16: the great plan of happiness, redemption, and mercy. Each name reveals something about God's purpose, while the chapter also insists that choices and accountability matter.

[Read Alma 42:8-16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/42?lang=eng&id=p8-p16#p8)

> "Mercy cometh because of the atonement."

Alma 42:23, excerpt.

[Read Alma 42:22-27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/42?lang=eng&id=p22-p27#p22)

Justice and mercy are not competing personalities in God. Verse 15 centers their relationship in Christ's Atonement. Mercy does not require calling harm harmless; it provides a real way for the penitent to be redeemed.

## Patrick Kearon - God's Intent Is to Bring You Home

**Verified teaching with references to Alma 42.** Elder Kearon, of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, describes watching a traffic officer repeatedly turn cars away. He contrasts that scene with God's plan:

> "Our Father's beautiful plan, even His 'fabulous' plan, is designed to bring you home, not to keep you out."

["God's Intent Is to Bring You Home," April 2024](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2024/04/45kearon?lang=eng)

He connects happiness, redemption, mercy, and salvation with what the Father intends to give. This does not mean choices no longer matter. His message encourages us to trust the Father's purpose and accept Christ's invitation, not remain unchanged.

## Come freely

Alma 42:27 invites whoever will come to partake of the waters of life freely and says those who will not come are not compelled. The invitation respects agency. We should extend it without coercion or making someone else our project.

Verses 29-31 then return to Corianton's response: let concern lead to repentance, do not deny justice to excuse sin, and go teach truthfully. The plan of mercy leaves a real next step open.

[Read Alma 42:29-31](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/42?lang=eng&id=p29-p31#p29)

## Questions to sit with

- What do the names of the plan teach us about God's intent?
- How is mercy through Christ different from pretending that choices have no consequences?
- What changes when we see repentance as an invitation to return rather than proof that God wants us excluded?
- What response can you make willingly, without trying to control someone else's decision?
