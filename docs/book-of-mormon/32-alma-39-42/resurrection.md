# Alma 40 - What We Know and What God Knows

## Alma takes the question seriously

Corianton is troubled about resurrection. Alma does not answer only with an instruction to stop asking. He explains what has been made known to him and identifies questions of timing that he leaves with God.

> "It sufficeth me to know that this is the case—that there is a time appointed that all shall rise from the dead."

Alma 40:5, excerpt.

[Read Alma 40:1-10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/40?lang=eng&id=p1-p10#p1)

This distinction is a model for careful teaching: be clear about the central promise and honest about details not established. Faith does not require us to fill every gap with speculation.

## Between death and resurrection

Alma describes the spirits of all people being taken home to God who gave them life, then describes different conditions of rest and darkness. Read verses 11-14 together rather than treating one phrase as proof that every person's final judgment is already complete.

[Read Alma 40:11-14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/40?lang=eng&id=p11-p14#p11)

The passage concerns the state between death and resurrection. It does not give us authority to declare a particular deceased person's condition. Other scriptures, including D&C 138, provide further teaching about gospel opportunity among the dead.

## Restoration of the person

Alma 40:23 describes the soul restored to the body and the body to the soul. Resurrection is not merely a continuing memory of someone. It is God's overcoming of death through Christ.

[Read Alma 40:21-26](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/40?lang=eng&id=p21-p26#p21)

The hope of a restored body does not make a disabled, ill, or aging body less worthy of care now. Nor does knowing the doctrine erase the pain of bereavement. Assurance and grief can coexist.

## Questions to sit with

- What does Alma state confidently, and what does he leave to God?
- How could that distinction improve the way we answer difficult gospel questions?
- What does resurrection hope teach us about the value of the whole person?
- How can we share hope without claiming to know a loved one's unseen circumstances?
