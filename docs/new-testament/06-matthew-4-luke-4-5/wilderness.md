# Matthew 4; Luke 4-5 - Make Room for God

## Before the public ministry

Matthew and Luke place the wilderness experience after Jesus' baptism. Luke describes Him as full of the Holy Ghost and led by the Spirit. The accounts include fasting, hunger, and temptation.

[Read Matthew 4:1-2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/4?lang=eng&id=p1-p2#p1)

[Read Luke 4:1-2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/4?lang=eng&id=p1-p2#p1)

The Joseph Smith Translation of Matthew 4:1 emphasizes going into the wilderness to be with God. Read that as a distinct textual rendering rather than silently substituting it for the KJV. The narrative should not be reduced to seeking temptation as a spiritual exercise.

## He withdrew and prayed

Luke later says that Jesus withdrew into the wilderness and prayed. Quiet communion belongs within an active ministry, not only at its beginning.

[Read Luke 5:15-16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/5?lang=eng&id=p15-p16#p15)

Ask when you last made room to pray without another task competing for attention. A brief quiet moment may be more realistic than a long retreat. People with caregiving responsibilities or crowded homes may need support to find such space.

## Fasting and human needs

The text records Jesus' hunger; it does not tell us that He felt none until His fasting ended. As an application, hunger during an appropriate fast can prompt prayer, but the forty days are not a pattern we should try to reproduce.

Health conditions, pregnancy, medication, age, and other needs affect whether fasting is appropriate. Prayer and devotion remain available when a person cannot abstain from food. Tiredness or hunger can make wise choices harder; rest and nourishment are not enemies of discipleship.

## Questions to sit with

- What do the accounts teach about the Spirit's place in Jesus' preparation?
- How could you make a little room for prayer in your actual circumstances?
- What helps you distinguish a spiritual discipline from neglect of necessary care?
- How can you support someone whose life leaves little quiet space?
