# Matthew 4:3-11 - It Is Written

## Read the invitations and the replies

[Read Matthew 4:3-11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/4?lang=eng&id=p3-p11#p3)

| Temptation | Jesus' response | Scripture behind the reply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Command stones to become bread | Life depends on God's word, not bread alone. | Deuteronomy 8:3 |
| Throw Himself from the temple, invoking promised angelic protection | Do not put the Lord to the test. | Deuteronomy 6:16 |
| Receive kingdoms in exchange for worship | Worship and serve the Lord alone. | Deuteronomy 6:13 |

Luke presents the last two in a different order. Read each Gospel's sequence attentively rather than pretending the order is identical.

## Identity is not something to prove to the tempter

The words "If thou be the Son of God" press Jesus to act on the tempter's terms. His response trusts the Father rather than using divine power to win the challenge.

We can reflect on pressures involving appetite, recognition, and power. These are helpful applications, not an exhaustive explanation of every temptation. Needing food is not itself sinful, and accepting help is not an attempt to obtain something undeserved.

## Scripture can be misused

The temple temptation quotes Psalm 91:11-12. Jesus responds with another passage rather than accepting a misuse of the promise. A quotation's presence does not make an argument faithful to God.

[Read Psalm 91:11-12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ps/91?lang=eng&id=p11-p12#p11)

Trust does not mean creating a danger and insisting that God rescue us. Nor does this account establish that a crowd watched at the temple or that Jesus was offered a specific publicity campaign. Those are imagined illustrations, not reported events.

## Prepare a response

Choose a scripture or hymn that helps you remember Christ, and consider a practical safeguard: step away, ask for help, change an environment, or pause before acting. Memorized words are a resource for discipleship, not a magical guarantee that a difficult habit will disappear.

Jesus' victory leads into ministry and care for others. The point is not self-sufficiency or earning salvation through willpower, but faithful dependence on the Father.

## Questions to sit with

- What does each reply reveal about Jesus' priorities?
- How can we recognize a true statement used for a harmful purpose?
- When does seeking reassurance turn into demanding that God meet our terms?
- What preparation would help you respond wisely to a recurring pressure?
