# Luke 4-5 - Good News and a Call to Follow

## The Spirit of the Lord is upon me

In Nazareth, Jesus reads from Isaiah about good news to the poor, liberty, sight, and the Lord's favor. He declares that the scripture is fulfilled in their hearing.

[Read Luke 4:16-30](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/4?lang=eng&id=p16-p30#p16)

The mission is not simply to display power. It concerns God's saving work among people in need. The mixed and eventually hostile response reminds us that familiar surroundings do not guarantee acceptance. Do not generalize that response to all Jewish people.

## From the nets to discipleship

In Luke 5:1-11, Jesus teaches from Simon's boat and directs him to let down the nets. Simon names their earlier fruitless labor, then acts on Jesus' word. His response to the catch includes awareness of his own unworthiness; Jesus answers with a call.

[Read Luke 5:1-11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/5?lang=eng&id=p1-p11#p1)

The story invites trust and service, not a promise of business success for anyone who repeats Simon's words. Jesus later calls Levi, receives people criticized by others, and compares new wine with old containers. Read those encounters as part of a ministry that calls for a changed response.

[Read Luke 5:27-39](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/5?lang=eng&id=p27-p39#p27)

Matthew 4:18-25 likewise joins calling disciples with teaching and healing. Following Jesus means learning His work, not merely admiring His resistance to temptation.

## Questions to sit with

- Who is named as a recipient of good news in Jesus' reading?
- How does Jesus respond to Simon's sense of inadequacy?
- What would a faithful next step look like without treating your circumstances as identical to Simon's?
- What could you offer in the Savior's work this week?
