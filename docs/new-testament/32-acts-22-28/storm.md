# Acts 27 - Faith and Practical Help in the Storm

## A decision under pressure

Paul warns against continuing the voyage. The centurion gives more weight to the pilot and shipowner, the harbor is judged unsuitable for winter, and a favorable wind seems to support departure. A violent storm follows.

[Read Acts 27:9-20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/acts/27?lang=eng&id=p9-p20#p9)

Consider the decision factors: expertise, convenience, the majority's preference, and apparent conditions. These can influence us too. The lesson is not that experts are generally untrustworthy or that a minority position is automatically inspired. Paul later relies on the sailors' presence and practical skills.

## Identity, purpose, and trust

> "The angel of God, whose I am, and whom I serve"

Acts 27:23, KJV excerpt.

[Read Acts 27:21-26](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/acts/27?lang=eng&id=p21-p26#p21)

Paul receives assurance that he will stand before Caesar and that the people aboard will survive, though the ship will be lost. His confidence rests on a particular promise, not on denying the storm's severity.

We can reflect on belonging to God without claiming the same prediction for every danger we encounter. A loss of possessions or a difficult detour is not, by itself, proof that God has abandoned someone.

## Stay together, eat, and give thanks

When sailors attempt to leave, Paul tells the centurion that they are needed. He then urges everyone to eat for their health and gives thanks before them. Assurance does not make cooperation, food, or preparation unnecessary.

[Read Acts 27:27-44](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/acts/27?lang=eng&id=p27-p44#p27)

The instruction to remain aboard belongs to this rescue. It must not be used to pressure a person to remain in abuse or danger. Verse 40 describes committing themselves to the sea; a spiritual application about entrusting oneself to God should not replace the nautical wording of the verse.

## Questions to sit with

- Which pressures affect our judgment when a decision is inconvenient?
- How does Paul's assurance differ from pretending nothing will be lost?
- Why do food, gratitude, and teamwork matter in a faith-centered account?
- What practical support can accompany prayer in a crisis you know about?
