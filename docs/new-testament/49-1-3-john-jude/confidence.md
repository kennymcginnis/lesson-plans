## When the heart condemns

First John 3:20 says God is greater than our heart and knows all things. A harsh inner verdict is not necessarily God's verdict. Bring both genuine wrongdoing and anxious self-accusation into a truthful relationship with Him; receive appropriate help where needed.

The statement that perfect love casts out fear belongs to a discussion of confidence in judgment. It should not be used to diagnose anxiety disorders, trauma responses, or ordinary fear as proof that someone has failed to love.

## According to His will

First John 5:14 qualifies prayer with God's will. Do not separate a promise of answered prayer from that relationship and turn it into a guarantee that every request is granted exactly as imagined.

Likewise, the language about anointing and knowledge is not permission to reject all teachers or assume our impressions are infallible. The letter itself teaches, corrects, and calls readers to test spirits in 4:1-3.

## Questions to sit with

- Which promise helps you trust Christ without pretending to be sinless?
- What changes when prayer is understood in relation to His will?
- How can we help someone whose conscience has become dominated by fear?
- What response could deepen fellowship rather than spiritual competition?
