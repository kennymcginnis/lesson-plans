# Learning That Leads to Discipleship

## Therefore, what?

A meaningful conversation about scripture should leave room for a practical question: what will this change? Learning includes understanding, but it also invites trust, repentance, service, and a different way of treating people.

[Read Matthew 11:28-30](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/11?lang=eng&id=p28-p30#p28)

Jesus invites weary people to come, take His yoke, and learn of Him. The invitation is relational, not simply academic. We do not become disciples by mastering information while refusing His help.

### Dallin H. Oaks - The Challenge to Become

**Verified teaching; thematic connection.** Elder Oaks, then of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, teaches that the gospel challenges us to become something, not merely acquire knowledge. He connects conversion with the effect of our thoughts and actions on who we become.

["The Challenge to Become," October 2000](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2000/10/the-challenge-to-become?lang=eng)

## Four ways to learn

| Approach | What it contributes | A practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explanation | Clarifies words, setting, and ideas | Ask what a verse means in context. |
| Exhortation | Invites a response | Identify what the passage calls us to do. |
| Example | Shows a principle in someone's actions | Notice both faithful choices and mistakes. |
| Experience | Helps learning become part of life | Try a sound application and reflect on it. |

These are study tools, not a rigid formula. Experiences should be interpreted with scripture, prayer, and careful judgment rather than assumed to prove every conclusion we draw.

Ask three further questions: is the teaching true, how is it relevant, and what appropriate response can begin now? Urgency need not mean anxiety or impulsive action. Sometimes the next step is listening, preparing, or seeking help.

## Take responsibility without studying alone

Personal responsibility does not mean learning without teachers or community. Prepare a question, read the surrounding passage, listen to other readers, and test an application against Christ's teachings. A class can support learning that continues through the week.

Choose a realistic rhythm and an accessible format. A few verses thoughtfully read can be useful; broad reading also helps us understand the whole narrative. Neither a perfect streak nor a large number of pages measures a person's worth.

## Questions to sit with

- Which of the four approaches do you most often use? Which might deepen your study?
- When has acting on a truth helped you understand it differently?
- What could "learn of me" mean in one relationship this week?
- What is a small practice you can sustain without turning study into a source of shame?
