# Four Gospel Voices

## Compare the openings

Give each group one opening passage. Ask what it emphasizes and what questions it prepares a reader to ask.

| Gospel | Read | Notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Matthew | Matthew 1:1-17 | Jesus within the story of Abraham, David, and Israel. |
| Mark | Mark 1:1-15 | Good news, preparation, baptism, and the beginning of ministry. |
| Luke | Luke 1:1-4 | An orderly account drawing on earlier witnesses and reports. |
| John | John 1:1-18 | The Word, creation, light, and the Word made flesh. |

Matthew, Mark, and Luke are called the Synoptic Gospels because of their shared perspective and substantial overlapping material. John has a distinctive selection and presentation. Imagining different vantage points can be useful, but it is not proof of where each author stood or whether every event was personally observed.

## Difference can help us listen

Matthew often connects Jesus with scripture's fulfillment. Mark's narrative is vivid and action-filled. Luke repeatedly attends to prayer, joy, and people on the margins. John emphasizes signs, extended conversations, and Jesus' relationship with the Father. These are tendencies, not exclusive categories.

Do not say that John omits Jesus' humanity: He becomes flesh, grows weary, weeps, and thirsts. Nor does Mark's attention to suffering deny Christ's divine authority. Read each account's own witness before harmonizing details.

Authorship traditions are important to Christian history, but the precise composition and sources of the Gospels remain subjects of study. Luke's prologue itself gives a model of attending to testimony and careful investigation.

## Questions to sit with

- What would we miss if we had only one of these openings?
- How does a distinctive emphasis help you learn something about Christ?
- Can a difference invite closer reading rather than an immediate forced explanation?
- What will you look for as you begin the Gospels?
