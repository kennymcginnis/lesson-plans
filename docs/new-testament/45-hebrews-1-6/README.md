# Come Boldly to the Throne of Grace

Week 45 - Hebrews 1-6

> What changes when we trust that the One who invites us to God understands our weakness?

## Lesson notes

Hebrews witnesses of the Son's greatness and His solidarity with those He saves. Its comparisons with angels, Moses, priesthood, and sanctuary service lead to invitations: pay attention, hold fast, enter God's rest, and approach the throne of grace.

The work is anonymous. Christian tradition has associated it with Paul, but its authorship, precise audience, and date remain debated. Its rich use of Israel's scriptures is not a reason to disparage Judaism or assume that every recipient had the same biography.

## A 25-minute class

1. **Hear the Son, 4 minutes:** Read Hebrews 1:1-3 and 2:14-18. Notice greatness and shared humanity together.
2. **Come for help, 8 minutes:** Read Hebrews 4:14-16 and 5:7-9. Discuss the Savior's understanding and the purpose of approaching Him.
3. **Respond to the invitation, 7 minutes:** Use the "let us" activity in Hebrews 4 and 6. Identify one response supported by the actual text.
4. **Hold to hope, 6 minutes:** Read Hebrews 6:10-12, 18-20. Choose one faithful next step and close with testimony.

## Scriptures to emphasize

Prioritize Hebrews 1:1-3; 2:14-18; 3:1-6; 4:9-16; 5:7-9; and 6:10-20. Later chapters about covenant, sanctuary, and sacrifice are companion readings, not part of the core chapter range.

## How the modern teachings connect

Read Hebrews' argument about fulfillment in Christ without turning "better" into a ranking of peoples or treating earlier covenant life as meaningless. Modern temple applications are devotional connections, not the literal architectural subject of every passage about entering God's rest.
