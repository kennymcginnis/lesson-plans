# Hebrews 1-3 - The Son Who Shares Our Life

## God has spoken by His Son

Hebrews opens by contrasting earlier prophetic speech with God's speaking in the Son. The Son is described in relation to creation, divine glory, sustaining power, and purification from sins.

[Read Hebrews 1:1-4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/1?lang=eng&id=p1-p4#p1)

The comparisons with angels are an argument about the Son's unique role, not a dismissal of angels' ministry. The chapter ends by describing them as ministering spirits serving God's purposes.

## Greatness does not mean distance

Chapter 2 speaks of Jesus sharing flesh and blood and becoming like His brethren. His suffering and testing are connected with His capacity to help those who are tested.

[Read Hebrews 2:9-18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/2?lang=eng&id=p9-p18#p9)

Read both chapters together. The One who reveals God's glory is not indifferent to mortal experience. This is the foundation of the invitation to approach Him, not merely an impressive claim about His status.

## Consider Christ Jesus

Hebrews 3 calls Jesus the Apostle and High Priest of our profession. The comparison honors Moses as faithful in God's house while distinguishing the Son's role over the house.

[Read Hebrews 3:1-6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/3?lang=eng&id=p1-p6#p1)

The point is not that Moses had no faith or that Jewish people are inferior. Hebrews draws on Israel's scriptures to articulate Christian confidence in the Son. Do not replace that argument with contempt for the people whose scriptures it uses.

## Questions to sit with

- What do the opening verses invite you to know about Jesus?
- How does chapter 2 deepen the picture given in chapter 1?
- Why might divine greatness and experience of suffering matter together?
- What concern could you bring to Him more honestly because He understands mortal life?
