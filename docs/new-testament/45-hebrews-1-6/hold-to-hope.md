# Hebrews 4-6 - Let Us Hold to Hope

## Find the invitations

| Passage | Invitation | A question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hebrews 4:1 | Take seriously the promise of rest | What response am I delaying? |
| Hebrews 4:11 | Labor to enter that rest | What faithful practice supports trust? |
| Hebrews 4:14 | Hold fast our profession | What conviction deserves a steady response? |
| Hebrews 4:16 | Come boldly for mercy and help | What do I need to bring honestly to Christ? |
| Hebrews 6:1 | Go on unto perfection | What growth is possible beyond merely repeating familiar ideas? |

[Read Hebrews 6:1-12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/6?lang=eng&id=p1-p12#p1)

The stern warning in verses 4-6 concerns repudiation after profound participation in the gospel. It should not be used to tell a person with doubts, recurring struggles, or a desire to repent that mercy is permanently closed. The chapter also speaks of confidence in better things, God's remembrance of loving service, and patient hope.

## An anchor for the soul

Verses 13-20 recall God's promise to Abraham and describe hope as an anchor, with Jesus entering beyond the veil as forerunner and High Priest. Our steadiness rests in God's trustworthiness rather than our ability to predict every outcome.

[Read Hebrews 6:13-20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/6?lang=eng&id=p13-p20#p13)

The source's wider theme of a better covenant continues in Hebrews 7:22, 8:6, and 9:11-14. Use these as companion readings when helpful. They develop why Christ's saving ministry accomplishes what ritual patterns anticipated; they are not grounds for scorning an earlier covenant or its faithful people.

## Questions to sit with

- Which invitation helps you identify a realistic next step?
- Why does the chapter name loving service alongside its warning?
- How can hope remain an anchor while questions remain unanswered?
- What would patient faithfulness look like without treating growth as instant perfection?
