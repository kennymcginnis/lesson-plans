# Mark 4; Matthew 8 - Peace amid Fear

## Carest thou not?

Jesus directs the disciples to cross the sea. A storm arises, and they wake Him with a question about whether He cares that they are perishing. He stills the wind and sea; they ask who He is that even these obey Him.

[Read Mark 4:35-41](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/mark/4?lang=eng&id=p35-p41#p35)

The account invites trust in Christ's authority. It also gives voice to a fear many people recognize: does God care when we feel overwhelmed? A difficult crossing does not automatically mean the initial direction was wrong.

This does not prove that every obstacle is deliberately sent to test us or that prayer guarantees immediate calm. Wise action, safety measures, and help from others can belong with faith.

## Authority and unexpected responses

Matthew 8:28-34 describes Christ's authority over demons and the town's request that He depart. A miraculous deliverance does not produce the same response in everyone.

[Read Matthew 8:28-34](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/8?lang=eng&id=p28-p34#p28)

Read this as the Gospel's account of deliverance, not a framework for diagnosing mental illness or disability as possession. People facing illness deserve appropriate care, not speculation about evil spirits.

## Hear the rest of the assignment

Mark 2-3 includes disputes over fasting and Sabbath practice, Jesus' calling of the Twelve, and His description of those who do God's will. Mark 4's seed parables concern hearing, receiving, and the growth of God's kingdom. Luke 7 also includes John's question and the woman's love at Simon's meal. These accounts connect Jesus' authority with how people receive Him.

[Read Mark 4:1-34](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/mark/4?lang=eng&id=p1-p34#p1)

[Read Luke 7:18-50](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/7?lang=eng&id=p18-p50#p18)

## Questions to sit with

- What do the disciples' questions reveal about their fear and growing understanding?
- How can we bring the feeling "Do you care?" honestly into prayer?
- What is one practical response that can accompany trust during a crisis?
- How do the seed parables invite patient discipleship rather than instant results?
