# Matthew 8; Luke 7 - Compassion and Belonging

## I will; be thou clean

The man in Matthew 8:1-4 asks whether Jesus is willing to cleanse him. Jesus touches him and responds. He also instructs him to show himself to the priest and offer the gift Moses commanded.

[Read Matthew 8:1-4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/8?lang=eng&id=p1-p4#p1)

The account shows compassion within a Jewish setting; it does not say that every member of the crowd felt disgust or that Jewish people lacked compassion. Ritual uncleanness is not the same as personal wickedness. We can identify with needing Christ without describing a sick person's body as a moral failure.

## Faith beyond our assumptions

Matthew presents the centurion appealing to Jesus; Luke describes Jewish elders and friends carrying his request. Preserve both presentations. Luke's elders speak warmly of the officer, who helped build their synagogue, so a claim that Jews never interacted with Gentiles would contradict this very story.

[Read Matthew 8:5-13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/8?lang=eng&id=p5-p13#p5)

[Read Luke 7:1-10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/7?lang=eng&id=p1-p10#p1)

The centurion recognizes authority and trusts Jesus' word. Christ's praise challenges assumptions about where faith can be found. It does not transfer superior human worth from one ethnic or religious group to another.

## He saw the widow

Luke 7:11-17 describes Jesus' compassion for a mother whose only son has died. No request from her is recorded before He acts. His words "Weep not" belong to this particular encounter and the life He restores; they are not instructions for us to silence bereaved people.

[Read Luke 7:11-17](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/7?lang=eng&id=p11-p17#p11)

Hope in resurrection can coexist with deep grief. Do not require someone to identify with a particular character or disclose a loss in order to participate.

## Healing and service

Peter's mother-in-law rises and ministers after her fever leaves. This can prompt gratitude expressed in service. It should not become a demand that everyone recovering from illness immediately return to work or care for others without support.

[Read Matthew 8:14-17](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/8?lang=eng&id=p14-p17#p14)

## Questions to sit with

- What does Jesus' willingness teach you about approaching Him?
- What assumptions might keep us from recognizing faith in someone else?
- How can we offer hope without trying to end someone's grief with a sentence?
- What care could you receive or offer this week?
