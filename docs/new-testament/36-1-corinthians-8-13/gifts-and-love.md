# 1 Corinthians 12-13 - Many Gifts, One Body, and Charity

## Given for the common good

> "But the manifestation of the Spirit is given to every man to profit withal."

1 Corinthians 12:7, KJV.

[Read 1 Corinthians 12:4-11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/12?lang=eng&id=p4-p11#p4)

The gifts differ, but the Spirit is the same. Paul names wisdom, knowledge, faith, healing, prophecy, discernment, and tongues with interpretation. Later he names roles such as teaching, helps, and governments. The purpose is benefit to the body, not a ranking of superior personalities.

## A gifts reflection

Read verses 14-26, then consider three questions privately or with a partner:

1. What gift or strength have you noticed in someone whose contribution is often quiet?
2. How does that contribution help another person?
3. What opportunity or support would help that gift be used well?

[Read 1 Corinthians 12:14-31](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/12?lang=eng&id=p14-p31#p14)

Patience, organization, curiosity, and hospitality can be useful strengths to notice. They are not automatically equivalent to each spiritual gift Paul lists. Neither introversion nor extroversion determines someone's place in Christ's body.

The body image rejects both "I do not belong" and "I have no need of you." Those regarded as weaker are necessary. A healthy community makes room for different capacities and shares both suffering and joy.

## The more excellent way

Chapter 13 follows directly from the discussion of gifts. Impressive speech, knowledge, faith, and generosity lose their purpose without charity.

[Read 1 Corinthians 13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/13?lang=eng)

Read verses 4-7 as qualities to practice, not a weapon for demanding that someone tolerate mistreatment. Love does not rejoice in iniquity; it rejoices in truth. Protection, accountability, and boundaries can be expressions of love.

Verses 9-12 also invite humility about partial knowledge. Love matters while understanding remains incomplete.

## Questions to sit with

- Who is easy to overlook when we celebrate visible gifts?
- How could your strength serve another person rather than your reputation?
- Which description of charity challenges one habitual response?
- How does knowing only in part affect the way we speak to someone who disagrees?
