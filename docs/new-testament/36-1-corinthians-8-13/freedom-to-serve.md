# 1 Corinthians 8-9 - Freedom to Serve

## Knowledge and care for a neighbor

Paul addresses food associated with idols and the conscience of other believers. He does not settle the question by asking only what a knowledgeable person has the right to do. He asks how that freedom affects another person.

[Read 1 Corinthians 8:1-13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/8?lang=eng&id=p1-p13#p1)

"Knowledge puffeth up, but charity edifieth" is not an attack on learning. It challenges knowledge used without love. Care for conscience should not become a device for controlling every harmless choice through threatened offense.

## What would an examination of my life show?

Chapter 9 defends Paul's apostleship and his right to material support, then explains why he has chosen not to exercise that right among these hearers. Verses 10-14 discuss the worker's share, including the support received by temple workers. Begin with that argument before drawing broader lessons about hope or sacrifice.

[Read 1 Corinthians 9:1-23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/9?lang=eng&id=p1-p23#p1)

Use these reflection prompts, not as quotations from Paul but as applications:

- **Hope:** What good am I working toward beyond immediate recognition?
- **Sacrifice:** Is there a right or preference I can freely set aside to help another?
- **Service:** What do my time and actions show that I value?
- **Adaptability:** Am I willing to learn enough about another person's life to communicate respectfully?

Genuine interest matters more than pretending to share every hobby or background. Becoming "all things to all men" is not permission to deceive or abandon integrity.

## Run with purpose

Paul compares disciplined ministry with training for a race. The image concerns wholehearted effort, not a claim that only one disciple can receive salvation.

[Read 1 Corinthians 9:24-27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/9?lang=eng&id=p24-p27#p24)

Rest, boundaries, and shared labor can support faithful service. The goal is not to earn grace through exhaustion or make ourselves indispensable.

## Questions to sit with

- When can being right fail to build another person up?
- What could we freely set aside for someone else's good?
- How can genuine curiosity help us serve without pretending to be someone we are not?
- What would focused, sustainable effort look like in one responsibility?
