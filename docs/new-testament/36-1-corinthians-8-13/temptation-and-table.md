# 1 Corinthians 10-11 - A Way to Escape and a Shared Table

## Do not assume immunity

Chapter 10 recalls Israel's history and warns those who think they stand securely to take heed. The warning is followed by assurance of God's faithfulness and a way of escape in temptation.

[Read 1 Corinthians 10:1-14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/10?lang=eng&id=p1-p14#p1)

Verse 13 should not be shortened to "God never gives you more than you can handle." It addresses temptation and God's help, not a promise that trauma, grief, or illness will always be manageable without support.

A way of escape may involve leaving a situation, seeking treatment or counsel, asking someone for help, or changing a repeated habit. Verse 14's instruction to flee idolatry makes action part of the response.

## Seek another's good

The later discussion of food and conscience returns to edification and the good of others. Read it with the earlier chapter rather than applying an isolated sentence to every modern choice.

[Read 1 Corinthians 10:23-33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/10?lang=eng&id=p23-p33#p23)

The aim is a life that honors God and does not needlessly obstruct another person's reception of the gospel. It is not a requirement to make everyone approve of us.

## A meal that exposes divisions

First Corinthians 11 addresses differences in worship and then condemns a gathering in which some eat abundantly while others go hungry. Paul's account of the Lord's Supper belongs within that concern for the body of believers.

[Read 1 Corinthians 11:17-34](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/11?lang=eng&id=p17-p34#p17)

Self-examination is not only private anxiety about being good enough. Ask whether our worship remembers Christ and recognizes the people beside us. In the passage, unworthy participation concerns the manner of eating; do not use it to imply that a person must be sinless before seeking the Savior through authorized worship.

The earlier head-covering discussion is culturally and interpretively complex. Read its appeal to mutual dependence in verses 11-12 as well, rather than turning the chapter into a simple hierarchy of human worth.

## Questions to sit with

- What practical escape from temptation might we overlook because it seems too ordinary?
- How is asking for help an expression of faith rather than its opposite?
- What divisions could our worship unintentionally preserve?
- How could remembering Christ change the way we notice another person's needs?
