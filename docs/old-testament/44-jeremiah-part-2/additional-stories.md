# Additional Stories: Competing Voices and a Later Choice

These two stories fall outside the October 26–November 1 assignment. They can enrich preparation, but they are not prerequisites for the 25-minute class.

## Hananiah and the yoke (Jeremiah 27–28)

Jeremiah wears a yoke as part of his warning about Babylon. Hananiah breaks that wooden yoke and promises a quick end to Babylon's control. Jeremiah challenges the claim and announces that the Lord has not sent Hananiah.

[Read Jeremiah 27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/27?lang=eng) and [Jeremiah 28](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/28?lang=eng).

Jeremiah's “Amen” in 28:6 does not establish a sarcastic tone; the verse can be read as a sincere wish that restoration would come. The text gives a test about whether the prophecy comes to pass. Use the story as context for Zedekiah's later choices, not as proof that we know everything he heard or thought.

## A request with a predetermined answer (Jeremiah 40–43)

After Jerusalem's fall, the people ask Jeremiah to seek the Lord's direction and pledge to obey. When he tells them to remain in the land, leaders reject the message and go to Egypt.

[Read Jeremiah 42](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/42?lang=eng) and [Jeremiah 43:1–7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/43?lang=eng&id=p1-p7#p1).

This later account can prompt a private question: *Am I willing to hear an answer different from the one I prefer?* Do not use it to assume that every uncertain decision has a clear, immediate answer to prayer.
