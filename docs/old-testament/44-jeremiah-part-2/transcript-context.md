# Transcript Context: Competing Voices and a Later Choice

The supplied transcript includes two substantial stories beyond the October 26–November 1 assignment. They can enrich preparation, but they are not prerequisites for the 25-minute class.

## Hananiah and the yoke (Jeremiah 27–28)

Jeremiah wears a yoke as part of his warning about Babylon. Hananiah breaks that wooden yoke and promises a quick end to Babylon's control. Jeremiah challenges the claim and announces that the Lord has not sent Hananiah.

[Read Jeremiah 27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/27?lang=eng) and [Jeremiah 28](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/28?lang=eng).

The transcript tells this story at about 5:35–10:39 to explore competing voices. Its dramatized guess that Jeremiah's “Amen” was sarcastic is not stated by the passage; verse 6 can be read as a sincere wish that restoration would come. The text gives a test about whether the prophecy comes to pass. Use the story as context for Zedekiah's later choices, not as proof that we know everything he heard or thought.

## A request with a predetermined answer (Jeremiah 40–43)

After Jerusalem's fall, the people ask Jeremiah to seek the Lord's direction and pledge to obey. When he tells them to remain in the land, leaders reject the message and go to Egypt. The transcript discusses this at about 27:28–33:27.

[Read Jeremiah 42](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/42?lang=eng) and [Jeremiah 43:1–7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/43?lang=eng&id=p1-p7#p1).

This later account can prompt a private question: *Am I willing to hear an answer different from the one I prefer?* Do not use it to assume that every uncertain decision has a clear, immediate answer to prayer.

## Keep the week's promises in view

The transcript also compares Jeremiah with Mormon and Jesus Christ. These are interpretive connections, not claims that the texts describe identical missions or outcomes. Jeremiah 31:31–34 and 33:14–16 provide the week's direct covenant and Messiah passages; Lamentations 1 and 3 provide language for grief and hope.
