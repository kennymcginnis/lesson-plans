# The Word That Was Written Again (Jeremiah 36)

## Why make a record?

<details><summary>Show a sample answer</summary><p>The Lord wants Judah to hear the warning, turn from evil, and be forgiven (Jeremiah 36:3). The written record lets Baruch read those words publicly.</p></details>

The Lord tells Jeremiah to write the words He has given him, hoping that Judah will hear of the coming disaster, turn from evil, and receive forgiveness. Baruch writes and publicly reads the scroll because Jeremiah cannot enter the Lord's house at that time.

[Read Jeremiah 36:1–10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/36?lang=eng&id=p1-p10#p1)

Ask students to find the purpose stated in verse 3 before discussing the king's reaction. The warning is severe, but the goal includes an opportunity to turn. God's word is being offered to people who still have choices.

## The king and the scroll

Officials bring the scroll to King Jehoiakim. As it is read, he cuts off sections and throws them into the fire. Some officials plead with him not to burn it, but he will not listen.

[Read Jeremiah 36:20–26](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/36?lang=eng&id=p20-p26#p20)

Ask: **What can the king destroy, and what can he not destroy?**

<details><summary>Show a sample answer</summary><p>Jehoiakim can burn that copy of the scroll. He cannot erase the Lord's word or undo his responsibility to hear it; Jeremiah and Baruch write the words again (Jeremiah 36:27–32).</p></details>

Avoid turning the story into a claim that every lost effort will be replaced in exactly the same way.

## Write it again

After the scroll is burned, the Lord directs Jeremiah to take another. Baruch writes again, and additional words are included. The account reports action rather than a detailed record of Jeremiah's feelings.

[Read Jeremiah 36:27–32](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/36?lang=eng&id=p27-p32#p27)

Ask how someone might continue when a faithful effort is rejected. Jeremiah 36 does not record Jeremiah's feelings or a complaint. Let his narrated response stand on its own.

## Questions to sit with

- What does verse 3 reveal about the Lord's purpose in giving a warning?

  <details><summary>Show a sample answer</summary><p>The Lord wants the people to hear, turn from evil, and receive forgiveness. The warning offers a chance to respond (Jeremiah 36:3).</p></details>
- How do the king, the officials, Baruch, and Jeremiah respond differently to the words?

  <details><summary>Show a sample answer</summary><p>Jehoiakim burns the scroll; some officials plead with him to stop. Baruch writes and reads the words, and Jeremiah receives the command to write them again (Jeremiah 36:4–10, 21–26, 27–32).</p></details>
- What work might need to be begun again after a disappointment?

  <details><summary>Show a sample answer</summary><p>A kind conversation, an attempt to reconcile, or a habit of scripture study may need a fresh start. Jeremiah 36 shows one particular faithful restart, not a promise that every effort will be restored in the same way.</p></details>
- When is persistence faithful, and when should we seek help or change the way we proceed?

  <details><summary>Show a sample answer</summary><p>Persistence is faithful when it serves a good purpose and respects others. We can also ask for help or change our approach when a plan is unsafe, ineffective, or beyond what we can do alone.</p></details>
