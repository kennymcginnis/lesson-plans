# The Word That Was Written Again (Jeremiah 36)

## Why make a record?

The Lord tells Jeremiah to write the words He has given him, hoping that Judah will hear of the coming disaster, turn from evil, and receive forgiveness. Baruch writes and publicly reads the scroll because Jeremiah cannot enter the Lord's house at that time.

[Read Jeremiah 36:1–10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/36?lang=eng&id=p1-p10#p1)

Ask students to find the purpose stated in verse 3 before discussing the king's reaction. The warning is severe, but the goal includes an opportunity to turn. God's word is being offered to people who still have choices.

## The king and the scroll

Officials bring the scroll to King Jehoiakim. As it is read, he cuts off sections and throws them into the fire. Some officials plead with him not to burn it, but he will not listen.

[Read Jeremiah 36:20–26](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/36?lang=eng&id=p20-p26#p20)

The scene in the supplied transcript begins around 1:28. It makes a useful opening question: **What can the king destroy, and what can he not destroy?** He can burn that copy; he cannot make the Lord's warning unspoken or undo his responsibility to hear it. Avoid turning the story into a claim that every lost effort will be replaced in exactly the same way.

## Write it again

After the scroll is burned, the Lord directs Jeremiah to take another. Baruch writes again, and additional words are included. The account reports action rather than a detailed record of Jeremiah's feelings.

[Read Jeremiah 36:27–32](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/36?lang=eng&id=p27-p32#p27)

The transcript imagines how frustrating this might have been, then asks how to continue when a faithful effort is rejected. That is a useful application, but Jeremiah 36 does not say he felt frustrated or complained. Let the narrated response stand on its own.

## Questions to sit with

- What does verse 3 reveal about the Lord's purpose in giving a warning?
- How do the king, the officials, Baruch, and Jeremiah respond differently to the words?
- What work might need to be begun again after a disappointment?
- When is persistence faithful, and when should we seek help or change the way we proceed?
