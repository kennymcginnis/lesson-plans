# Courage with Rags and Ropes (Jeremiah 38–39)

## A person who acts

After officials put Jeremiah into a muddy cistern, Ebed-melech hears what happened. He approaches the king, calls the act wrong, and receives permission to pull Jeremiah out before he dies.

[Read Jeremiah 38:1–13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/38?lang=eng&id=p1-p13#p1)

The transcript highlights this scene at about 16:49–18:39. Ask students to notice its specific verbs: Ebed-melech **hears**, **speaks**, **gathers helpers**, **lowers rags**, and **pulls**. His action is courageous and practical. The rags protect Jeremiah where the ropes touch him; care appears in small details as well as public words.

## Trust and deliverance

Jeremiah 39:15–18 records the Lord's promise to Ebed-melech that he will be delivered because he trusted Him. The promise is addressed to this person in this situation. It can encourage trust without becoming a blanket guarantee that every act of courage receives the same immediate outcome.

[Read Jeremiah 39:15–18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/39?lang=eng&id=p15-p18#p15)

The contrast with Zedekiah is worth exploring carefully. The king has a throne but hesitates; Ebed-melech has a lower position yet uses the access and help available to him. Avoid reducing either person to a simple personality label. Students can focus on what each one does in the text.

## Questions to sit with

- What risks might Ebed-melech have understood, and what does the text actually tell us?
- Why do the rags matter in the rescue?
- Who can we help with the resources and relationships already within our reach?
- How could courage include asking others to help?
