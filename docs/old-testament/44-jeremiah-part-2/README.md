# I Will Turn Their Mourning into Joy

Week 44 · October 26–November 1, 2026 · Jeremiah 31–33; 36–39; Lamentations 1; 3

> When a true message is painful or unwelcome, what helps us hear it, act with courage, and hold to the Lord's promise of renewal?

## Lesson notes

[Open the presentation](old-testament/44-jeremiah-part-2/presentation.md). The supplied video transcript asks, “What do we do when God's word is hard to accept?” Its strongest scenes within this week's assignment are the burned scroll (Jeremiah 36), Zedekiah's hesitant meetings with Jeremiah and Ebed-melech's rescue (37–39), and Jeremiah's promise of a new covenant (31). The transcript's accounts of Hananiah (27–28) and Johanan's journey to Egypt (40–43) are helpful background, but they are outside this week's assigned reading. The class ends in the hope of Jeremiah 31–33 and Lamentations 3, not at the fall of the city.

## A 25-minute class

1. **Opening and the scroll, 10 minutes:** Begin with an unwelcome-answer scenario. Read Jeremiah 36:1–3, 21–26, 32. Contrast the king's response with Jeremiah and Baruch's decision to write again. Ask what the burning scroll can and cannot destroy.
2. **Hearing versus acting, 2 minutes:** Read Jeremiah 38:14–20. Zedekiah seeks a word from the Lord but is afraid to follow it. Name the pressure in the passage before applying it to ourselves.
3. **Courage with ordinary means, 3 minutes:** Read Jeremiah 38:7–13. Notice Ebed-melech's words, the rags, and the ropes. Ask what faithful action is within his reach.
4. **The Lord's future, 5 minutes:** Read Jeremiah 31:31–34 and 33:14–16. Ask what the Lord promises to write, forgive, and bring forth. The promised Branch points Christian readers to Jesus Christ.
5. **Hope that makes room for grief, 5 minutes:** Read Lamentations 1:1–2 and 3:21–26. Allow a private response: What truth would help you take one faithful step this week?

The class does not need to resolve every difficult historical question. Let students read the verses, describe what they actually say, and distinguish a promise of ultimate restoration from a guarantee of an easy immediate outcome.

## Reading the account carefully

Jeremiah 36 names Jehoiakim as the king who burns the scroll. Jeremiah 37–39 concerns Zedekiah, the later king who asks Jeremiah for counsel. Do not merge the two kings. The transcript tells the Hananiah story from Jeremiah 27–28 and Johanan's Egypt story from Jeremiah 40–43; these are context, not assigned passages. The text of Jeremiah 38 shows Zedekiah's fear and hesitation, but it does not reveal every motive or prove he heard every competing claim described elsewhere.

Lamentations gives voice to devastation. Its expressions of hope should be read beside grief, not used to hurry someone past it. Jeremiah's future-facing covenant promises are given to a people facing loss. Their fulfillment in Jesus Christ offers hope without making faithful people immune to setbacks.
