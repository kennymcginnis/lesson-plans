# Lament and New Mercies (Lamentations 1; 3)

## Hear the grief first

Lamentations 1 describes Jerusalem as desolate and weeping. It gives voice to loss after the city's fall. The book does not ask the reader to call that loss good or easy.

[Read Lamentations 1:1–2, 16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lam/1?lang=eng&id=p1-p2,p16#p1)

Before turning to hope, let students name what the speaker grieves. Avoid asking anyone to recount a private trauma. A class can learn from a lament without making a hurting person perform one for the room.

## Hope within the lament

Lamentations 3 moves through affliction and then recalls the Lord's mercy, compassion, and faithfulness. The hope appears within the same poem as the pain. It is not a claim that every circumstance has already improved.

[Read Lamentations 3:21–26, 31–33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lam/3?lang=eng&id=p21-p26,p31-p33#p21)

The supplied transcript emphasizes that faithful people can work hard and still face setbacks (about 33:51–36:19). Lamentations gives scriptural language for that truth. We can bring grief to God and still look for the mercy He offers. For Christian disciples, the promised Branch and new covenant in Jeremiah 31–33 center that hope in Jesus Christ.

## Questions to sit with

- What do the first chapter's images ask us to acknowledge?
- What does the speaker deliberately call to mind in chapter 3?
- How can someone express hope without minimizing present suffering?
- What small act of care might help a person who is mourning?
