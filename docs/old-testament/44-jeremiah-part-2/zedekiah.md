# Hearing and Acting (Jeremiah 37–38)

## A king asks for a word

Zedekiah sends to Jeremiah and later questions him privately. He wants to know whether there is a word from the Lord, but the word is not the outcome he hopes for. Jeremiah warns that Jerusalem will fall and calls the king to surrender so lives can be spared.

[Read Jeremiah 37:17–21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/37?lang=eng&id=p17-p21#p17)

[Read Jeremiah 38:14–23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/38?lang=eng&id=p14-p23#p14)

Students can compare Zedekiah's requests for counsel with his words and actions after hearing it. Ask what fear he actually names in Jeremiah 38:19. The story does not require us to invent every motive he had.

## When fear is part of the decision

Jeremiah 38:20 answers the king's concern directly and repeats the invitation to obey the Lord's voice. The account does not portray courageous action as the absence of fear; it puts a difficult choice in front of a frightened ruler.

This is a better starting point for application than asking students to disclose a personal failure. A private writing prompt could be: *What true counsel do I understand, and what support would help me act on it?* Different situations need different kinds of support, including careful planning, counsel, and help from trusted people. Do not use Zedekiah's wartime instruction to pressure someone to remain in danger.

<details><summary>Show a sample answer</summary><p>For example, I may understand that I should apologize to someone. I could pray, plan what to say, and ask a trusted person to help me act kindly and honestly.</p></details>

## What follows

Jeremiah 39 recounts the fall of Jerusalem and Zedekiah's capture. Reading this history can help students understand the stakes, but avoid suggesting that every hardship today is proof someone ignored God. The chapter describes this specific siege and its consequences.

[Read Jeremiah 39:1–10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/39?lang=eng&id=p1-p10#p1)

## Questions to sit with

- What does Zedekiah seek from Jeremiah, and what does he fear?

  <details><summary>Show a sample answer</summary><p>He asks for a word from the Lord, then says he fears the Judeans who have gone over to the Chaldeans (Jeremiah 37:17; 38:19).</p></details>
- Why can hearing a message and following it be two different decisions?

  <details><summary>Show a sample answer</summary><p>Hearing gives us information; following may require a costly choice. Zedekiah names a fear that makes action difficult (Jeremiah 38:19–20).</p></details>
- What would have helped the king respond differently?

  <details><summary>Show a sample answer</summary><p>The passage suggests that trusting the Lord's instruction despite his stated fear would have mattered. We cannot know all his motives or predict a different outcome beyond the promise Jeremiah gives in 38:20.</p></details>
- How can we seek the Lord with a heart genuinely open to correction?

  <details><summary>Show a sample answer</summary><p>We can read the passage closely, pray without demanding a preferred answer, and seek wise counsel before choosing the next faithful step.</p></details>
