# Psalm 102–103 — When Life Feels Heavy

## Scripture

## That's not exactly praise. And that's the point.

Read enough of Psalm 102 out loud that the weight of it lands. This psalm gives ancient words to very familiar feelings
**— anxiety, depression, exhaustion.**

It ends still begging God to hear. Don't rush past this. Let it feel heavy for a moment.

**Psalm 102 (selected)**

> 1. **Hear my prayer, O Lord, and let my cry come unto thee.**
> 2. Hide not thy face from me in the day when I am in trouble; incline thine ear unto me: in the day when I call answer
>    me speedily.
> 3. For **my days are consumed like smoke**, and my bones are burned as an hearth.
> 4. My heart is smitten, and **withered like grass**; so that I forget to eat my bread.
> 5. By reason of the voice of my groaning my bones cleave to my skin.
> 6. **I am like a pelican of the wilderness: I am like an owl of the desert.**

## One psalm away

Right after the desperate prayer of Psalm 102 comes Psalm 103:

**Psalm 103:1–5**

> 1. **Bless the Lord, O my soul:** and all that is within me, bless his holy name.
> 2. Bless the Lord, O my soul, **and forget not all his benefits:**
> 3. **Who forgiveth all thine iniquities; who healeth all thy diseases;**
> 4. **Who redeemeth thy life from destruction; who crowneth thee with lovingkindness and tender mercies;**
> 5. **Who satisfieth thy mouth with good things;** so that thy youth is renewed like the eagle's.

> Praise the Lord, my soul; all my inmost being, praise his holy name. (Psalms 103:1 NIV)

On days like 102, it's easy to forget what God is capable of. Psalm 103 is the reminder — and it's only one psalm away.

If you're living in a 102 kind of week, you are not far from being reminded who God is.

Mark the word **"all"** in verse 3 — that's not an exaggeration, it's the text.

**You were eating ashes one chapter ago. Now your mouth is satisfied with good things.**
