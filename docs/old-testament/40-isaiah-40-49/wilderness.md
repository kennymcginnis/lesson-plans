# Isaiah 40; 41; 43; 48 - A Way in the Wilderness

## Prepare the way of the Lord

> 3. "Prepare ye the way of the Lord, make straight in the desert a highway for our God."

Isaiah 40:3, KJV excerpt.

[Read Isaiah 40:3-5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/40?lang=eng&id=p3-p5#p3)

Valleys rise, mountains are brought low, and rough places become plain. The image concerns preparing a way for the Lord and the revealing of His glory. It is larger than a promise that our plans will meet no obstacles.

**Explicit New Testament connection:** Luke 3:3-6 applies this passage to John the Baptist's ministry of preparation. Read Luke's use alongside Isaiah's setting of comfort and restoration.

[Read Luke 3:3-6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/3?lang=eng&id=p3-p6#p3)

As a personal application, consider the people and practices that help prepare us to receive Christ: prophetic teaching, repentance, prayer, and someone who helps us take a difficult next step. These are applications of the image, not a list supplied by Isaiah 40.

## Water where it is needed

Isaiah 41:17 begins with poor and needy people seeking water. The Lord says He hears them and will not forsake them. Verse 18 promises rivers, fountains, and water in the wilderness.

[Read Isaiah 41:17-18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/41?lang=eng&id=p17-p18#p17)

Isaiah 43 gives a related promise:

> 19. "I will even make a way in the wilderness, and rivers in the desert."

Isaiah 43:19, KJV excerpt.

[Read Isaiah 43:16-21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/43?lang=eng&id=p16-p21#p16)

The language recalls deliverance and invites hope for a new work of God. The people need a way forward and sustenance along it. God's care is not only a reward waiting at the destination.

## Leaving Babylon

Isaiah 48:20 calls the people to leave Babylon and announce the Lord's redemption of Jacob. Verse 21 recalls water flowing from rock in the desert. Read the departure with the promise of God's provision.

[Read Isaiah 48:17-22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/48?lang=eng&id=p17-p22#p17)

We may recognize a similar challenge when a sound spiritual invitation takes us beyond familiar routines. But the passage does not teach that every secure circumstance must be abandoned or that every risky choice is an act of faith. Babylon's apparent stability was not the same as freedom for exiled people.

Seek the Lord's guidance, consider responsibilities, and accept appropriate help. Dependence on Him does not require rejecting planning or the support of others.

## God is present along the way

The lesson's central invitation is to walk with the Lord, not merely aim toward a future point when we will finally need Him. The nourishment, direction, and companionship matter throughout the journey.

## Questions to sit with

- How does the phrase "a highway for our God" change a purely self-centered reading of the promise?
- What forms of help have sustained you during an uncertain transition?
- How can we distinguish a faithful next step from unnecessary risk?
- Who might need practical support on a journey they did not choose?
