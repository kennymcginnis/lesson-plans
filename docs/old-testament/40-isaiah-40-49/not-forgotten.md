# Isaiah 49:13-16 - I Will Not Forget Thee

## Zion voices the fear

Verse 13 calls heaven, earth, and mountains to rejoice because the Lord comforts His people. Immediately afterward, Zion says:

> 14. "The Lord hath forsaken me, and my Lord hath forgotten me."

Isaiah 49:14, KJV excerpt.

[Read Isaiah 49:13-16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/49?lang=eng&id=p13-p16#p13)

Read those verses together. A promise of comfort has been spoken, yet the feeling of abandonment remains. The text gives that fear a voice rather than pretending a hopeful announcement makes every wound vanish.

The complaint echoes Israel's fear in Isaiah 40:27 that its way is hidden from God. Both passages answer the concern through the Lord's character and commitment.

## A bond that will not fail

The Lord asks whether a mother can forget her nursing child. The image draws on a relationship of tenderness and dependence, then acknowledges that even human care can fail:

> 15. "Yea, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee."

Isaiah 49:15, KJV excerpt.

The promise is not a judgment on a struggling parent or a denial of painful family experiences. Its force is that God's commitment is not limited by the failures that can occur in human relationships.

For someone whose experience of family care is painful, let the final assurance speak without requiring them to idealize their own past. The Lord's remembrance does not depend on having received perfect care from others.

## Engraved upon His hands

> 16. "Behold, I have graven thee upon the palms of my hands; thy walls are continually before me."

Isaiah 49:16, KJV.

The verse addresses Zion, including her walls. Engraving conveys enduring remembrance, not a passing thought. The collective promise matters: God has not lost sight of a devastated community.

**Christian devotional connection:** We may reflect on this image alongside the wounds of the risen Jesus. John 20:27 records Him inviting Thomas to see His hands, and 3 Nephi 11:14-15 records His invitation to the people to feel the prints of the nails.

[Read John 20:26-29](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/20?lang=eng&id=p26-p29#p26)

[Read 3 Nephi 11:14-15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/3-ne/11?lang=eng&id=p14-p15#p14)

These passages deepen our reflection on Christ's love and sacrifice. Isaiah 49:16 does not itself explicitly describe crucifixion wounds or say that individual names are literally written in them. Keep the devotional association distinct from the words of the prophecy.

## Remembering and being remembered

What has Jesus Christ given for you? Consider His mortal ministry, compassion, suffering, death, and Resurrection. Our assurance of His love can rest in His saving work even when we cannot explain why a present difficulty continues.

We can also reflect that remembrance through care for others: following up, remembering a need, listening, or making room for someone whose absence has gone unnoticed. These acts do not replace God's care; they can express our willingness to participate in it.

## Questions to sit with

- Why is it important that Zion's complaint remains in the text beside the promise?
- What does engraving add to the assurance that God remembers?
- How does Christ's saving work help you understand His love without answering every question about suffering?
- Who could feel less forgotten through one action you take this week?
