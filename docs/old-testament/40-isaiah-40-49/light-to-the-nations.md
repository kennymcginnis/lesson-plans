# Isaiah 49:1-6 - A Light beyond Israel

## Has the work been in vain?

Isaiah 49 opens with a servant called from the womb and prepared for the Lord's work. Verse 3 names the servant Israel, yet verse 5 describes the servant bringing Jacob back to God. Attend to that complexity rather than reducing the whole passage to one simple label.

[Read Isaiah 49:1-6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/49?lang=eng&id=p1-p6#p1)

Verse 4 gives voice to disappointment:

> 4. "I have laboured in vain, I have spent my strength for nought, and in vain: yet surely my judgment is with the Lord, and my work with my God."

Isaiah 49:4, KJV excerpt.

The sense that effort has accomplished nothing is not the end of the passage. The servant entrusts judgment to the Lord, and the calling that follows reaches beyond what is immediately visible.

## More than restoring one people

> 6. "I will also give thee for a light to the Gentiles, that thou mayest be my salvation unto the end of the earth."

Isaiah 49:6, KJV excerpt.

The mission includes restoring Israel and bringing light to the nations. The point is not that Israel's restoration is unimportant or that it is an effortless task. The scope of the Lord's saving purpose is wider still.

In Christian reading, the servant's work directs attention toward Jesus Christ and the salvation He brings. Keep that reading connected to the scriptural witness rather than treating every personal project as equivalent to the servant's mission.

## Paul and Barnabas use the passage

**Explicit New Testament quotation:** Acts 13:47 quotes Isaiah's language about light to the Gentiles as Paul and Barnabas explain their ministry among the nations.

[Read Acts 13:46-48](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/acts/13?lang=eng&id=p46-p48#p46)

This supplies a scriptural bridge from Isaiah's words to sharing the gospel. It is more than a general resemblance between two discussions of light. It also keeps the purpose centered on God's salvation rather than the status of the messengers.

## Receive light and share it

The people comforted by God are not invited only to preserve their own security. As a devotional application, consider how receiving the Savior's care might prepare us to bless someone beyond our usual circle.

An invitation, a conversation, a practical act of kindness, or support for someone who feels overlooked can be a small response. Respect the person's agency and keep friendship genuine regardless of whether an invitation is accepted.

## Questions to sit with

- What tension do you hear between the weariness of verse 4 and the mission of verse 6?
- How does the reach of God's purpose challenge a concern only for our own group?
- What does the explicit use of Isaiah in Acts help us understand?
- What light or kindness have you received that you could share this week?
