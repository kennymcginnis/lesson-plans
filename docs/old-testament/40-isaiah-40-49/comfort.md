# Isaiah 40:1-11 - Comfort Ye My People

## A word to people who need hope

> "Comfort ye, comfort ye my people, saith your God."

Isaiah 40:1, KJV.

[Read Isaiah 40:1-11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/40?lang=eng&id=p1-p11#p1)

These chapters speak into the horizon of exile, devastation, and return. Jerusalem needs more than advice to feel optimistic. The announcement in verse 2 concerns the end of her period of hardship and the pardon of her iniquity.

The shift from the earlier chapters is a shift of emphasis and prophetic horizon, not a simple diary entry announcing that exactly two centuries have passed. Isaiah 39 has already anticipated Babylonian captivity; Isaiah 40 now gives voice to comfort and restoration.

Verse 2's language about receiving double is part of this poetic announcement. It should not become a formula for calculating how much suffering an individual deserves, nor an explanation that every disaster is punishment for personal sin.

## Dallin H. Oaks - Scripture Reading and Revelation

**Verified teaching about interpreting Isaiah.** Elder Oaks, then a member of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, writes:

> "The book of Isaiah contains numerous prophecies that seem to have multiple fulfillments."

He discusses meanings related to Isaiah's own setting, later events surrounding the Savior's mortal ministry, and the Second Coming. He emphasizes seeking the Holy Ghost's help in interpretation.

["Scripture Reading and Revelation," Ensign, January 1995](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/ensign/1995/01/scripture-reading-and-revelation?lang=eng)

This teaching invites attentive reading, not unlimited meanings. Begin with the passage itself, then identify whether a connection is made by another scripture, taught by a reliable source, or proposed as a personal application. Not every prophecy must have exactly three fulfillments.

## A word that lasts

> "The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever."

Isaiah 40:8, KJV.

The contrast gives comfort a foundation beyond changing circumstances. Human strength and beauty are temporary; God's word endures. Trust is directed toward the One who speaks, not toward our ability to keep every condition stable.

The opening repeatedly addresses a people and a city. We can receive personal encouragement from it while respecting that collective setting. A promise to restore Zion is not automatically a prediction of a particular outcome in every individual's life.

## Greatness joined with tenderness

Verse 11 pictures the Lord as a shepherd who gathers lambs, carries them, and gently leads those with young. His power is not detached from their needs.

Read that image alongside the opening command to comfort. How might we reflect such care in our words and actions? Comfort can include listening, practical help, an assurance of belonging, and room to grieve. It need not begin with an explanation of why the trouble happened.

## Questions to sit with

- What do the words "my people" add to the invitation to comfort?
- Why might enduring promises matter especially when familiar sources of security are gone?
- How does the shepherd image shape your understanding of divine power?
- What kind of comfort could you offer without minimizing another person's loss?
