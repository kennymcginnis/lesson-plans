# Isaiah 40:27-31; 43:1-3 - They That Wait upon the Lord

## My way is hidden

Isaiah 40:27 voices Israel's fear that the Lord has overlooked their way and their cause. The answer begins with who God is: the everlasting Creator, whose understanding is beyond searching and whose strength is not exhausted.

[Read Isaiah 40:27-31](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/40?lang=eng&id=p27-p31#p27)

> 29. "He giveth power to the faint; and to them that have no might he increaseth strength."

Isaiah 40:29, KJV.

The promise is addressed to people who need strength, not only those who already appear resilient. Even the young and vigorous become weary. Need is not a disqualification from the Lord's care.

## Waiting with hope

> 31. "But they that wait upon the Lord shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint."

Isaiah 40:31, KJV.

Waiting here invites patient, expectant trust. It is not merely watching a clock and resenting a delay. Prayer, rest, accepting help, and doing the good already within our reach can all belong to that trust.

One teaching illustration is to think of a person ready to serve, asking, "What would Thou have me do next?" That is an English analogy, not the lexical meaning of waiting in this verse. Nor does God require constant activity or productivity before He will help us.

Notice walking as well as soaring. Sometimes strength appears as an ordinary next step, not an extraordinary achievement. The promise should not be used to shame someone whose illness, fatigue, or disability continues.

## When thou passest through the waters

Isaiah 43:1 speaks to Jacob and Israel as created, redeemed, and called by name. The next verse promises presence in danger:

> 2. "When thou passest through the waters, I will be with thee."

Isaiah 43:2, KJV excerpt.

[Read Isaiah 43:1-3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/43?lang=eng&id=p1-p3#p1)

The passage says "when," not "if." Water and fire are part of its powerful imagery of deliverance. In applying it personally, do not promise that every faithful person is immune to drowning, burns, persecution, or grief. It is not an invitation to test God's protection by entering danger.

The assurance is grounded in belonging to the Redeemer. We can seek safety and assistance while trusting His presence. Our trust does not require us to deny how much an experience hurts.

## Questions to sit with

- What do you hear in the complaint that one's way is hidden from God?
- Which part of God's answer addresses that fear most directly?
- What could patient trust include when the wisest next step is rest or receiving help?
- What form of strength would help you take one faithful step this week?
