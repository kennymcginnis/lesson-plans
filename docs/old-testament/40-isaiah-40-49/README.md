# I Will Not Forget Thee

Week 40 - Isaiah 40-49

> What helps us trust that the Lord remembers us when the way ahead feels uncertain?

## Lesson notes

Isaiah 40 opens with comfort for God's people. The chapters that follow describe a way through the wilderness, water in dry places, strength for the weary, and the Lord's power to redeem. They also give voice to the fear that God has forgotten His people.

The final image answers that fear with a promise: Zion is engraved upon the palms of His hands. The God whose greatness exceeds our understanding also knows and remembers His people.

These studies develop teaching notes credited to Alex McGinnis. The central movement is from receiving comfort to trusting the Lord on the journey, rejecting inadequate substitutes, and joining His work of bringing light to others.

## Read the whole assignment

The topic pages follow selected themes rather than every verse. Keep the surrounding chapters in view:

- **Isaiah 42:** The servant brings judgment and light; the chapter also speaks of blindness and deafness among those who should attend to the Lord. Read verses 1-9 and 18-25 without reducing every use of "servant" to an identical description.
- **Isaiah 44-45:** The contrast between idols and the Redeemer continues, and Cyrus is named in connection with restoration. These chapters give the hope of return a historical dimension as well as a theological one.
- **Isaiah 46-47:** Gods that must be carried are contrasted with the Lord who carries His people. Babylon's pride and apparent security are confronted. Read the warnings as a challenge to oppressive power, not permission for contempt toward present-day peoples.

## A 25-minute class

1. **Begin with comfort, 3 minutes:** Read Isaiah 40:1-2, 8. Introduce the setting of displacement and hoped-for return, and ask why a people who have lost so much need assurance of God's word.
2. **Strength for the journey, 8 minutes:** Read Isaiah 40:27-31 and 43:1-2. Discuss the difference between God's presence in trouble and a promise that trouble will never come.
3. **Remembered and sent, 9 minutes:** Read Isaiah 49:14-16, then verse 6. Ask how being remembered by the Lord can change both our hope and our response to others.
4. **Remember the Savior, 5 minutes:** Reflect on what Jesus Christ has given for us. Invite one private response of trust or service and close with your testimony.

The fuller wilderness and idolatry studies are available for preparation or follow-up. Protect the closing five minutes rather than trying to discuss every passage.

## Scriptures to emphasize

Prioritize Isaiah 40:1-2, 8, 27-31; 43:1-2; and 49:6, 14-16. Companion passages include Isaiah 40:3-5, 12-18; 41:17-18, 21-24; 43:19; and 48:17-22.

## How the modern teachings connect

Read a prophecy first in its literary and historical setting, then distinguish a later scriptural fulfillment from a personal application. Collective promises to Israel or Zion can deepen individual trust without making every detail a private guarantee about our circumstances.

Scripture excerpts use the King James Version unless otherwise identified. The interpretation of the engraved palms in relation to Christ's wounds is a Christian devotional connection; Isaiah 49:16 itself addresses Zion and does not explicitly describe the Crucifixion.
