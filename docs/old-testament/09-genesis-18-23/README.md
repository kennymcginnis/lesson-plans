# Is Anything Too Hard for the Lord?

Week 9 - Genesis 18-23

> What helps us trust God's promises when we cannot yet see how they will be fulfilled?

## Lesson notes

Sarah's laughter, Lot's tragedy, and the Lord's provision for Abraham and Isaac invite us to consider faith from different angles. We see a promise that seems impossible, choices that shape a household, mercy that urges people forward, and a ram provided where Isaac had been bound.

The lesson points beyond admiration for Abraham's resolve to gratitude for God's provision. Read the accounts honestly, including their difficult questions, and let the scriptural witness of Jesus Christ guide the application.

## Read the whole assignment

The teaching pages follow selected themes, not a verse-by-verse commentary on every chapter. Keep these accounts in view as you read Genesis 18-23:

- **Genesis 20:** Abraham again identifies Sarah as his sister. Abimelech's encounter with God complicates any assumption that covenant membership makes every choice right or that outsiders lack integrity. Read God's protection of Sarah alongside the harm caused by fear and incomplete truth.
- **Genesis 21:8-21:** Hagar and Ishmael face displacement and desperate need. God hears the boy, speaks to Hagar, and opens her eyes to water. The story of the covenant through Isaac does not mean that Hagar and Ishmael are beyond God's care.
- **Genesis 21:22-34:** Abraham and Abimelech address a disputed well and make a covenant. Faith is lived through ordinary questions of trust, resources, and fair dealing as well as extraordinary experiences.
- **Genesis 23:** Sarah dies, Abraham mourns, and he purchases a burial place. Grief remains part of the life of faith. Receiving a promise does not remove the need to mourn or attend to practical responsibilities.

## A 25-minute class

1. **Begin with the question, 3 minutes:** Read Genesis 18:14. Ask what makes it difficult to trust a promise when circumstances seem to rule it out.
2. **Sarah's laughter and God's faithfulness, 7 minutes:** Read Genesis 18:10-15 and 21:1-6. Discuss the movement from disbelief to joy, then note Hebrews 11:13: not every promise is fully received in this life.
3. **The Lord will provide, 10 minutes:** Read Genesis 22:7-14 and Jacob 4:5. Focus on God's provision and the scriptural connection to His Only Begotten Son. Be explicit that this account does not authorize harming anyone in response to a claimed revelation.
4. **Faith for the future, 5 minutes:** Briefly introduce Luke 17:32 and the invitation to move forward with Christ. Read Romans 8:32, allow a private response, and close with your testimony.

The fuller discussions of Lot, intercession, and scriptural symbolism are available for personal study or a different teaching route. Preserve the closing time rather than trying to cover every story.

## Scriptures to emphasize

Prioritize Genesis 18:14; 21:1-6; 22:7-14; Jacob 4:5; and Romans 8:32. Companion passages include Genesis 18:23-33; 19:15-17; Luke 17:32; and Hebrews 11:8-19.

## How the modern teachings connect

Distinguish direct scripture citations from teachings that address a related theme. Jacob 4:5 explicitly connects Abraham and Isaac with God and His Only Begotten Son; other proposed parallels require more caution. Scripture excerpts in these notes use the King James Version unless otherwise identified.

The lesson's historical narratives are not instructions to reproduce harmful actions. Trust in God includes honesty about suffering, care for vulnerable people, and humility about what the text does not tell us.
