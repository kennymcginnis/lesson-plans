# Genesis 18; 21 - From Disbelief to Joy

## A promise Sarah can hear

Abraham welcomes visitors, offers rest and food, and hurries to prepare a meal. During the encounter, the promise of a son is repeated where Sarah can hear it. She laughs within herself at the thought of bearing a child in old age.

[Read Genesis 18:1-15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/18?lang=eng&id=p1-p15#p1)

> "Is any thing too hard for the Lord?"

Genesis 18:14, KJV excerpt.

The question does not deny the reality of Sarah's age or the years of waiting. It asks whether those circumstances exhaust what God can do. Verse 15 also records her fear and denial. Let her be a person in a difficult encounter, not merely a lesson about how other people should never struggle.

Genesis records her laughter and the Lord's answer, but not a prolonged argument. Avoid supplying imagined dialogue as if it were scripture.

## Who makes the promise?

Many English Bibles use small capitals, LORD, to represent the divine name commonly rendered Jehovah or Yahweh. This differs from ordinary uses of the title Lord. The typography helps us notice who is speaking; it does not by itself settle every question about the visitors in Genesis 18.

**Latter-day teaching:** In *The Living Christ*, the First Presidency and Quorum of the Twelve identify Jesus Christ as the Great Jehovah of the Old Testament. This directs a Latter-day Saint reading of the promise toward the Savior's divine identity.

[Read The Living Christ](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/the-living-christ/the-living-christ?lang=eng)

## Laughter changes its meaning

Genesis 21 begins by emphasizing that the Lord does what He has spoken. Isaac is born, and Sarah speaks of laughter shared with others:

> "God hath made me to laugh, so that all that hear will laugh with me."

Genesis 21:6, KJV excerpt.

[Read Genesis 21:1-7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/21?lang=eng&id=p1-p7#p1)

Hebrews 11:11 remembers Sarah as one who judged the promiser faithful. Her moment of fear is not the whole story of her faith. We should be equally careful about defining a person by one anxious response.

[Read Hebrews 11:11-13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/11?lang=eng&id=p11-p13#p11)

## Russell M. Nelson - The Power of Spiritual Momentum

**Verified quotation; thematic connection.** President Nelson, then President of the Church, invites us to:

> "Seek and expect miracles."

He encourages prayer and the spiritual work of exercising faith, pointing to Jesus Christ's power to strengthen the weary. This is a teaching about faith and divine help, not presented here as a direct citation of Sarah's experience.

["The Power of Spiritual Momentum," April 2022](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2022/04/47nelson?lang=eng)

Do not turn this invitation into a promise that every person will receive a child, a cure, or a desired outcome on a chosen timetable. Hebrews 11:13 speaks of faithful people who died without receiving the fullness of the promises. Hope in God can include waiting and grief.

## A companion promise to Mary

Read Luke 1:31-38. Mary asks how the promised birth can happen, and Gabriel witnesses of God's power. Sarah's and Mary's circumstances are different; the thematic connection is a promise that exceeds what the recipient can presently explain.

[Read Luke 1:31-38](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/1?lang=eng&id=p31-p38#p31)

## Questions to sit with

- What might long waiting do to our ability to hear a hopeful promise?
- How does Hebrews' remembrance of Sarah broaden the picture of her faith?
- What is the difference between trusting God and insisting on a particular outcome?
- How can we make room for another person's hope without dismissing their grief?
