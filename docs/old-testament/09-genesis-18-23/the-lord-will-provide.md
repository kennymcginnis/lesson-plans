# Genesis 22 - The Lord Will Provide

## A difficult command in the story of a promise

Genesis 22 begins by describing a test of Abraham. The KJV word "tempt" in verse 1 has the sense of testing in this account. Isaac is the son through whom the covenant promise has been named, which makes the command especially difficult to understand.

[Read Genesis 22:1-8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/22?lang=eng&id=p1-p8#p1)

Read slowly rather than hurrying to an easy moral. The story concerns a parent, a beloved child, and a command that raises serious questions. It is not a general permission to harm a child or anyone else because someone claims to have received revelation. The command at the decisive moment is to stop and not harm Isaac.

Genesis also knows of Ishmael. The language about Abraham's only son should be read in the particular covenant drama involving Isaac, not used to pretend that Ishmael does not exist or has no value to God.

## Where is the lamb?

Isaac asks his father about the missing offering. Abraham answers:

> "My son, God will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering: so they went both of them together."

Genesis 22:8, KJV excerpt.

Isaac's question is in verse 7. Abraham's earlier statement in verse 5 says that he and the boy will worship and return. Hebrews 11:17-19 interprets Abraham's faith in terms of God's ability to raise the dead. Genesis itself does not give us every thought behind his words.

[Read Hebrews 11:17-19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/11?lang=eng&id=p17-p19#p17)

Abraham has already lived through other experiences of incomplete understanding: leaving for an unknown destination and waiting for a promised child. These connections help us see a history of faith without inventing conversations in which God answers every question with the same phrase.

## Do not harm him

At the crucial moment, the angel of the Lord calls Abraham by name:

> "Lay not thine hand upon the lad, neither do thou any thing unto him."

Genesis 22:12, KJV excerpt.

[Read Genesis 22:9-14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/22?lang=eng&id=p9-p14#p9)

Abraham looks up, sees a ram caught in a thicket, and offers it instead of his son. Do not leave the story at the raised knife. The interruption and the provision belong at the center of its telling.

For a class with children or people affected by trauma, avoid graphic descriptions, reenactments, or questions asking whether someone would harm a family member to prove faith. We can examine trust and divine provision without putting anyone in that position.

## The name remembers the Provider

Genesis 22:14 names the place Jehovah-jireh, commonly rendered "The Lord will provide." Abraham's memorial is not a celebration of his own greatness. It directs attention to the Lord who provides.

The same shift matters in our discipleship. We can recognize the importance of a faithful response without making our resolve the source of salvation. We depend on Jesus Christ and what He gives, not simply on our ability to endure a test.

Trust does not mean that every situation will resolve as this one does. It means bringing our questions and needs to God, acting on sound direction, and seeking help while the way ahead remains unclear.

## Questions to sit with

- What changes when we give the command not to harm Isaac as much attention as the test itself?
- What does Hebrews help us understand about Abraham's trust?
- Why might the name of the place be important to the way this story is remembered?
- Where do we need to shift attention from proving ourselves to receiving the Savior's help?
