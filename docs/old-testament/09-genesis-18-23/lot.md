# Genesis 18-19 - Choices, Mercy, and Intercession

## Abraham asks about justice

Before the account of Sodom's destruction, Abraham asks whether the righteous will be swept away with the wicked. He continues to plead as the number of righteous people under discussion decreases.

> 25. "Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?"

Genesis 18:25, KJV excerpt.

[Read Genesis 18:23-33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/18?lang=eng&id=p23-p33#p23)

Abraham's conversation provides a place to consider intercession and honest questions about justice. He is not indifferent to people who may suffer. The account does not portray God as needing Abraham to teach Him to care; it lets us hear a person bring concern to the Lord.

## Where do we turn our attention?

Read the earlier separation of Abraham and Lot in Genesis 13:8-13. Abraham offers Lot a choice of land. Lot sees the well-watered plain and pitches his tent toward Sodom. Later, Genesis 14:12 describes him living there, and Genesis 19:1 places him at the gate.

[Read Genesis 13:8-13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/13?lang=eng&id=p8-p13#p8)

[Read Genesis 14:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/14?lang=eng&id=p12#p12)

These details invite reflection on the direction of our choices. They do not reveal every motive Lot had or prove that living among people with different beliefs inevitably corrupts someone.

As a companion image, Mosiah 2:6 describes families facing their tents toward the temple. Matthew 6:22 uses the eye as an image of the body's light. Ask what receives our repeated attention and what direction that attention encourages us to take.

[Read Mosiah 2:6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/2?lang=eng&id=p6#p6)

[Read Matthew 6:19-24](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/6?lang=eng&id=p19-p24#p19)

Familiarity can influence what feels normal. That is a useful question to ask about habits and media, not proof that we know Lot's psychology or a reason to fear contact with our neighbors.

## Mercy meets hesitation

The messengers urge Lot and his family to leave. Lot lingers, and they take the family by the hand:

> 16. "The Lord being merciful unto him: and they brought him forth, and set him without the city."

Genesis 19:16, KJV excerpt.

[Read Genesis 19:15-17](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/19?lang=eng&id=p15-p17#p15)

Compare Abraham's haste to welcome his visitors in Genesis 18:2, 6-7 with Lot's hesitation here. The contrast can help us examine our own response to clear direction. It should not become contempt for someone who needs help leaving a harmful situation.

The account includes mercy as well as warning. Sometimes the next step becomes possible because someone helps us take it.

## Read the difficult parts honestly

Genesis 19 includes threatened sexual violence, Lot's troubling response involving his daughters, and later abuse within the family. These are not patterns to imitate. In a short class, summarize without graphic detail and do not invite personal disclosures.

The closing account connects the births of Moab and Ben-ammi with peoples who later interact with Israel. An ancient origin account is not a warrant for judging the worth of their descendants or any modern group. Nor should the violence at Sodom be treated as a description of all people who share a particular identity today.

## Questions to sit with

- What does Abraham's pleading teach us about concern for others?
- What direction do our repeated choices give our lives?
- What do you notice when you read Lot's hesitation alongside the Lord's mercy?
- How can we offer practical help to someone who knows they need to move forward but feels stuck?
