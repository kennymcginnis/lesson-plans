# Abraham and Isaac - Seeing Christ Carefully

## Begin with the connection scripture makes

Jacob explains that the law of Moses points souls toward Christ and describes Abraham's offering of Isaac as:

> "a similitude of God and his Only Begotten Son."

Jacob 4:5, excerpt.

[Read Jacob 4:4-5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/jacob/4?lang=eng&id=p4-p5#p4)

**Explicit scriptural interpretation:** The connection between Abraham and Isaac and the Father and Son is stated in the Book of Mormon. We need not invent it. But that explicit connection does not establish that every detail has a single intended counterpart.

## Three kinds of comparison

- **Scripturally identified likeness:** A later scripture explicitly identifies a person, event, or ordinance as pointing to Christ. Jacob 4:5 provides this foundation for the account.
- **Illustrative parallel:** Two accounts have features that help us reflect on Christ, without an explicit statement that each shared detail was intended as prophecy.
- **Unsupported claim:** An interpretation adds facts not supplied by the text or treats a resemblance as proof of a conclusion it cannot establish.

These distinctions help us find meaning without confusing imagination with revelation.

## A beloved son and a Father's gift

Read Genesis 22:2 with John 3:16 and Romans 8:32. Genesis speaks of Abraham's beloved son; the New Testament bears witness that God gives His Son for us.

[Read John 3:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/3?lang=eng&id=p16#p16)

> "He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?"

Romans 8:32, KJV.

[Read Romans 8:31-39](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rom/8?lang=eng&id=p31-p39#p31)

The focus is God's love and provision. Romans does not promise freedom from suffering; the surrounding passage names tribulation and danger while witnessing that they cannot separate us from God's love in Christ.

## Wood, an offering, and deliverance

Genesis 22:6 describes Isaac carrying the wood. John 19:17 describes Jesus bearing His cross. This is a meaningful illustrative parallel, but neither verse explicitly interprets the other.

[Read Genesis 22:6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/22?lang=eng&id=p6#p6)

[Read John 19:17](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/19?lang=eng&id=p17#p17)

In Genesis 22:13, the ram is offered instead of Isaac. John 1:29 calls Jesus the Lamb of God, and 1 Peter 1:18-19 connects redemption with His precious blood. The relationship is not a perfect one-to-one diagram: Isaac is spared, while Jesus freely accomplishes the saving work for which we need Him.

[Read John 1:29](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/1?lang=eng&id=p29#p29)

[Read 1 Peter 1:18-19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-pet/1?lang=eng&id=p18-p19#p18)

## Do not supply what the accounts leave unstated

Jesus' submission to the Father's will is explicit in Luke 22:42. Genesis 22 does not state Isaac's precise age, strength, or inner willingness. His carrying wood does not establish that he could have overpowered Abraham or that his silence proves consent.

[Read Luke 22:42](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/22?lang=eng&id=p42#p42)

Second Chronicles 3:1 places Solomon's temple on Mount Moriah, providing a biblical connection with the region named in Genesis 22:2. It does not establish that Isaac's altar and Jesus' crucifixion occupied the same exact spot.

[Read 2 Chronicles 3:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/2-chr/3?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)

Sarah is not given a speaking part in Genesis 22. A comparison between her absence from the narrated conversation and Mary's experience at the cross may prompt reflection on mothers, but it is not an explicit scriptural identification. Silence in an account does not tell us what either woman felt or said outside what was recorded.

## The question beneath the comparison

The point is not to collect the largest number of parallels. It is to recognize the Father's love and the Son's gift. We do not make ourselves secure by exaggerating what we know about Isaac; we look to what scripture says about Jesus Christ.

## Questions to sit with

- What does Jacob 4:5 explicitly authorize us to see in the story?
- Which companion passage most clearly helps you understand God's love?
- How can recognizing the limits of a comparison strengthen rather than weaken our study?
- How does the gift of the Son help us trust God while difficult questions remain?
