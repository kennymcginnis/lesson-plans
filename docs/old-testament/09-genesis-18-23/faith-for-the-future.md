# Remember Lot's Wife - Faith for the Future

## A short warning with a long history

> 32. "Remember Lot's wife."

Luke 17:32, KJV.

[Read Luke 17:28-33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/17?lang=eng&id=p28-p33#p28)

Jesus uses the memory of Lot's household in a teaching about readiness and the danger of turning back. Read Genesis 19:17, 26 for the earlier account: the family is told not to look behind, and Lot's wife does so.

[Read Genesis 19:17-26](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/19?lang=eng&id=p17-p26#p17)

Genesis does not give her an extended speech or describe every feeling behind her action. Distinguish the brief account from later interpretations of what it can teach us.

## Jeffrey R. Holland - Remember Lot's Wife

**Verified quotation and interpretation of Luke 17:32 and Genesis 19.** Elder Holland, then a member of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, spoke at a BYU devotional on January 13, 2009. He proposes that Lot's wife's problem was more than a glance backward: she longed to return. He presents this as a considered interpretation, not additional dialogue from Genesis.

> "Faith is for the future. Faith builds on the past but never longs to stay there."

His address encourages people to trust Christ's future and stop continually using earlier mistakes against those who are sincerely repenting, including themselves.

["Remember Lot's Wife," January 13, 2009](https://speeches.byu.edu/talks/jeffrey-r-holland/remember-lots-wife/)

## Learn from the past without living there

Read Philippians 3:12-14. Paul describes pressing toward what lies ahead, not claiming that he is already perfect. Forward movement is compatible with acknowledging unfinished growth.

[Read Philippians 3:12-14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/philip/3?lang=eng&id=p12-p14#p12)

There is a difference between remembering enough to repair harm and repeatedly declaring that a person can never become more than a mistake. Repentance involves responsibility and change; it need not become endless humiliation.

Holland also recalls a difficult moment when he wanted to give up his education and return home. His wife, Patricia, helped him see a future worth continuing toward. We can sometimes offer that kind of perspective to each other without pretending to know every outcome.

## Forgiveness and wise boundaries

Encouragement to leave past wrongdoing behind is not an instruction to suppress trauma, erase necessary records, or restore trust before it is warranted. Healing can take time. Accountability, appropriate boundaries, and professional support may remain necessary.

The application is to stop making hopelessness our final judgment about ourselves or others. Jesus Christ makes repentance and a changed future possible.

## Questions to sit with

- What is the difference between learning from the past and wishing we could remain there?
- How can we acknowledge a wrong without making it someone's permanent identity?
- Who has helped you see a future when you could see only reasons to retreat?
- What would one forward-looking act of faith look like this week?
