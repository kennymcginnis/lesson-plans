# I Know That My Redeemer Lives

Week 33 - Job

Calendar reference: August 10-16, 2026. This is the curriculum week, not a confirmed teaching date.

Assigned reading: Job 1-3; 12-14; 19; 21-24; 38-40; 42.

> What can we trust about our Redeemer when suffering does not make sense?

Job challenges the assumption that prosperity always proves righteousness or that suffering proves wrongdoing. His lament, his friends' explanations, and his encounter with God invite us to consider honest prayer, the limits of our understanding, and trust in the Redeemer.

## A path through Job

Begin with a person described as upright, not with a hidden sin that supposedly explains his losses. Follow the movement from grief and companionship into argument, a plea for vindication, and an encounter with God. The ending challenges the friends' confident accusations without making every word Job has spoken a complete account of God's ways.

The recurring hymn theme, "I Know That My Redeemer Lives," gives a Christian response to the book's questions. It should deepen compassion, not require people to sound cheerful or say they are grateful for a tragedy.

## A 25-minute class

1. **Challenge the assumption, 4 minutes:** Read Job 1:1 and 2:3. Ask why suffering cannot be used as proof that someone has done wrong.
2. **Be with the person, 7 minutes:** Read Job 2:11-13 and 3:1-3. Discuss the difference between presence, listening, and an explanation offered too quickly.
3. **Hope in a Redeemer, 8 minutes:** Read Job 19:23-27, then Job 38:1-7. Consider hope beyond our understanding without pretending these verses answer every question about suffering.
4. **Respond with humility and care, 6 minutes:** Read Job 42:5-8. Choose one act of support or one honest prayer, reflect on the hymn's witness of Christ, and close with testimony.

Use the fuller debate and restoration studies for preparation or follow-up. Protect time for reflection, especially when the subject touches recent grief.

## Scriptures to emphasize

Job 1-2 introduces the suffering of a person described as upright. Job 19:25-26 gives voice to hope in a living Redeemer. Job 38-42 challenges limited explanations and brings the conversation into God's presence.

## Reading the book carefully

Job is wisdom literature, with a prose frame and extended poetic speeches. Distinguish the narrator, Job, his friends, Elihu, and the Lord. A statement appears in scripture without necessarily being endorsed as sound counsel by the book's conclusion.

The studies use KJV excerpts and clearly distinguish Christian applications from questions about the ancient poem's language. Neither Job's restoration nor the promise of redemption is a formula guaranteeing replacement possessions, restored health, or an explanation on our timetable.
