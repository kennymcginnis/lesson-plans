# Closing - Trust, Speak Honestly, and Stay Near

## Remember the living Redeemer

> "For I know that my redeemer liveth."

Job 19:25, KJV excerpt.

[Read Job 19:23-27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/job/19?lang=eng&id=p23-p27#p23)

The book invites trust without giving us permission to explain every loss. It gives suffering people room to lament and warns those beside them against turning limited understanding into accusation.

## Gather the lesson

- **Integrity:** Job's suffering is not evidence of the concealed wrongdoing his friends imagine.
- **Presence:** We can mourn with someone before attempting to solve or explain their pain.
- **Honesty:** Grief, questions, and faith can appear in the same life and prayer.
- **Humility:** God knows more than we do; we should not claim His complete perspective.
- **Redemption:** Christians place their hope in Jesus Christ, whose living care and Resurrection reach beyond our present understanding.

The hymn *I Know That My Redeemer Lives* can be read or sung as a response. Invite class members to notice a phrase about comfort, intercession, or being heard. Participation need not require anyone to claim feelings they do not presently have.

## Questions to sit with

- What assumption about suffering has this reading challenged?
- What would it mean to bring your real questions to God rather than an edited version of them?
- Who needs your presence or practical care more than your explanation?

## One response for the week

Choose one honest prayer and one act of care. Offer company, a meal, help with a task, or support in finding appropriate professional assistance. Ask what would help rather than assume.

Keep personal grief private unless someone freely chooses to share it. Close with your own testimony of Jesus Christ. The invitation is to trust the Redeemer and care for one another, not to prove that pain no longer hurts.
