# Job 1-3 - Faith without a Bargain

## Upright before the losses

Job 1:1 describes Job as perfect and upright, fearing God and turning from evil. Here, "perfect" conveys integrity or blamelessness; it need not imply that Job possesses divine perfection or will never need to grow.

[Read Job 1:1-12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/job/1?lang=eng&id=p1-p12#p1)

The opening gives him a household and substantial wealth. The adversary challenges whether his devotion is merely payment for protection and prosperity: would Job still trust God without the benefits?

That question exposes a transactional understanding of faith. We can ask what our relationship with God rests on without pretending that losing people, health, or livelihood is a small test of attitude.

## Loss and worship together

After the reports of catastrophe, Job tears his clothing, shaves his head, falls to the ground, and worships. His mourning and worship occur together.

[Read Job 1:13-22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/job/1?lang=eng&id=p13-p22#p13)

Do not make grief and faith into mutually exclusive choices. Job is not required to remain composed in order for the narrator to recognize his integrity.

The heavenly scene is part of the book's literary framing. It does not authorize us to claim that a particular person's tragedy resulted from an analogous heavenly conversation. Job himself is not given that scene as an explanation of his experience.

## Faithfulness does not immediately end the trial

Chapter 2 adds physical suffering. The Lord again affirms Job's integrity and speaks of what has happened "without cause" in verse 3. That directly challenges the friends' later assumption that his losses prove concealed wrongdoing.

[Read Job 2:1-10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/job/2?lang=eng&id=p1-p10#p1)

Job's wife also belongs to the grieving household. Her short, painful speech is not a complete account of her character or a reason to treat her with contempt. Job's answer should not become a rebuke we deliver to someone overwhelmed by loss.

In the KJV, receiving "evil" in verse 10 concerns calamity in this exchange, not a command to commit evil or accept abuse without seeking protection.

## Chapter 3 belongs in the story

Job curses the day of his birth and asks anguished questions about life. The earlier words of trust do not prevent later lament.

[Read Job 3:1-3, 20-26](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/job/3?lang=eng&id=p1-p3,p20-p26#p1)

Allow the language to communicate the depth of his distress without diagnosing him from a chapter. If someone expresses a current wish to die or intent to harm themselves, take it seriously and help them connect with appropriate crisis or emergency support. A lesson discussion is not sufficient care.

## Questions to sit with

- Why does the narrator establish Job's integrity before describing his suffering?
- How do Job's actions show that mourning and worship can coexist?
- What is lost when we make faith a bargain for prosperity?
- How can we respond to another person's distress without demanding that they repeat Job's most confident words?
