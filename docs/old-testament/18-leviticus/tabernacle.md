# Exodus 35-40 - A Place for God's Presence

## Willing hearts and willing hands

Before considering the regulations in Leviticus, notice the people building the tabernacle. Exodus 35 describes people bringing materials willingly and contributing skill. The work includes both gifts and craftsmanship. In Exodus 36:5-7, the contributions become more than the work requires.

[Read Exodus 35:20-29](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ex/35?lang=eng&id=p20-p29#p20)

[Read Exodus 35:30-35](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ex/35?lang=eng&id=p30-p35#p30)

[Read Exodus 36:1-7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ex/36?lang=eng&id=p1-p7#p1)

God's people contribute more than money. Wisdom, skill, teaching, time, and willing work have a place. This can broaden our understanding of what it means to offer something to the Lord.

## Glory fills the tabernacle

Exodus 37-39 describes the furnishings, priestly clothing, and completion of the work. In chapter 40, the tabernacle is set up and the Lord's glory fills it:

> "And Moses was not able to enter into the tent of the congregation, because the cloud abode thereon, and the glory of the Lord filled the tabernacle."

Exodus 40:35, KJV.

[Read Exodus 40:33-38](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ex/40?lang=eng&id=p33-p38#p33)

The presence of God is the purpose of the sanctuary, not a decorative addition to a finished building. The closing scene also leaves us with a question: how can people approach that holy presence?

## The Lord calls from the tabernacle

Leviticus begins:

> "And the Lord called unto Moses, and spake unto him out of the tabernacle of the congregation, saying,"

Leviticus 1:1, KJV.

[Read Leviticus 1:1-4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/1?lang=eng&id=p1-p4#p1)

Later, Numbers 1:1 describes the Lord speaking to Moses in the tabernacle. This sequence offers a helpful way to read Leviticus: God provides instructions for a people called to live near Him.

[Read Numbers 1:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/num/1?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)

Do not make the change of wording carry more than the accounts say. Exodus 40 describes the overwhelming presence of God's glory; it does not accuse Moses of a particular sin. Leviticus addresses offerings, priests, purity, atonement, and holy living, not simply an entrance procedure for one person.

## More than a list of restrictions

It is easy to read an unfamiliar law and ask only, "Why would anyone do that?" Add another question: "What does this practice teach this covenant community about life with God?"

The answer will not always be obvious. We do not need to invent a symbolic explanation for every detail. We can begin with what Leviticus states clearly: God is holy, He calls His people to holiness, and He provides a way for cleansing and reconciliation.

## Questions to sit with

- What kinds of contributions do you notice in the building of the tabernacle?
- How does the ending of Exodus change the questions you bring to Leviticus?
- What is the difference between treating worship as a task and seeking God's presence through it?
- What could a willing heart contribute to our congregation or household this week?
