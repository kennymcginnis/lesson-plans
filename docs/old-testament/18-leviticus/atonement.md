# Leviticus 16 - Christ Opens the Way

## The Day of Atonement

Leviticus 16 describes a solemn ceremony involving the high priest, the sanctuary, and the whole congregation. Aaron must prepare and make an offering for himself as well as for the people. Even the one serving as priest needs atonement.

[Read Leviticus 16:2-6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/16?lang=eng&id=p2-p6#p2)

Follow the two goats in verses 15-22. One is offered as a sin offering, and its blood is brought within the veil. Over the living goat, Aaron confesses the people's sins; that goat is sent into the wilderness, bearing those iniquities away.

[Read Leviticus 16:15-22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/16?lang=eng&id=p15-p22#p15)

The images bring cleansing and removal together. Verse 24 also describes Aaron washing and changing his garments as the ceremony continues. This is not a performance of personal self-sufficiency. The sanctuary, the priest, and the congregation all appear within the provision God makes.

> 30. "For on that day shall the priest make an atonement for you, to cleanse you, that ye may be clean from all your sins before the Lord."

Leviticus 16:30, KJV.

[Read Leviticus 16:24-34](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/16?lang=eng&id=p24-p34#p24)

## A symbol is not the Savior

Hebrews 10:4 says that the blood of bulls and goats cannot take away sins. Read that alongside Hebrews 9 rather than concluding that the ancient ordinances were meaningless. Hebrews interprets them as anticipatory patterns whose saving fulfillment is in Christ.

[Read Hebrews 10:1-4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/10?lang=eng&id=p1-p4#p1)

**Explicit scriptural interpretation:** Hebrews 9 compares the priest's sanctuary service with Jesus Christ's entry into God's presence on our behalf. This is a connection made by the New Testament itself, not merely a modern analogy.

> 24. "For Christ is not entered into the holy places made with hands, which are the figures of the true; but into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us:"

Hebrews 9:24, KJV.

[Read Hebrews 9:24-28](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/9?lang=eng&id=p24-p28#p24)

The high priest's annual observance is contrasted with Christ offering Himself once. Jesus does not need to repeat His Atonement each time we repent or worship. Our repeated acts of faith respond to His completed sacrifice.

Hebrews 9:12 speaks of Christ obtaining eternal redemption through His own blood. Verse 28 speaks of His appearing a second time unto salvation. These are related but distinct statements.

[Read Hebrews 9:7-12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/9?lang=eng&id=p7-p12#p7)

## Cleansing and the removal of sin

The two goats can help us reflect on the cost of reconciliation and the removal of guilt. The central Christian claim rests on Christ's self-offering, not on assigning one certain meaning to every ritual detail. The term associated with the goat sent away has a history of differing interpretations; this lesson does not require settling them.

Leviticus 16:33 includes the priests and all the congregation. Revelation 5:9 widens our view to the Lamb redeeming people from every kindred, tongue, people, and nation. This is a companion vision of the reach of Christ's redemption, not a claim that the two verses describe the same ceremony.

[Read Revelation 5:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/5?lang=eng&id=p9#p9)

## The way in begins with God's love

Romans 5:8 teaches that Christ died for us while we were still sinners. We do not first make ourselves perfectly lovable and then persuade Him to receive us. His love makes repentance and return possible.

[Read Romans 5:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rom/5?lang=eng&id=p8#p8)

Holiness, service, and daily discipleship matter, but they do not replace the Savior. The movement of the lesson is not from imperfect people to impressive people who no longer need grace. It is toward people who trust Christ enough to draw near and let Him change them.

## Questions to sit with

- What do the images of cleansing and carrying sin away help you understand about forgiveness?
- Why does it matter that Christ offers Himself, rather than only something outside Himself?
- How does His once-for-all sacrifice change the way we think about repeated repentance?
- What might keep someone outside when God is inviting them to receive mercy?
