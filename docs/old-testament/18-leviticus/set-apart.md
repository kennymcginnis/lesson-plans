# Leviticus 19 - Set Apart to Belong to God

## Be holy, for I am holy

> "Ye shall be holy: for I the Lord your God am holy."

Leviticus 19:2, KJV excerpt.

[Read Leviticus 19:1-4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/19?lang=eng&id=p1-p4#p1)

Leviticus 20:26 connects holiness with being separated to belong to the Lord. Being set apart has a purpose: a covenant relationship with Him. It is not difference for its own sake or a claim of greater human worth.

[Read Leviticus 20:26](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/20?lang=eng&id=p26#p26)

## Doctrine, principle, and application

One useful study approach is to distinguish a truth about God, the direction that truth gives our lives, and a particular practice through which people respond.

- **Doctrine:** God is holy and calls His people to belong to Him.
- **Principle:** Our relationship with Him should shape the way we live.
- **Application:** A covenant community follows particular instructions in its circumstances.

Leviticus includes Sabbath observance, dietary distinctions, and grooming regulations. Read Leviticus 23:3; 11:7-8; and 19:27 as examples. These instructions mattered in Israel's covenant life; they are not automatically a list of practices Christians must reproduce unchanged.

[Read Leviticus 23:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/23?lang=eng&id=p3#p3)

[Read Leviticus 11:7-8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/11?lang=eng&id=p7-p8#p7)

[Read Leviticus 19:27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/19?lang=eng&id=p27#p27)

For Latter-day Saints, Sabbath worship, the Word of Wisdom, and choices consistent with our covenants can invite a similar examination of daily life. These are present-day applications, not claims that Israel's food laws and the Word of Wisdom are identical.

## Holiness includes how we treat people

Do not stop with the most visible differences. Leviticus 19 also requires leaving food for the poor and the stranger, honesty, fair treatment, and refusing revenge.

> "Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am the Lord."

Leviticus 19:18, KJV excerpt.

[Read Leviticus 19:9-18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/19?lang=eng&id=p9-p18#p9)

Verse 34 extends the instruction to the stranger living among the people. Their memory of being strangers in Egypt should shape their hospitality.

[Read Leviticus 19:33-34](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/19?lang=eng&id=p33-p34#p33)

A community can look distinctive yet fail to love. Leviticus does not let us separate devotion to God from justice, generosity, and the dignity of our neighbors.

## Questions to sit with

- What is one gospel standard that helps you remember whom you belong to? How has keeping it blessed your life?
- How might our Sabbath choices express devotion rather than simply difference?
- What do our choices about consumption and appearance express about our commitments, without making them a measure of someone else's worth?
- How do verses 9-18 and 33-34 challenge a merely outward idea of holiness?
