# Sacrifice - Wholehearted, Willing, and Daily

Main readings: Leviticus 1; 4; 22:18-23; Exodus 29:38-39. Companion readings: Isaiah 53:6; John 10:17-18; Luke 9:23.

## An offering on a person's behalf

> "And he shall put his hand upon the head of the burnt offering; and it shall be accepted for him to make atonement for him."

Leviticus 1:4, KJV.

[Read Leviticus 1:3-9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/1?lang=eng&id=p3-p9#p3)

The burnt offering is presented before the Lord and burned on the altar. Its extensive offering of the animal can prompt a devotional question: what would it mean to offer our whole lives to God rather than reserve a part where He is unwelcome?

Read Isaiah 53:6 alongside the offering: the Lord lays the iniquity of all upon His servant. Christians read this passage in relation to Jesus Christ. The comparison points us toward His saving work; it does not mean that an animal's death independently accomplishes what Christ alone can do.

[Read Isaiah 53:6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/53?lang=eng&id=p6#p6)

Jesus gave Himself wholly for us so that we might be made holy in Him.

## A way to respond when wrongdoing becomes known

Leviticus 4:27-31 describes a person who sins through ignorance and then becomes aware of the wrongdoing. The person brings an offering, and the passage ends with a promise of forgiveness.

[Read Leviticus 4:27-31](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/4?lang=eng&id=p27-p31#p27)

Notice both accountability and a way forward. Becoming aware of a wrong is not a reason to hide forever. We can acknowledge it, turn toward God, and seek forgiveness. Our Christian response centers on repentance and Christ's Atonement, not repeating the animal offering.

## Freely given

Leviticus 22:18-23 includes offerings brought in connection with vows or given freely. Consider willingness as a quality of our response to God.

[Read Leviticus 22:18-23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/22?lang=eng&id=p18-p23#p18)

Jesus describes His own offering as voluntary:

> "No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself."

John 10:18, KJV excerpt.

[Read John 10:17-18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/10?lang=eng&id=p17-p18#p17)

As a hypothetical example, imagine a parent who feels worn down by repeated care tasks. Remembering the love behind that care might help them recover a sense of purpose. It does not make exhaustion imaginary or remove the need for rest, shared responsibility, and support. Willing service is not a demand to pretend that every sacrifice feels easy.

## Offered daily

Exodus 29:38-39 describes a morning and evening offering. Luke 9:23 gives disciples a daily invitation to take up their cross and follow Jesus.

[Read Exodus 29:38-39](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ex/29?lang=eng&id=p38-p39#p38)

[Read Luke 9:23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/9?lang=eng&id=p23#p23)

A dramatic sacrifice can be easier to imagine than years of steady faithfulness. Daily scripture study, repentance, kindness, and keeping commitments give our devotion a repeated expression.

Wholehearted, willing, and daily are three lenses for reflection, not three mutually exclusive categories in the sacrificial laws. A regularly offered sacrifice can also be a burnt offering. The modern applications here are thematic comparisons.

### A teaching attributed to Thomas S. Monson

> "Each of us can be true for just one day--and then one more and then one more after that"

This excerpt is attributed to Thomas S. Monson, but its original address and publication have not been independently verified for these notes. It illustrates daily faithfulness; Luke 9:23 supplies the scriptural foundation. Do not present it as a verified conference citation until its source is established.

## Questions to sit with

- What does wholehearted devotion look like without confusing it with perfectionism or burnout?
- How can remembering why we chose a commitment change the way we approach it?
- Which matters more in your life right now: a dramatic gesture or a small, repeated act?
- Choose one lens: wholehearted, willing, or daily. How could it shape an offering of your time, attention, or service this week?
