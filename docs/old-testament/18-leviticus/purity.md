# Purity - Christ Draws Near

Main readings: Leviticus 13:45-46; 15:19, 25; Mark 1:40-42; 5:25-34.

## Understand the word unclean

Leviticus describes ritual conditions that affect participation in the community's sacred life. Skin disease, bodily discharges, and contact with certain unclean things are among the situations addressed. These categories can feel unfamiliar and painful to modern readers.

[Read Leviticus 13:45-46](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/13?lang=eng&id=p45-p46#p45)

[Read Leviticus 15:19, 25](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/15?lang=eng&id=p19,p25#p19)

Do not use ritual uncleanness as a synonym for wickedness. Menstruation, illness, and other bodily experiences do not make a person morally inferior. The laws describe ritual status and procedures; a particular person's illness is not evidence of personal sin.

Leviticus also addresses guilt and offerings related to impurity in passages such as 5:2-6. Read those instructions in their context rather than collapsing every use of uncleanness and every kind of wrongdoing into one category.

[Read Leviticus 5:2-6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lev/5?lang=eng&id=p2-p6#p2)

## I will; be thou clean

A man with leprosy comes to Jesus, kneels, and asks whether Jesus is willing to make him clean. Mark describes the response:

> 41. "And Jesus, moved with compassion, put forth his hand, and touched him, and saith unto him, I will; be thou clean."

Mark 1:41, KJV.

[Read Mark 1:40-44](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/mark/1?lang=eng&id=p40-p44#p40)

Notice compassion, touch, and cleansing together. Jesus also tells the man to show himself to the priest and offer what Moses commanded. The account is not simply a declaration that Israel's law never mattered. It reveals the Savior's willingness and power to help a person whose condition affected his place in the community.

## Daughter, go in peace

A woman who has suffered bleeding for twelve years approaches Jesus through the crowd. Mark describes her long suffering and unsuccessful efforts to obtain help. She touches His clothing and is healed.

> 34. "Daughter, thy faith hath made thee whole; go in peace, and be whole of thy plague."

Mark 5:34, KJV.

[Read Mark 5:25-34](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/mark/5?lang=eng&id=p25-p34#p25)

Read her story with Leviticus 15:25 in mind, while remembering that Mark does not narrate every social consequence she experienced. Jesus does not reduce her to a condition or a problem. He addresses her as daughter and speaks peace.

These healings witness of Christ's power. They do not justify promising that every illness will disappear immediately if someone believes strongly enough. Continuing illness is not proof of deficient faith.

## Cleansing that reaches outward

Some purity regulations describe uncleanness passing through contact. In these Gospel encounters, we see Jesus bringing healing and cleansing to those who approach Him. His holiness is not fragile in the face of their need.

As a devotional image, we might say that His holiness reaches outward. This is not a medical theory of contagion or a claim that our presence automatically transforms every harmful situation.

First Corinthians 15:33 warns about corrupting influences. We can take that warning seriously while refusing to treat people who are ill, isolated, or different from us as threats to our goodness. Compassion and appropriate boundaries can coexist.

[Read 1 Corinthians 15:33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/15?lang=eng&id=p33#p33)

### A question attributed to Wendy Watson Nelson

> "What would a holy [person] do?"

This adapted question is attributed to Wendy Watson Nelson in a message titled "One Question That Can Change Your Life." Its original publication and exact wording have not been independently verified for these notes. It is a reflective prompt, not an apostolic quotation or a direct citation of Leviticus.

Ask the question in the light of Jesus' actions: what response would combine compassion, integrity, and wise care for everyone involved?

## Questions to sit with

- What changes when we distinguish ritual status from a person's worth or moral character?
- What do Jesus' actions and words teach us about His willingness to receive people?
- Who might need to experience belonging rather than avoidance in our community?
- How can we be a good influence while keeping boundaries that protect health and safety?
