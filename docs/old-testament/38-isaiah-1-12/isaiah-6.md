# Isaiah 6:1-8 - Here Am I; Send Me

## Isaiah 6:8

> 8. Also I heard the voice of the Lord, saying, Whom shall I send, and who will go for us? Then said I, Here am I; send me.

[Read Isaiah 6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/6?lang=eng&id=p1-p8#p1)

## Notice what comes before the invitation

Isaiah sees the Lord's holiness and becomes painfully aware of his own uncleanness. In verses 6-7, the live coal touches his lips and he is told that his iniquity is taken away. Only then does he volunteer.

The sequence matters: awareness of weakness, cleansing, and a willing response. Isaiah's willingness is not a claim that he has never needed help. Neither is the live coal an instruction to imitate literally; it is part of the vision's imagery of purification.

The rest of the chapter also keeps us from assuming that willingness guarantees an easy assignment or an immediately receptive audience.

### John C. Pingree Jr. - I Have a Work for Thee

**Explicit citation of Isaiah 6:8; quotation from Abraham 3:27.** Elder Pingree, of the Seventy, describes Jesus Christ's premortal willingness to do the Father's work:

> 27. "Here am I, send me"

The address cites Abraham 3:27 and adds "see also Isaiah 6:8." Its primary scene is the premortal Savior, not Isaiah's temple vision. The connection is the willingness to offer ourselves to God and His work.

["I Have a Work for Thee," October 2017](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2017/10/i-have-a-work-for-thee?lang=eng)

### Susan H. Porter - Here Am I, Send Me

**Thematic connection to Isaiah 6:8; the premortal response is cited to Abraham 3:27.** President Porter, Primary General President, invites children to offer themselves to Heavenly Father and act on promptings to help others.

> "Heavenly Father can provide miracles when you simply offer what you have."

Her examples include comforting someone who is lonely, helping family, and teaching someone to pray. She also explains that children do not need to solve every problem around them. We can offer what we have without expecting ourselves to fix everything.

["Here Am I, Send Me," April 2026](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2026/04/54porter?lang=eng)

## Questions to sit with

- What difference does it make that Isaiah receives cleansing before volunteering?
- How can recognizing a weakness make us more dependent on God rather than unwilling to serve?
- What small act of service could be a sincere response to "Whom shall I send?"
- How can we offer what we have without assuming we must fix everything?
