# Isaiah 6:1-8 - Here Am I; Send Me

Example answers are starting points, not the only right responses. Personal examples are hypothetical.

## Isaiah 6:8

> 8. Also I heard the voice of the Lord, saying, Whom shall I send, and who will go for us? Then said I, Here am I; send me.

[Read Isaiah 6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/6?lang=eng&id=p1-p8#p1)

## Notice what comes before the invitation

Isaiah sees the Lord's holiness and becomes painfully aware of his own uncleanness. In verses 6-7, the live coal touches his lips and he is told that his iniquity is taken away. Only then does he volunteer.

The sequence matters: awareness of weakness, cleansing, and a willing response. Isaiah's willingness is not a claim that he has never needed help. Neither is the live coal an instruction to imitate literally; it is part of the vision's imagery of purification.

The rest of the chapter also keeps us from assuming that willingness guarantees an easy assignment or an immediately receptive audience.

### John C. Pingree Jr. - I Have a Work for Thee

**Explicit citation of Isaiah 6:8; quotation from Abraham 3:27.** Elder Pingree, of the Seventy, describes Jesus Christ's premortal willingness to do the Father's work:

> 27. "Here am I, send me"

The address cites Abraham 3:27 and adds "see also Isaiah 6:8." Its primary scene is the premortal Savior, not Isaiah's temple vision. The connection is the willingness to offer ourselves to God and His work.

["I Have a Work for Thee," October 2017](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2017/10/i-have-a-work-for-thee?lang=eng)

### Susan H. Porter - Here Am I, Send Me

**Thematic connection to Isaiah 6:8; the premortal response is cited to Abraham 3:27.** President Porter, Primary General President, invites children to offer themselves to Heavenly Father and act on promptings to help others.

> "Heavenly Father can provide miracles when you simply offer what you have."

Her examples include comforting someone who is lonely, helping family, and teaching someone to pray. She also explains that children do not need to solve every problem around them. We can offer what we have without expecting ourselves to fix everything.

["Here Am I, Send Me," April 2026](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2026/04/54porter?lang=eng)

## What do you expect God to do?

Imagine the scene before explaining its symbols. Try reading verses 1-5 that way: a throne, a temple filled with the Lord's train, voices declaring His holiness, shaking doorposts, and smoke. Here, "train" means the trailing part of a robe.

What would you notice first? Which detail helps you understand why Isaiah responds with such alarm?

<details>

The shaking doorposts and voices declaring the Lord's holiness make the scene feel overwhelming. They help me understand Isaiah's awareness of his own uncleanness before God.

</details>

Isaiah identifies himself as a person of unclean lips among a people of unclean lips. His words reveal distress and awareness of his need in the Lord's holy presence.

Before reading further, ask: **what do you expect to happen when someone admits their need before God?** Then read verses 6-7. What actually happens?

<details>
<summary>Show an example answer</summary>

Someone might expect rejection or punishment. In this scene, a seraph touches Isaiah's lips with a coal from the altar and announces that his iniquity is taken away and his sin purged.

</details>

## The surprise is cleansing

A seraph brings a live coal from the altar to Isaiah's lips. The message is that his iniquity is taken away and his sin purged. The place he names as unclean becomes the place where he receives cleansing.

How does the announcement of cleansing compare with what you expected? Isaiah does not tell us what he thought the coal would do; distinguish your expectations from the events recorded in the passage.

<details>
<summary>Show an example answer</summary>

"I expected Isaiah to be sent away, but he receives cleansing." Another reader might expect mercy already; either response can lead us back to what the passage actually says.

</details>

Now compare Isaiah's words in verse 5 with his response in verse 8. What happened between his distress and his willingness to go? He receives cleansing before he offers himself for service. His usefulness does not depend on a past without mistakes.

<details>
<summary>Show an example answer</summary>

The announcement of cleansing comes between his acknowledgment of uncleanness and his offer to serve. He responds to help he has received rather than first proving himself flawless.

</details>

- What difference is there between recognizing a wrong and deciding you can never be useful to God?
- Why might receiving help make someone more willing to help others?
- What does this scene help you trust about the Lord?

<details>
<summary>Show example answers</summary>

- **Wrongdoing and usefulness:** Admitting a wrong names something that needs to change; deciding I can never be useful denies the possibility of forgiveness and growth.
- **Receiving and offering help:** Being helped can make us more compassionate and give us courage to support someone else.
- **Trust:** The Lord can cleanse and call someone who acknowledges their need. Weakness does not make turning to Him pointless.

</details>

## Turning toward Christ in worship

In our own worship, we can remember Jesus Christ, repent, and renew our willingness to follow Him. You do not need to announce private mistakes to explore what His mercy means. What could help you approach worship as an opportunity to turn toward Him rather than a performance of already being perfect?

<details>
<summary>Show an example answer</summary>

I could come with an honest prayer for help and focus on remembering Christ, instead of comparing myself with people who appear to have everything together.

</details>

## Willing does not mean everything becomes easy

Read verses 9-13 as well as verse 8. Isaiah's commission includes difficult words about people not understanding and devastation ahead. These verses are more sobering than a promise that everyone will welcome his message.

As we share what we have learned of Christ, we can be patient and respectful, without pressuring others or measuring our faith by their response. A sincere witness can also be a small act of care or an honest statement of something we are still learning.

Privately finish one thought: **"Something I am beginning to understand about Jesus Christ is..."** Questions and unfinished understanding are welcome too.

<details>
<summary>Show an example answer</summary>

"Something I am beginning to understand about Jesus Christ is that needing His help does not mean I have nothing to offer." Another honest response could be, "I want to understand better how to trust His forgiveness."

</details>

## Questions to sit with

- What difference does it make that Isaiah receives cleansing before volunteering?
- How can recognizing a weakness make us more dependent on God rather than unwilling to serve?
- What small act of service could be a sincere response to "Whom shall I send?"
- How can we offer what we have without assuming we must fix everything?

<details>
<summary>Show example answers</summary>

- **Cleansing first:** Service is a response to God's grace, not a way to earn the right to approach Him.
- **Depending on God:** Recognizing a weakness can prompt us to pray, prepare, and accept help rather than give up.
- **A small act:** Check in on someone who seems isolated or offer practical help at home.
- **Appropriate limits:** Do what is within our ability, involve trusted adults when needed, and respect other people's choices instead of taking responsibility for every outcome.

</details>
