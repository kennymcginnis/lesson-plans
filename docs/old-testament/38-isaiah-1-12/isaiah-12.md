# Isaiah 12:1-6 - I Will Trust and Not Be Afraid

Example answers are starting points, not the only right responses. Personal examples are hypothetical.

## Isaiah 12:2-3

> 2. Behold, God is my salvation; I will trust, and not be afraid: for the Lord Jehovah is my strength and my song; he also is become my salvation.
> 3. Therefore with joy shall ye draw water out of the wells of salvation.

[Read Isaiah 12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/12?lang=eng)

## The response to God's saving work

Isaiah 12 is short enough to read aloud in full. Coming after Isaiah 11's promises of righteous rule, peace, and gathering, it gives words to a people's response: praise, trust, joy, and witness.

Verse 1 speaks of anger turned away and comfort received. Verse 2 names God, not personal strength, as the source of salvation. Verses 4-6 turn outward: declare His doings, sing, and rejoice because the Holy One of Israel is among His people.

The opening phrase "in that day" connects the song with the preceding promises. We can also pray its words now without claiming that every promised condition is already fully realized.

## Strength, song, and water

Notice the images rather than hurry past them. Strength sustains us; a song gives voice to what we remember and love; water is something we need repeatedly. What do these images suggest about depending on the Lord?

<details>
<summary>Show an example answer</summary>

Depending on Him is an ongoing relationship, not a one-time rescue. We return for strength and renewal, and our relationship can include joy as well as requests for help.

</details>

As a companion scripture, read John 4:13-14, where Jesus speaks of living water. This is a thematic scriptural pairing, not a claim that John explicitly cites Isaiah 12:3.

## A song you can make your own

Notice how personal the opening of Isaiah 12 sounds. Look for "I," "me," and "my" in verses 1-2. The Lord's saving work concerns His people together, but it also reaches individual lives. The chapter then opens outward into an invitation to declare His works and rejoice together.

Read verse 2 again, slowly. What changes when "God is my salvation" becomes a statement you are considering for yourself, not only something someone else believes?

<details>
<summary>Show an example answer</summary>

The verse becomes a question about my own trust and choices. I can begin seeking His help personally instead of only repeating what others say, even while my faith is still developing.

</details>

You do not have to force certainty you do not feel. You could begin with a prayer such as, "Help me learn to trust You with this."

## Remembering what God has done

Verse 5 invites praise for the excellent things the Lord has done. Think of something specific for which you can sincerely give thanks: an opportunity to begin again, help from another person, a moment of comfort, or something you have learned about Christ.

You might write a few private lines:

- Something good I want to remember is...
- Something I still need help with is...
- One reason I want to keep turning toward God is...

<details>
<summary>Show example responses</summary>

- **Something good:** "Someone listened when I needed help."
- **Something unresolved:** "I still need help knowing how to repair a friendship."
- **A reason to return:** "I want to learn to love and forgive as Christ does."

These are imagined examples, not experiences you are expected to have had.

</details>

Gratitude and unresolved sorrow can exist together. This is not a test of whether you have enough positive experiences or a reason to compare your life with someone else's.

We can hope for a future when today's suffering no longer defines life. That is a hope in God's completed redeeming work, not a promise that every difficulty will disappear on our preferred schedule. Isaiah 12:6 centers the joy in the Holy One of Israel being among His people. Compare that with [Immanuel, God with Us](old-testament/38-isaiah-1-12/isaiah-7.md): His presence matters now as well as in the future.

## Build a picture of Christ from the words

Read for the Lord's character before trying to explain every detail. Read all six verses and make two short lists: **what the Lord does** and **how His people respond**. Keep the verse number beside each discovery.

For example, verse 1 describes comfort; verse 2 speaks of salvation and strength; verse 6 celebrates His presence. Praise, trust, joy, and declaring His works are responses. Keeping those lists distinct helps us see both God's action and the relationship it invites.

Choose one image to explore:

- **Strength:** What kind of help might someone need even when their circumstances cannot change immediately?
- **Song:** What can a meaningful song do for you that a list of instructions cannot? Why might someone call the Lord their song?
- **Presence:** How is being accompanied different from being handed a solution and left alone?

<details>
<summary>Show example answers</summary>

- **Strength:** Someone may need courage for the next day, patient companionship, or help carrying ordinary responsibilities while a difficulty continues.
- **Song:** A meaningful song can give words to feelings, restore courage, or remind us we are not alone. Calling the Lord our song can express joy and gratitude in knowing Him.
- **Presence:** A solution addresses a task; companionship offers continuing care as we face uncertainty and changing needs.

</details>

What draws your heart toward God beyond the rules you associate with religion? Which discovery in the passage gives you a reason to want to know Him better? You can answer from the words themselves; a dramatic personal experience is not required.

<details>
<summary>Show an example answer</summary>

The comfort in verse 1 and God's presence in verse 6 make the relationship appealing. I want to know someone who offers care and salvation, not just instructions.

</details>

## Questions to sit with

- What changes between the fear in Isaiah 7 and the trust expressed here?
- Which image speaks to you most today: strength, song, or water?
- What has God done that you want to remember or give thanks for?
- What might "I will trust" look like before a difficult situation is resolved?

<details>
<summary>Show example answers</summary>

- **Fear and trust:** Isaiah 7 describes people facing danger; Isaiah 12 voices praise for God's saving work. The contrast directs attention toward the Lord, not toward pretending danger never existed.
- **An image:** Water suggests something I need repeatedly, which helps me picture returning to God rather than expecting one prayer to settle everything.
- **Gratitude:** Someone could give thanks for Christ's promise of forgiveness or for care received through another person, even without a dramatic experience.
- **Trust before resolution:** Keep praying honestly, seek wise support, and choose the next responsible action without demanding immediate certainty.

</details>

Ongoing anxiety or fear does not mean you lack faith. You can turn toward God in trust while still needing support and care.
