# Isaiah 11:6-9 - A World Without Hurt

Example answers are starting points, not the only right responses. Personal examples are hypothetical.

## Isaiah 11:6, 9

> 6. The wolf also shall dwell with the lamb, and the leopard shall lie down with the kid; and the calf and the young lion and the fatling together; and a little child shall lead them.
>
>
> 9. They shall not hurt nor destroy in all my holy mountain: for the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the Lord, as the waters cover the sea.

[Read Isaiah 11:6-9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/11?lang=eng&id=p6-p9#p6)

## Peace reaches the vulnerable

Isaiah describes a world where creatures that normally threaten one another dwell together and children are safe. Notice the relationships and the absence of harm. What stands out to you?

<details>
<summary>Show an example answer</summary>

The safety of the child stands out. The picture suggests more than powerful beings agreeing to a truce: the vulnerable no longer have to live in fear.

</details>

Verse 9 gives the vision's center: the earth is full of the knowledge of the Lord. The passage follows the description of righteous rule in verses 1-5. Peace is connected with who governs and with knowing Him.

### Peace in the Millennium

**Curriculum interpretation: Isaiah 11:6-9.** The teacher manual identifies these verses with the Millennium and the peace the Savior will bring to the world. It invites learners to consider how knowing the Lord will affect the earth.

["Isaiah 11-12: An Ensign for the Nations," Old Testament Seminary Teacher Manual, 2026](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/old-testament-seminary-manual-2026/37-isaiah-1-12/374-isaiah-11-12?lang=eng)

We do not need to understand every detail of the Millennium to find hope in the Savior's promise of peace.

## Live toward the promise

Our present efforts cannot produce the whole promised world. Still, Christ's promise gives direction to ordinary choices: refusing cruelty, protecting the vulnerable, repairing harm, and making our homes and congregations safer places.

Peace is not asking someone to remain in danger or pretend that mistreatment is acceptable. Protecting people from harm fits the vision of verse 9.

## A place where nobody has to be a target

Notice more than unusual animal pairings. See a picture of life without predation, fear, or people being treated as disposable. This personal application does not replace Isaiah's promise of the Savior's future peace.

Imagine a group chat where nobody becomes the target everyone bonds over mocking. Or a school friendship where being accepted does not depend on excluding someone else. Which habits would have to change for those places to become safer?

<details>
<summary>Show an example answer</summary>

People would need to stop forwarding humiliating screenshots, rewarding cruel jokes, and using exclusion to gain status. They could challenge harm safely and involve a trusted adult when needed.

</details>

Verse 9 links the absence of harm with knowledge of the Lord. Read verses 2-5 for the qualities of His rule, then ask: **if we are learning to know Him, what might someone experience differently in the way we treat them?** Knowing facts about Christ is not the same as allowing His character to shape our actions.

<details>
<summary>Show an example answer</summary>

They might experience fairer treatment, more patient listening, and less judgment based on appearance or popularity. Our knowledge would show up as care, not just correct answers.

</details>

Verse 12 adds the gathering of people described as outcasts. As we help others come to know Christ, what would an invitation look like that communicates belonging rather than pressure? Respecting someone's choice and protecting them from harm remain important.

<details>
<summary>Show an example answer</summary>

"You are welcome to come with me, and I'm happy to answer questions. We are still friends whether or not you come." The friendship would continue after a refusal.

</details>

## Questions to sit with

- Which detail of Isaiah's picture helps you feel the difference between this world and the world he describes?
- What connection does verse 9 make between peace and knowing the Lord?
- How can hope for future peace motivate action rather than passivity now?
- What is one way you could help make your home, school, or church a place where people are less likely to be hurt?

<details>
<summary>Show example answers</summary>

- **A striking detail:** Creatures normally in danger can rest beside those that would threaten them. Safety is ordinary rather than exceptional.
- **Knowing the Lord:** Verse 9 connects a world without harm with the earth being full of His knowledge. His righteous rule shapes its peace.
- **Hope and action:** A promise of future peace gives direction to present choices; we can practice fairness and protect others now.
- **One contribution:** Refuse to join ridicule, support someone being targeted, and seek help when harm is beyond what we can address safely.

</details>
