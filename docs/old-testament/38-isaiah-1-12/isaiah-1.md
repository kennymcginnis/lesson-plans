# Isaiah 1:16-18 - Scarlet to Snow

## Isaiah 1:16-18

> 16. Wash you, make you clean; put away the evil of your doings from before mine eyes; cease to do evil;
> 17. Learn to do well; seek judgment, relieve the oppressed, judge the fatherless, plead for the widow.
> 18. Come now, and let us reason together, saith the Lord: though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow; though they be red like crimson, they shall be as wool.

[Read Isaiah 1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/1?lang=eng&id=p16-p18#p16)

## A 25-minute teen lesson: Come now

**One principle:** You do not have to wait until you are better to turn to God. Through Jesus Christ, our sins need not define our future. Repentance includes changing how we treat people, not just feeling bad.

**Prepare:** Read Isaiah 1:11-20. Have a board and something students can use for a private written response. Optionally bring a red and a white piece of paper or cloth to show the color contrast; this is a visual, not a stain-removal demonstration. No video or handout is needed.

### 0-4 minutes - Start with a situation

Offer this hypothetical situation:

"Someone hasn't prayed for a while because they're embarrassed about choices they've made. They think, 'I'll start again once I'm doing better.' What makes that reaction understandable?"

Give students 20 seconds to think, then let them compare thoughts with a neighbor before taking a few responses. Discuss the hypothetical person, not anyone's personal sins. If the room is quiet, ask whether embarrassment, fear, or feeling unworthy might explain the hesitation.

### 4-8 minutes - Discover the invitation

Give a brief introduction:

"Isaiah often teaches through pictures. Here he is speaking to people whose worship doesn't match how they treat others. God takes that seriously. Listen for what He asks them to do."

Read Isaiah 1:16-18 together. Ask: **"Which words sound like an invitation? Which words ask for a change?"**

Write their discoveries on the board under "Invitation" and "Change." Let students point to words in the passage before you explain. Clarify that "judge the fatherless" means defending their cause, not condemning them.

### 8-14 minutes - Stay with one picture

Show the red and white objects, or write **scarlet -> white as snow**. Ask:

- Why describe forgiveness using such a dramatic change?
- What is the difference between "I did something wrong" and "This is all I'll ever be"?
- What does this promise give someone reason to hope for?

Follow what students notice rather than requiring an answer to every question. Draw the discussion together: **through Jesus Christ, cleansing is possible. Our mistakes need not determine who we become.**

Forgiveness does not mean the past never occurred or that consequences disappear. Repairing harm, rebuilding trust, and healing can take time. Continuing painful feelings are not proof that God has refused forgiveness. The picture works without claims about Hebrew word origins or ancient dye-making.

### 14-21 minutes - Return to the opening situation

"They decide to pray tonight. But they've also been joining in when friends ridicule someone. Looking at verses 16-17, what could turning toward God look like tomorrow?"

Let students suggest realistic actions. If needed, ask what the person could stop doing, what harm they could begin to repair, and who they could support. Keep the situation hypothetical and do not name classmates.

Ask: **"How is that different from trying to become good enough before asking God for help?"**

Keep both truths together: Christ offers cleansing, and repentance includes changing our treatment of people. Service is not payment for forgiveness. We can seek His help as we begin to change, not only after we have succeeded.

### 21-25 minutes - Leave with the invitation

Reread Isaiah 1:18. Invite a private response on paper or a phone:

**"What is one step toward God I can take now, instead of waiting?"**

Do not collect responses or require anyone to share. Allow a quiet minute, then invite an optional observation about the verse. Close with your own brief testimony of Jesus Christ's willingness and power to help us repent.

**If time runs short:** Take fewer reports during discussion; protect these final four minutes. The lesson is complete here. The material below is teacher preparation and optional follow-up, not more content to fit into the class.

## Cleansing changes how we live

Read verses 11-18 together. Isaiah speaks to people who still bring offerings and pray, yet neglect righteousness. The problem is not a lack of religious activity alone; their worship has become disconnected from their treatment of others.

Notice the verbs in verses 16-17: cease, learn, seek, relieve, judge, and plead. Here, judging the fatherless means defending their cause, not condemning them. Repentance includes turning toward people who need justice and care.

Then comes the invitation to reason together. The Lord does not say that a stained life is disposable. He offers cleansing. Verses 19-20 preserve the seriousness of our response: willingness and obedience matter.

### Gordon B. Hinckley - Stand True and Faithful

**Direct quotation: Isaiah 1:16, 18.** President Hinckley, President of the Church, speaks to young women about faithfulness and the hope available after sin. He teaches:

> "There is hope for you."

He then quotes Isaiah's invitation to wash and become clean and the promise of scarlet becoming white. His message is that a person's past need not determine the whole future. Repentance is part of a gospel intended to save and help people.

["Stand True and Faithful," April 1996](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/1996/04/stand-true-and-faithful?lang=eng)

### Boyd K. Packer - The Brilliant Morning of Forgiveness

**Direct quotation: Isaiah 1:18-19.** President Packer, then Acting President of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, connects the promise of cleansing with being willing and obedient. He teaches:

> "There is a way back."

His address does not describe repentance as effortless or promise immediate relief from every painful feeling. He encourages persistence, including when forgiving oneself is difficult. Do not use a person's continuing sorrow as proof that forgiveness has been withheld.

["The Brilliant Morning of Forgiveness," October 1995](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/1995/10/the-brilliant-morning-of-forgiveness?lang=eng)

### Sharon Eubank - Christ: The Light That Shines in Darkness

**Direct quotation and interpretation: Isaiah 1:18.** Sister Eubank, then First Counselor in the Relief Society General Presidency, describes scarlet dye as colorfast: repeated washing would not remove its color from wool. She uses that image to explain the miracle of Christ's cleansing grace.

> "Christ makes wool white."

This is her explanation of Isaiah's imagery, not a claim that human effort can accomplish the cleansing apart from Christ. Her message answers the fear that a mistake has made someone permanently beyond repair.

["Christ: The Light That Shines in Darkness," April 2019](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2019/04/42eubank?lang=eng)

## Questions to sit with

- What changes when we read the promise in verse 18 alongside the responsibilities in verse 17?
- How is repentance different from deciding that we are worthless?
- What might it look like to repair harm and rely on Christ at the same time?
- Which invitation in verses 16-17 could shape one private decision this week?

Keep personal repentance private; invite observations about the passage rather than confessions to the class.
