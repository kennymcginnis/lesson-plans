# Isaiah 2:2-5 - Come to the Lord's House

## Isaiah 2:3

> 3. And many people shall go and say, Come ye, and let us go up to the mountain of the Lord, to the house of the God of Jacob; and he will teach us of his ways, and we will walk in his paths: for out of Zion shall go forth the law, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem.

[Read Isaiah 2:2-5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/2?lang=eng&id=p2-p5#p2)

## Come, learn, and walk

Isaiah's image is not simply a beautiful building on a mountain. People from many nations invite one another to come, learn God's ways, and walk in His paths. The movement toward the Lord's house is meant to change life after worship.

Verse 4 extends the vision to peace among nations. Verse 5 makes the invitation immediate: walk in the light of the Lord. We can begin living toward that promised peace before the world fully reflects it.

### Gordon B. Hinckley - An Ensign to the Nations, a Light to the World

**Direct quotation and interpretation: Isaiah 2:2-3.** President Hinckley, President of the Church, quotes these verses and explains:

> "Ever since the Salt Lake Temple was dedicated, we have interpreted that scripture from Isaiah, repeated again in Micah (see Micah 4:1-2), as applying to this sacred house of the Lord."

He describes people from across the world coming to learn God's ways. He identifies an application to the Salt Lake Temple without claiming that this is the only meaning of Isaiah's prophecy.

["An Ensign to the Nations, a Light to the World," October 2003](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2003/10/an-ensign-to-the-nations-a-light-to-the-world?lang=eng)

## Bring the invitation home

Find the actions in verse 3. What is the relationship between being taught and walking differently?

Temple worship, preparing for temple covenants, family history, and treating others with covenant faithfulness are ways to respond. Our circumstances and access to a temple may differ, but the invitation to learn and walk in God's light includes each of us.

## Swords into plowshares

Read verse 4. A plowshare is the cutting part of a plow: metal once used to hurt people becomes a tool for growing food. Isaiah pictures more than a pause in fighting. Under the Lord's judgment, nations no longer learn war.

In the video's discussion, one speaker describes seeing a sculpture of this transformation at the United Nations. She draws a personal application from it: an ability we have used to hurt others can be redirected toward good. Isaiah's promise concerns peace among nations; applying the image to our own choices is one way to reflect on that promise.

Think about the difference between rejecting yourself and changing what you do:

- Quick wit can embarrass someone, or help someone feel included.
- A strong voice can dominate a group, or speak up for someone being mistreated.
- Determination can keep an argument going, or help you persist in repairing a relationship.

Harmful behavior needs to stop. But changing it does not require believing that everything about you is bad. What ability could you ask God to help you use differently?

## Drawn toward God's ways

Verse 2 pictures nations flowing toward the Lord's mountain. The video notices something surprising about the image: rivers normally flow downhill, while these people move toward a mountain. As a reflection on the poetry, it asks what could draw us beyond our usual habits.

Verse 3 gives us a place to start: people want to learn God's ways and walk in His paths. What about Jesus Christ makes you want to change, rather than merely feel that you have to?

Some conflicts in families or across the world are beyond our ability to resolve alone. Verse 4 places the hope for lasting peace in the Lord, not in our ability to fix everything. What is one peaceful choice within your reach while you trust Him with what is not?

## Questions to sit with

- What does the phrase "he will teach us" suggest about our attitude toward worship?
- How can an invitation to the Lord's house feel welcoming rather than pressuring?
- What would show that worship is changing the way we treat people?
- How might walking in God's light help us become peacemakers?
