# Isaiah 2:2-5 - Come to the Lord's House

## Isaiah 2:3

> 3. And many people shall go and say, Come ye, and let us go up to the mountain of the Lord, to the house of the God of Jacob; and he will teach us of his ways, and we will walk in his paths: for out of Zion shall go forth the law, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem.

[Read Isaiah 2:2-5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/2?lang=eng&id=p2-p5#p2)

## Come, learn, and walk

Isaiah's image is not simply a beautiful building on a mountain. People from many nations invite one another to come, learn God's ways, and walk in His paths. The movement toward the Lord's house is meant to change life after worship.

Verse 4 extends the vision to peace among nations. Verse 5 makes the invitation immediate: walk in the light of the Lord. We can begin living toward that promised peace before the world fully reflects it.

### Gordon B. Hinckley - An Ensign to the Nations, a Light to the World

**Direct quotation and interpretation: Isaiah 2:2-3.** President Hinckley, President of the Church, quotes these verses and explains:

> "Ever since the Salt Lake Temple was dedicated, we have interpreted that scripture from Isaiah, repeated again in Micah (see Micah 4:1-2), as applying to this sacred house of the Lord."

He describes people from across the world coming to learn God's ways. Preserve the scope of his statement: he identifies an application to the Salt Lake Temple; he does not say that this exhausts every meaning of Isaiah's prophecy.

["An Ensign to the Nations, a Light to the World," October 2003](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2003/10/an-ensign-to-the-nations-a-light-to-the-world?lang=eng)

## Bring the invitation home

Ask the class to find the actions in verse 3. What is the relationship between being taught and walking differently?

Temple worship, preparing for temple covenants, family history, and treating others with covenant faithfulness can be part of this discussion. Do not assume everyone has the same access to a temple or the same personal circumstances. The invitation to learn and walk in God's light can include everyone in the room.

## Questions to sit with

- What does the phrase "he will teach us" suggest about our attitude toward worship?
- How can an invitation to the Lord's house feel welcoming rather than pressuring?
- What would show that worship is changing the way we treat people?
- How might walking in God's light help us become peacemakers?
