# 1 Kings 18 - If the Lord Be God, Follow Him

## A question of allegiance

Ahab calls Elijah the one troubling Israel. Elijah answers that the trouble lies in forsaking the Lord's commandments and following Baal. The confrontation at Mount Carmel is about whom the people will worship, not simply whose voice is loudest.

[Read 1 Kings 18:17-21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-kgs/18?lang=eng&id=p17-p21#p17)

> 21. "How long halt ye between two opinions? if the Lord be God, follow him: but if Baal, then follow him."

1 Kings 18:21, KJV excerpt.

The verse ends with the people answering nothing. Elijah asks for more than an opinion about God. The invitation is to follow Him.

Here, wavering between two opinions concerns divided worship. It should not become a rebuke to every person who has an honest question. We can seek understanding and still choose to pray, keep covenants, and act on the light we have.

## Noise is not the same as an answer

Read verses 22-29 and 30-39. The prophets of Baal call repeatedly without receiving an answer. Elijah repairs the Lord's altar and prays that the people will know the Lord and turn their hearts back to Him.

> 37. "Hear me, O Lord, hear me, that this people may know that thou art the Lord God, and that thou hast turned their heart back again."

1 Kings 18:37, KJV.

[Read 1 Kings 18:30-39](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-kgs/18?lang=eng&id=p30-p39#p30)

The prayer's center is not Elijah's celebrity or vindication. It is the people's recognition of God and the turning of their hearts. When the fire falls, they acknowledge the Lord.

Elijah appears outnumbered, but a majority does not determine whether a claim is true. Equally, being unpopular is not proof that we are right. The question is whether we are listening to and following the Lord.

This chapter also contains mockery, self-injury, and the killing of Baal's prophets. These are elements of a difficult ancient account, not instructions to ridicule, harm, or coerce people of other faiths. Keep our present application centered on faithful worship and a changed heart.

## A cloud like a hand

After the public confrontation, Elijah prays and repeatedly sends his servant to look toward the sea. At first the report is that there is nothing. On the seventh visit, a small cloud appears.

> 44. "Behold, there ariseth a little cloud out of the sea, like a man's hand."

1 Kings 18:44, KJV excerpt.

[Read 1 Kings 18:41-46](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-kgs/18?lang=eng&id=p41-p46#p41)

Elijah tells Ahab to prepare to leave before the rain stops him. The small beginning is followed by great rain. Notice prayer, persistence, attentiveness, and action together.

The account is not a rule that seven prayers guarantee an answer or that every small event is a divine sign. It can help us consider whether we dismiss modest beginnings because they are not yet the full result we hoped for.

## Questions to sit with

- What is the difference between believing something about God and following Him?
- What competing loyalties can make our choices inconsistent with our convictions?
- How does Elijah's prayer in verse 37 clarify the purpose of the miracle?
- What can we learn from his willingness to keep praying and looking before the rain arrives?
