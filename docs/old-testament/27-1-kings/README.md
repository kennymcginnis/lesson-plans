# If the Lord Be God, Follow Him

Calendar reference: June 27-July 3, 2022 - 1 Kings 17-19

The main reading is 1 Kings 17-19, with 1 Kings 12:6-8 as background. The calendar reference identifies the matching reading week, not a confirmed teaching date.

> What does trusting the Lord look like when what we can see seems to tell a different story?

## Lesson notes

Elijah's story moves through hunger, provision, public confrontation, answered prayer, exhaustion, and a quiet encounter with God. Faith is present in all these settings, not only in the moments that look triumphant.

Begin with the contrast between leadership that serves and leadership that demands. Then consider the widow who acts on a promise, the people asked to choose whom they will worship, and the prophet who still needs food, rest, reassurance, and companionship.

The Lord's question is not only whether we recognize His power. It is whether we will follow Him, receive His care, and help others do the same.

## A 25-minute class

1. **Introduce the question, 3 minutes:** Read 1 Kings 18:21. Briefly introduce Elijah and the conflict over worship of the Lord and Baal. Ask what it means to follow the Lord beyond agreeing that He exists.
2. **Trust when resources are small, 8 minutes:** Read 1 Kings 17:8-16. Discuss what the widow and Elijah each had to trust. Emphasize the Lord's particular promise in this account rather than promising that faithful people never go hungry.
3. **Care for the weary, 9 minutes:** Read 1 Kings 19:4-8, then verses 11-12. Notice the food and rest before the renewed direction. Ask how we might offer practical help and make room to listen for the Lord.
4. **Choose one response, 5 minutes:** Return to 1 Kings 18:21 and briefly read Elisha's response in 19:19-21. Invite one private decision to trust, serve, listen, or follow. Close with your testimony of Jesus Christ.

The leadership discussion and the fuller account of Mount Carmel are available for additional study. Protect the closing five minutes rather than rushing through every page.

## Scriptures to emphasize

Prioritize 1 Kings 17:13-16; 18:21; and 19:4-12. For a fuller discussion, include 1 Kings 12:6-8; 17:17-24; 18:36-46; and 19:15-21.

## Reading the accounts carefully

These notes center on the scriptural accounts rather than additional conference quotations. Scripture excerpts use the King James Version, and links open the surrounding passages.

Distinguish what the text records from what we imagine a person might have felt. The widow's inner experience is not fully described, the small cloud is part of a particular answered prayer, and Elijah's discouragement is not evidence that his earlier faith was false. The accounts invite trust in God without giving us a formula for predicting every outcome.
