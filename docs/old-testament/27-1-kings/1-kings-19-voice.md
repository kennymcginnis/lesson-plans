# 1 Kings 19:9-21 - Listen and Follow

## The Lord lets Elijah speak

At Horeb, the Lord asks Elijah what he is doing there. Elijah describes his service, Israel's broken covenant, and the threat against his life. He believes he is the only faithful one left.

[Read 1 Kings 19:9-10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-kgs/19?lang=eng&id=p9-p10#p9)

Hear the loneliness in his answer before moving to the next instruction. God does not require Elijah to hide what is troubling him. After the quiet encounter, the question and Elijah's answer are repeated in verses 13-14.

## A still small voice

> "And after the earthquake a fire; but the Lord was not in the fire: and after the fire a still small voice."

1 Kings 19:12, KJV.

[Read 1 Kings 19:11-14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-kgs/19?lang=eng&id=p11-p14#p11)

Wind, earthquake, and fire precede the voice, but in this encounter the Lord is not in those dramatic events. Elijah recognizes something worth attending to in the quiet that follows.

Chapter 18 has just described God answering by fire. Read the two chapters together: the contrast does not mean God can only communicate quietly. It warns us against assuming that the most forceful or spectacular experience must always carry His message.

Our lives contain competing voices that reward urgency and demand attention. Prayer, scripture study, and a little unhurried space can help us listen. Quietness itself is not proof of revelation; test impressions against the Lord's teachings and seek wise counsel where needed.

## Elijah is not as alone as he feels

The Lord gives further direction, including the appointment of Elisha as prophet in Elijah's place. He also tells Elijah that seven thousand in Israel have not bowed to Baal.

[Read 1 Kings 19:15-18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-kgs/19?lang=eng&id=p15-p18#p15)

Elijah's feeling of isolation is real, but it is not the whole picture. There are faithful people he has not counted, and the work will continue beyond his individual efforts. We need not carry the Lord's whole work alone.

## Elisha responds

Elijah finds Elisha plowing and casts his mantle upon him. Elisha leaves the oxen, asks to say farewell to his parents, provides food for the people, and follows Elijah.

> "Then he arose, and went after Elijah, and ministered unto him."

1 Kings 19:21, KJV excerpt.

[Read 1 Kings 19:19-21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-kgs/19?lang=eng&id=p19-p21#p19)

The account is brief. It does not tell us that Elisha had no previous knowledge of Elijah or describe every consideration behind his decision. It does show a willingness to respond through concrete action and service.

The story brings us back to the invitation in 1 Kings 18:21: if the Lord is God, follow Him. Listening becomes discipleship when it shapes how we live. That does not require copying Elisha's exact change of occupation or abandoning responsibilities without discernment.

## Questions to sit with

- What helps you make room to listen without demanding a dramatic experience?
- How do chapters 18 and 19 together broaden our understanding of God's ways of communicating?
- What difference might it make to remember faithful people beyond those we can see?
- What is one act of service or obedience that could follow from direction you have already received?
