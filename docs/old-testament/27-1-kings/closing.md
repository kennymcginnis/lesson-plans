# Closing - Trust, Listen, and Follow

## Return to the invitation

> "If the Lord be God, follow him."

1 Kings 18:21, KJV excerpt.

[Read 1 Kings 18:21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-kgs/18?lang=eng&id=p21#p21)

These chapters do not give us just one picture of faith. They show a prophet receiving food, a widow acting on a promise, people confronted with divided loyalties, prayer continuing before rain appears, and an exhausted servant receiving care and renewed direction.

## Remember the movement

- **Serve:** Rehoboam's advisers invite him to lead by serving and speaking good words.
- **Trust:** Elijah and the widow depend on God's word when their visible resources seem insufficient.
- **Choose:** At Mount Carmel, the people are asked to move from divided allegiance to following the Lord.
- **Receive care:** Elijah needs food, rest, and help; faithful service does not remove human limits.
- **Listen:** The quiet voice at Horeb matters as much as the dramatic answer at Carmel.
- **Follow:** Elisha responds through a changed course and practical service.

The promise about the widow's meal and oil is a particular miracle, not a guarantee that our resources will never run low. The broader invitation is to trust the Lord's faithfulness, seek His direction, and care for one another while the way ahead is still uncertain.

## Questions to sit with

- Which part of these accounts speaks most directly to your life right now?
- Do you need to take a step, receive help, make room to listen, or offer care?
- What could following Jesus Christ look like in one ordinary choice this week?

Allow a quiet moment for a private response. There is no need to disclose a personal crisis or explain a difficult decision publicly.

## One response for the week

Choose one specific action: make a respectful offer of help, accept needed support, set aside time for prayer and scripture, or act on a clear invitation to serve. Keep it small enough to do and meaningful enough to remember.

Close with your own testimony of Jesus Christ. We can trust Him without pretending we already know how every difficulty will end.
