# Proverbs 4 - A Path That Grows Brighter

> 7. Wisdom is the principal thing; therefore get wisdom: and with all thy getting get understanding.
>
>
> 18. But the path of the just is as the shining light, that shineth more and more unto the perfect day.
>
> 
> 26. Ponder the path of thy feet, and let all thy ways be established.
> 27. Turn not to the right hand nor to the left: remove thy foot from evil.

[Read Proverbs 4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/4?lang=eng)

## Wisdom is something we seek and practice

Proverbs 1:7 begins with reverence for the Lord. Proverbs 2:1-6 invites us to seek understanding like hidden treasure. Chapter 4 brings that search into everyday choices: consider where your feet are taking you.

Verse 18 offers an image worth lingering over. The path grows brighter. We may have enough light for the next step before we have enough to see the destination.

### David A. Bednar - The Spirit of Revelation

**Thematic connection: Proverbs 4:18.** Elder Bednar, of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, compares revelation to both a light switched on suddenly and the gradual light of sunrise. He teaches:

> "Most frequently, revelation comes in small increments over time and is granted according to our desire, worthiness, and preparation."

This is a suggested pairing with the imagery of Proverbs 4:18, not a claim that this talk directly quotes the verse.

["The Spirit of Revelation," April 2011](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2011/04/the-spirit-of-revelation?lang=eng)

## Guard the heart that guides the path

> 23. Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life.

Proverbs 4:23, KJV.

[Read Proverbs 4:23-27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/4?lang=eng&id=p23-p27#p23)

The heart here concerns our inner life: what we desire, attend to, and choose. The surrounding verses connect that inner life with speech, sight, and the direction of our feet. A brighter path is not only about knowing where to go; it also concerns what we cultivate within us.

Guarding the heart need not mean closing it to people or trying to suppress every difficult feeling. It can mean nourishing what is truthful and good, reconsidering a habit that feeds resentment, and seeking help when we need it.

## Questions to sit with

- What is the difference between knowing many things and becoming wise?
- When have you recognized guidance only after looking back?
- Does expecting a sudden answer sometimes keep us from noticing gradual light?
- What would it look like to "ponder the path of thy feet" before making a decision?
- What are you giving repeated attention to, and how is it shaping your choices?
- What would help you guard your heart without becoming guarded toward other people?
