# Proverbs 3:5-7 - Trust When You Do Not Have All the Answers

## Proverbs 3:5-7

> 5. Trust in the Lord with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding.
> 6. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.
> 7. Be not wise in thine own eyes: fear the Lord, and depart from evil.

[Read Proverbs 3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/3?lang=eng&id=p5-p7#p5)

## Trust does not require having every answer

The promise is that the Lord will direct our paths. It does not say that we will see the whole path at once.

Acknowledging Him can include study, prayer, counsel, and a willingness to act on the direction we have already received. Verse 7 adds humility: our understanding is real, but it is not complete.

### Dallin H. Oaks - Trust in the Lord

**Direct quotation: Proverbs 3:5-6.** President Oaks, then First Counselor in the First Presidency, introduces these verses as:

> "this familiar Bible teaching, which has been most helpful to me on a multitude of unanswered questions"

His discussion concerns unanswered questions about the spirit world and relationships in the next life. He counsels against teaching speculation as official doctrine. Trust leaves room to admit what we do not know.

["Trust in the Lord," October 2019](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2019/10/17oaks?lang=eng)

### Paul B. Pieper - Trust in the Lord

**Direct quotation: Proverbs 3:5.** Elder Pieper, of the Seventy, teaches:

> "Sometimes the best way to learn to trust God is simply by trusting Him."

He describes following a prompting to relocate and then facing employment uncertainty and serious medical challenges in his family. Difficulties did not automatically mean the earlier guidance had been wrong.

["Trust in the Lord," April 2024](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2024/04/44pieper?lang=eng)

## Questions to sit with

- What does trusting God look like while an important question remains unanswered?
- How do we distinguish using our understanding from relying on it as our only source of guidance?
- When have you followed a prompting and still encountered difficulty?
- What might it mean to acknowledge Him in one ordinary decision this week?
