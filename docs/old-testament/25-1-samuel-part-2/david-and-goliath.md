# David and Goliath - The Battle Is the Lord's

Main reading: 1 Samuel 17.

## The contrast in the story

Read Goliath's challenge in 1 Samuel 17:8-9 alongside the description of his height, armor, and weapons in verses 4-7. Against that imposing picture stands David, the youngest of Jesse's sons, who tended his father's sheep.

The point is not simply that one person is large and another small. Saul and the Israelites see the challenge and are terrified; David speaks about the Lord who has already delivered him.

> 37. "The LORD...will deliver me."

1 Samuel 17:37, KJV.

> 45. "I come to thee in the name of the LORD of hosts."

1 Samuel 17:45, KJV.

> 47. "The battle is the LORD'S."

1 Samuel 17:47, KJV.

[Read 1 Samuel 17:32-47](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-sam/17?lang=eng&id=p32-p47#p32)

## Remember earlier help

In verses 34-36, David recalls protecting the flock from a lion and a bear. David does not enter this moment without a history. His memory of earlier deliverance informs his trust in the present.

In verse 46, David declares that the outcome will make God's reality known throughout the earth. His words direct attention beyond his own reputation to the God of Israel.

As a teaching application, distinguish dependence on the Lord from passivity. David still prepares and acts. Trust does not mean refusing help, ignoring danger, or assuming that every struggle will end exactly as we prefer.

## Who are you in this story?

> David declared, "The battle is the LORD'S." Imagine a person who faces serious challenges. What would change in their life if they truly believed that about their struggles?

Consider a second question:

> Who are you in this story?

Allow more than one answer. We may recognize ourselves in David, in those watching with fear, or in someone who needs another person's courage and help. How might recognizing our need for help deepen our trust in Jesus Christ?

## Questions to sit with

- What does David notice that the frightened Israelites seem to have forgotten?
- How can remembering previous help change the way we face a present difficulty?
- What is the difference between trusting God and predicting a particular outcome?
- Who might need our practical help while facing a struggle of their own?
