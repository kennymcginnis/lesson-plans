# David and Abigail - The Courage to Intercede

Main reading: 1 Samuel 25.

## An insult and a dangerous response

David's men ask Nabal for provisions after protecting his shepherds. Nabal dismisses the request and questions who David is. David responds by ordering his men to arm themselves. Abigail's intervention changes the course of what follows.

[Read 1 Samuel 25:7-13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-sam/25?lang=eng&id=p7-p13#p7)

The question is no longer whether David can defeat an enemy. It is whether he will allow anger to lead him into wrongdoing, and whether he will listen when someone interrupts that course.

## Abigail appeals to David's better future

> "Upon me alone, my lord, be the guilt."

1 Samuel 25:24, NRSVue.

In verses 28-31, Abigail appeals to the Lord's promises for David's future and asks him not to burden that future with needless bloodshed. She speaks with both humility and moral courage. Her intervention does not endorse his anger; it redirects him.

David responds:

> "Blessed be your good sense, and blessed be you, who have kept me today from bloodguilt!"

1 Samuel 25:33, NRSVue.

[Read 1 Samuel 25:23-35](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-sam/25?lang=eng&id=p23-p35#p23)

## A soft answer can still be courageous

Proverbs 15:1 offers a companion principle:

> "A soft answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger."

Proverbs 15:1, NKJV.

Abigail does not merely soothe David's feelings. She helps him see the consequences of what he is about to do. A gentle answer can include a clear moral appeal.

[Read Proverbs 15:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/prov/15?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)

## Intercession and Jesus Christ

Abigail's appeal invites us to consider intercession: mediating or pleading on another person's behalf, seeking reconciliation.

**Thematic scriptural connection:** Read Abigail's intervention alongside 1 Peter 2:24 and Hebrews 7:25, which describe Christ bearing our sins and interceding for us. These New Testament passages teach about Christ; they do not explicitly identify Abigail as a prophecy of Him.

> "[Christ] is able for all time to save those who approach God through him, since he always lives to make intercession for them."

Hebrews 7:25, NRSVue.

[Read 1 Peter 2:24](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-pet/2?lang=eng&id=p24#p24)

[Read Hebrews 7:25](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/7?lang=eng&id=p25#p25)

The comparison helps us appreciate intercession, but Christ's saving work is unique. Abigail's words are not an instruction that a person must assume guilt for another's wrongdoing or place themselves in danger to prevent it.

## Questions to sit with

> Abigail interceded on behalf of Nabal. What might it look like for a person to intercede today?

- What does Abigail help David remember about the person he can become?
- How can we receive a needed correction as a gift rather than an insult?
- When might interceding mean speaking up, seeking qualified help, or praying for someone?
- What does it mean to approach God knowing that Jesus Christ intercedes for us?
