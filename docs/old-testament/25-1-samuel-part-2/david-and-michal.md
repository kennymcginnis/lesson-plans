# David and Michal - Words That Wound or Heal

Main readings: 1 Samuel 18; 2 Samuel 3; 6. Companion reading: Alma 60-61.

## A relationship with a history

Begin with the people's celebration of David, Michal's love for him, and David's awareness of his low social position in 1 Samuel 18. Saul's conditions for the marriage entangle the relationship with his conflict against David. Later, 2 Samuel 3:13 shows David demanding Michal's return.

Read these passages as a difficult royal and family history, not as a model courtship to imitate. The later exchange between David and Michal does not occur without that background.

[Read 1 Samuel 18:6-29](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-sam/18?lang=eng&id=p6-p29#p6)

[Read 2 Samuel 3:13-16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/2-sam/3?lang=eng&id=p13-p16#p13)

## Celebration becomes conflict

> "David danced before the LORD with all his might."

2 Samuel 6:14, KJV.

In 2 Samuel 6:16-23, Michal watches David, expresses contempt, and receives his response. She attacks his dignity; his reply invokes the Lord's choice of him instead of her father's household. The exchange offers a place to consider how words can deepen an existing wound.

[Read 2 Samuel 6:14-23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/2-sam/6?lang=eng&id=p14-p23#p14)

Verse 23 records that Michal had no child. Do not turn that statement into a general explanation of infertility or a judgment about people without children. The text gives no basis for making such claims about someone in our class.

## Gordon B. Hinckley - Slow to Anger

**Verified quotation; thematic connection:** President Gordon B. Hinckley, then President of the Church, teaches in his October 2007 address "Slow to Anger":

> "So many of us make a great fuss of matters of small consequence. We are so easily offended. Happy is the man who can brush aside the offending remarks of another and go on his way."

["Slow to Anger," October 2007](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2007/10/slow-to-anger?lang=eng)

This teaching invites us to consider our response to offense. It is a thematic connection, not a direct citation of David and Michal in the address.

## Pahoran shows another response

Compare this exchange with Moroni's accusation and Pahoran's answer in Alma 60-61. Moroni warns that he will act against those withholding support from the army; Pahoran responds without matching his anger.

[Read Alma 60:30-35](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/60?lang=eng&id=p30-p35#p30)

Pahoran answers:

> "In your epistle you have censured me, but it mattereth not. I am not angry, but do rejoice in the greatness of your heart."

Alma 61:9.

[Read Alma 61:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/61?lang=eng&id=p9#p9)

Pahoran could have answered by defending his status or attacking Moroni's judgment. Instead, his recorded response makes room to see Moroni's concern even while correcting his misunderstanding.

## Practice a different sentence

Try these three steps:

1. Think of a recent conversation where you wish you had responded differently. What was said?
2. Think of one phrase or sentence you could have spoken instead.
3. Consider practical habits that could help you speak with kinder words.

Keep the first reflection private. Invite examples of a better response without asking people to identify or criticize a spouse or family member publicly. Kindness does not require accepting abuse or abandoning appropriate boundaries.

## A covenant relationship with Christ

Revelation 19:7 describes the marriage of the Lamb. This image invites us to consider covenant relationship with Christ alongside what we have learned about human relationships. Do not treat David's conduct as a complete picture of the Savior's character.

[Read Revelation 19:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/19?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)

## Questions to sit with

- What changes when we listen for the concern beneath another person's words?
- What can Pahoran teach us about correcting a misunderstanding without retaliating?
- What habit could help us pause before responding sharply?
