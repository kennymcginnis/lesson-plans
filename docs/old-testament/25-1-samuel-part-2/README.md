# The Battle Is the Lord's (David's Rise)

David's rise and relationships in 1 Samuel, with companion passages in 2 Samuel and the New Testament.

> Every heart has a throne. Who sits on yours? You, or the Son of David?

## Lesson notes

This lesson follows four relationships: David and Goliath, David and Michal, David and Abigail, and David and Jonathan. It closes with the promise to David's house and the New Testament witness of Jesus as the Son of David.

The central movement is from trusting the Lord in our struggles to giving Him first place in our lives. The comparisons with Jesus are devotional readings of these stories, not claims that every action or relationship in David's life is a direct prophecy of Christ.

## A 25-minute class

Keep all four relationship studies available, but choose depth over trying to cover every story in 25 minutes.

1. **Introduce the question, 3 minutes:** Name the four relationships and ask: who sits on the throne of our heart? Explain that David's successes and failures can both turn us toward Jesus Christ.
2. **Trust in the struggle, 7 minutes:** Read 1 Samuel 17:37, 45-47. Ask what would change if a person believed the battle is the Lord's. Invite one or two responses.
3. **Choose one relationship, 10 minutes:** Use Michal and Pahoran to discuss a kinder response, Abigail to discuss receiving correction and interceding, or Jonathan to discuss helping someone find strength in God. Read the central passage and use one main question rather than covering all three stories.
4. **Give Christ first place, 5 minutes:** Read Mark 10:47 and connect the plea for mercy with Jesus as the Son of David. Return to the throne question, allow a private reflection, and close with your testimony.

Keep the final five minutes even if an earlier discussion runs long. The other pages remain available for personal study.

## Scriptures to emphasize

Focus on 1 Samuel 17:37, 45-47; 25:24, 28-33; 2 Samuel 6:14; 7:16; Hebrews 7:25; and Philippians 2:7-8. Closing references include Mark 10:47 and Matthew 21:9.

## How the modern teachings connect

Gordon B. Hinckley's "Slow to Anger" offers a verified thematic teaching about responding to offense. It is not presented as a direct citation of David and Michal. Other quotation attributions that remain unverified are kept in the source notes rather than used as established authorities in the teaching pages.

Scripture quotations identify their translation. The linked Church scripture pages generally display KJV wording, which may differ from the modern translations quoted here.
