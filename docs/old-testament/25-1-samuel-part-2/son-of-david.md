# The Son of David - An Enduring Kingdom

Main readings: 2 Samuel 7:16; Matthew 1:1; Mark 10:47; Matthew 22:42; 21:9.

## The promise to David's house

> 16. "And thine house and thy kingdom shall be established for ever before thee: thy throne shall be established for ever."

2 Samuel 7:16, KJV.

[Read 2 Samuel 7:12-16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/2-sam/7?lang=eng&id=p12-p16#p12)

David's story includes an enduring promise associated with his house and kingdom. Read the promise in its immediate setting, including the reference to David's descendants, before considering the New Testament's witness of Jesus.

## Matthew introduces the Son of David

Matthew's Gospel opens with these words:

> 1. "The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham."

Matthew 1:1, KJV.

[Read Matthew 1:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/1?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)

Matthew presents Jesus within Israel's covenant history. The title connects Him with David, but the Gospel will show more than the return of an ordinary earthly king.

## A cry for mercy

> 47. "Jesus, thou Son of David, have mercy on me."

Mark 10:47, KJV.

[Read Mark 10:46-52](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/mark/10?lang=eng&id=p46-p52#p46)

In the account of Bartimaeus, the title is part of an appeal for mercy. It is not only a statement about genealogy. Consider what Bartimaeus trusts Jesus to do and how Jesus responds to him.

## Whose son is He?

Read Jesus' question in Matthew 22:42 and the answer identifying Christ as David's son. Continue through verse 46: Jesus presses His listeners beyond that answer by asking how David can also call Him Lord.

[Read Matthew 22:41-46](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/22?lang=eng&id=p41-p46#p41)

At Jesus' entry into Jerusalem, the crowd cries:

> 9. "Hosanna to the Son of David"

Matthew 21:9, KJV excerpt.

[Read Matthew 21:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/21?lang=eng&id=p9#p9)

The closing question makes this personal: who sits on the throne of our heart? Recognizing a title is one thing; allowing the Savior to govern our lives is another.

## Questions to sit with

- What does the title "Son of David" add to Matthew's introduction of Jesus?
- What can Bartimaeus teach us about asking the Savior for mercy?
- How does reading beyond Matthew 22:42 deepen the question of who Christ is?
- What is the difference between welcoming Christ with words and following Him in a difficult choice?
