# David and Jonathan - You Will Be First

Main readings: 1 Samuel 18:1-4; 20:30-33; 23:16-18.

## Friendship expressed through covenant

Read about Jonathan's love for David, their covenant, and Jonathan's gift of his robe, clothing, and weapons in 1 Samuel 18:1-4. These are actions, not just expressions of admiration.

> "Jonathan took off the robe he was wearing and gave it to David, along with his tunic, and even his sword..."

1 Samuel 18:4, NIV.

[Read 1 Samuel 18:1-4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-sam/18?lang=eng&id=p1-p4#p1)

Jonathan's gift can be read as a gesture of loyalty and willingness to yield status, but 1 Samuel 18 does not record a speech declaring that he surrenders all power. His explicit recognition of David's future kingship appears later in 1 Samuel 23:17.

## Loyalty that has a cost

In 1 Samuel 20:30-33, Saul is angry with Jonathan for siding with David. Saul sees David as a threat to Jonathan's future kingdom. Jonathan nevertheless asks what David has done to deserve death, and Saul turns his anger against his own son.

[Read 1 Samuel 20:30-33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-sam/20?lang=eng&id=p30-p33#p30)

Jonathan's friendship is not simply pleasant companionship. He speaks for David when doing so costs him something. We can consider that courage without making reckless exposure to danger a requirement of faithful friendship.

## Help someone find strength in God

> "Jonathan went to David...and helped him find strength in God."

1 Samuel 23:16, NIV.

In verses 17-18, Jonathan assures David that he will be king and that Jonathan will be second to him. They renew their covenant before the Lord.

[Read 1 Samuel 23:16-18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/1-sam/23?lang=eng&id=p16-p18#p16)

The story invites us to examine rivalry. Can another person's calling or success become something we support rather than something that diminishes us? Jonathan helps David find strength in God, not dependence on Jonathan alone.

## Christ's self-giving love

**Thematic scriptural connection:** Read Jonathan's giving alongside Philippians 2:7-8, where Christ humbles Himself, takes the form of a servant, and is obedient unto death. Consider the question: "Was this fair?"

[Read Philippians 2:5-8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/philip/2?lang=eng&id=p5-p8#p5)

The comparison highlights voluntary self-giving, not an exact equivalence between Jonathan and Jesus. Christ's Atonement is unique. His humility also invites a response in us: read verse 5 with verses 7-8.

Trust in Christ's redeeming work does not imply that present injustice does not matter or needs no remedy. His self-giving love invites us to care for those who are suffering.

## Give Christ first place

Return to Jonathan's willingness to be second in 1 Samuel 23:17. As a devotional application, consider the difference between admiring Jesus Christ and allowing Him to direct our lives. Are we willing to yield first place to Him?

In this final application, the question is not whether we should make another human being our absolute authority. It is whether Jesus Christ has first place in our choices.

## Questions to sit with

- What does Jonathan do that turns affection into covenant loyalty?
- Who has helped you find strength in God?
- How can we support someone else's success without treating life as a competition?
- What would change in one decision if we sincerely said to Christ, "You are first"?
