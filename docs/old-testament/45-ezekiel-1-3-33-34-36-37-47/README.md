## A 25-minute class

1. **The Lord meets Ezekiel, 6 minutes:** Read Ezekiel 1:1, 28; 2:1–3; 3:1–3. Ask where the Lord appears and what Ezekiel is asked to receive.
2. **Warning and mercy, 5 minutes:** Read Ezekiel 3:17; 33:10–11. Ask what a watchman does and what the Lord desires for people who turn.
3. **The Shepherd, 6 minutes:** Read Ezekiel 34:2–6, 11–16. Contrast self-serving shepherds with the Lord's own actions. Invite one practical example of caring without pressure.
4. **New life and gathering, 6 minutes:** Read Ezekiel 36:26–27; 37:11–14, 21–23. Ask what the Lord promises to change and gather.
5. **Healing water and a private response, 2 minutes:** Read Ezekiel 47:8–9, 12. Invite students to choose one next step.

**Where does Ezekiel see the Lord’s glory, and why does that matter?**

<details><summary>Show a sample answer</summary><p>Ezekiel sees the Lord’s glory while living among exiles in Babylon. The Lord has not stopped reaching His people because they are far from Jerusalem (Ezekiel 1:1, 28).</p></details>

**What can we learn from the Lord’s promise of a new heart?**

<details><summary>Show a sample answer</summary><p>The Lord promises cleansing, a heart of flesh, and His Spirit. Renewal is His gift and invites a willing response (Ezekiel 36:25–27).</p></details>

## Reading carefully

Ezekiel 37:11 identifies the dry bones as “the whole house of Israel” saying their hope is lost. The vision promises restoration and gathering; its imagery can also invite reflection on resurrection and personal hope. In 37:15–28, the joined sticks represent a divided people becoming one. The [study guide](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-old-testament-2026/45?lang=eng) also invites Latter-day Saint readers to consider the Bible and Book of Mormon as companion witnesses that help gather people to Christ. Keep those two interpretive levels distinct.

Ezekiel 8–11 and 40–43 help explain the larger story of the Lord's glory leaving and returning, but they are outside this week's assigned chapters. The class does not need to resolve every detail of Ezekiel's visions.
