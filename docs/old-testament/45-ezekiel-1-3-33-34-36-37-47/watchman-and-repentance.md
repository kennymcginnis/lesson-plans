# Watchman and Repentance (Ezekiel 3; 33)

The Lord appoints Ezekiel a watchman who hears a word and gives a warning. In chapter 33, the exiles ask how they can live when their sins weigh on them. The Lord's answer includes an invitation to turn, because He desires life rather than death.

[Read Ezekiel 3:17](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/3?lang=eng&id=p17#p17) and [33:10–11, 14–16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ezek/33?lang=eng&id=p10-p11,p14-p16#p10).

A watchman's warning should serve people's welfare. Avoid using this image to justify controlling another person's choices or treating every hardship as proof of sin. The passage gives responsibility both to the messenger and to the listener.

## Questions to sit with

**What is Ezekiel asked to do as a watchman?**

<details><summary>Show a sample answer</summary><p>He is to hear the word from the Lord and warn the people on the Lord’s behalf (Ezekiel 3:17).</p></details>

**What does the Lord say He desires for a person who has gone astray?**

<details><summary>Show a sample answer</summary><p>He calls the person to turn and live; He says He takes no pleasure in the death of the wicked (Ezekiel 33:11).</p></details>

**How could a warning be given with care rather than superiority?**

<details><summary>Show a sample answer</summary><p>A caring warning tells the truth, protects the person’s dignity, listens, and leaves room for repentance and agency.</p></details>
