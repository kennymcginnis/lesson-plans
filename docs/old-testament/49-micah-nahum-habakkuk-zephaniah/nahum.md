# Nahum 1 - A Stronghold in Trouble

## Judgment against oppressive power

Nahum's opening identifies Nineveh as the subject of his vision. This is not simply a warning about an unpleasant neighbor. Assyria's imperial power had brought fear, conquest, and oppression to surrounding peoples. Nahum 3:1 describes a city marked by blood, lies, and plunder.

[Read Nahum 1:1-8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/nahum/1?lang=eng&id=p1-p8#p1)

[Read Nahum 3:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/nahum/3?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)

The announcement that such power will not last can be heard as hope by people living under it. At the same time, the book's severe imagery should not become a script for personal revenge or hostility toward present-day nations. Read judgment alongside the Lord's patience and goodness in chapter 1.

## He knows those who trust Him

> 7. "The Lord is good, a strong hold in the day of trouble; and he knoweth them that trust in him."

Nahum 1:7, KJV.

Three ideas belong together: the Lord's character, His strength, and His knowledge of those who trust Him. A stronghold is a place of refuge. The verse gives us an image of protection and dependence, not a prediction that believers will never experience loss.

As a Christian application, consider the ways Jesus Christ sustains people through trouble: grace to endure, courage to act, wise counsel, and help through others. Seeking spiritual refuge is compatible with seeking physical safety and practical assistance.

## A broken yoke

> 13. "For now will I break his yoke from off thee, and will burst thy bonds in sunder."

Nahum 1:13, KJV.

[Read Nahum 1:12-15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/nahum/1?lang=eng&id=p12-p15#p12)

The yoke represents domination in the passage's setting. The promise is relief from oppressive power, not a celebration of one person's strength over another.

We can connect this image thematically with Christ's deliverance from sin and with hope for the end of persecution and injustice. Keep the distinction clear: the verse first speaks within Judah's historical circumstances; the Christian application looks toward the Savior's fuller work of redemption.

## Good news and peace

Nahum 1:15 pictures a messenger bringing good tidings and announcing peace. Judah is invited to keep its feasts and fulfill its vows. Relief is connected with renewed worship and covenant life.

Hope is not merely the disappearance of enemies. It includes the freedom to live faithfully. What would we do with the peace we pray for? What kind of community would we build?

## Questions to sit with

- How does knowing Nahum's setting change the way we hear the promise of refuge?
- Which part of verse 7 matters most to you today: goodness, strength, or being known?
- What is the difference between hoping for justice and cultivating revenge?
- How can we help another person experience safety or relief now?
