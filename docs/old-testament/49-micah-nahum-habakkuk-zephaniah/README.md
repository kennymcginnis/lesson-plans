# Yet I Will Rejoice in the Lord

Week 49 - Nahum; Habakkuk; Zephaniah

> How can we trust the Lord while injustice remains and the answers are not yet clear?

## Lesson notes

Nahum speaks against oppressive power and describes the Lord as a stronghold. Habakkuk brings questions about injustice to God and learns to live by faith while waiting. Zephaniah moves from judgment to a vision of restored people and the Lord rejoicing over them.

These books are short, but their voices are not interchangeable. Read their warnings and promises in context, then consider what they teach about God's character and our response to Him. The studies here focus on these three prophets; they do not provide a separate study of Micah.

## A 25-minute class

1. **Learn a way to read, 3 minutes:** Introduce Understand, Identify, Apply. Use Nahum 1:7 to distinguish the words of the passage from a personal application.
2. **Bring the hard question, 8 minutes:** Read Habakkuk 1:2-4, 2:4, and 3:17-19. Ask what changes in the prophet's response even though the closing prayer still describes loss.
3. **Find the promise, 9 minutes:** Use three or four pairs from the scripture-matching activity. Read each verse before discussing its promise. Include Zephaniah 3:17 and ask what it means that the Lord rejoices over His people.
4. **Choose a response, 5 minutes:** Return to Habakkuk 3:18. Invite class members to choose one promise and one way to live toward it this week. Close with your testimony of Jesus Christ.

The fuller historical discussion and the remaining activity pairs are available for personal study. Protect the final five minutes rather than trying to cover every passage.

## Scriptures to emphasize

Prioritize Nahum 1:7; Habakkuk 2:4; 3:17-19; and Zephaniah 3:17. Additional promises for the activity are Nahum 1:13, 15 and Zephaniah 3:9, 13-15, 19.

## Reading the promises carefully

The studies center on scripture rather than additional conference quotations. Scripture excerpts use the King James Version. Romans 1:17 explicitly quotes the teaching about living by faith; other connections to Christ and the future are identified as Christian applications rather than replacements for the original setting.

Hope in the Lord's final victory does not mean every hardship ends immediately or that we can explain why a particular person suffers. These promises invite faithful action and care for others, not indifference to present injustice.
