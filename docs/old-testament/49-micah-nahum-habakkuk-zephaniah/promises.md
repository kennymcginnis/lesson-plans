# Find the Promise - Scripture Matching

## Read, match, and explain

Work individually, in pairs, or as a class. Read a passage, match it with a promise below, and identify the words that support your answer. No additional materials are needed. For a short class, choose three or four passages rather than using all nine.

The promise descriptions are summaries, not direct quotations. After matching, distinguish what the verse says in its setting from the application you would make today.

## From matching to meaning

Do not stop at the right letter. Apply Understand, Identify, Apply to one pair. For example, Nahum 1:13 speaks of deliverance from an oppressor's yoke. A Christian application can look toward Christ's deliverance while also asking how we can relieve a burden now. It is not a promise that every difficulty will disappear tomorrow.

Similarly, Zephaniah 3:15's promise of seeing evil no more belongs to the restored situation it describes. It is not an instruction to ignore evidence of harm or deny the need for protection today.

## Questions to sit with

> Which of these promises is your favorite, and why?

- Which words in the verse help you understand God's character?
- Which promise speaks to a concern you can reflect on privately?
- How could our choices about speech, care, or fairness anticipate the community these verses describe?
- What is one action you could take without assuming the entire promise must be fulfilled immediately?
