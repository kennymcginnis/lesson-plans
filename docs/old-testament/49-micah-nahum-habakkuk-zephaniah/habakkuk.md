# Habakkuk 1-3 - Faith with Questions Still Open

## How long, O Lord?

Habakkuk begins with a complaint about violence, strife, and distorted justice. He has cried for help and cannot understand why the wrong continues.

> "O Lord, how long shall I cry, and thou wilt not hear!"

Habakkuk 1:2, KJV excerpt.

[Read Habakkuk 1:1-4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/hab/1?lang=eng&id=p1-p4#p1)

This is a prayer of lament, not a tidy answer about the purpose of suffering. The prophet speaks to God because he expects God's justice to matter. Honest questioning can be part of a relationship of faith.

## An answer that raises another question

In verses 5-11, the Lord announces the rise of the Chaldeans, or Babylonians. Verse 6 says, "I raise up the Chaldeans." The response is more challenging than simply saying that God is uninvolved and watches events unfold.

[Read Habakkuk 1:5-17](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/hab/1?lang=eng&id=p5-p17#p5)

Habakkuk's second complaint asks how the Holy One can allow the wicked to swallow people more righteous than themselves. The apparent instrument of judgment is itself oppressive. The book does not ask us to call Babylon's violence morally good; chapter 2 pronounces woes against plunder, exploitation, and bloodshed.

Keep the two complaints in order. The opening prayer concerns injustice around Habakkuk. His question about the more wicked power follows the announcement of the Chaldeans. Do not flatten both into a single speech or claim the book resolves every question about God's relationship to human evil.

## Watch, wait, and live faithfully

Habakkuk takes his place on the watchtower, ready to hear a response. The vision is for an appointed time. Waiting is then joined to a contrast between pride and faith:

> "The just shall live by his faith."

Habakkuk 2:4, KJV excerpt.

[Read Habakkuk 2:1-4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/hab/2?lang=eng&id=p1-p4#p1)

Faith here invites more than a prediction that events will turn out as we wish. Consider trust, loyalty, and a faithful way of life while the promised outcome is still unseen.

**Explicit New Testament quotation:** Romans 1:17 quotes, "The just shall live by faith," in Paul's discussion of the gospel and the righteousness of God. Read Paul's use alongside Habakkuk's setting rather than assuming the two passages address identical circumstances.

[Read Romans 1:16-17](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rom/1?lang=eng&id=p16-p17#p16)

The woes that follow in Habakkuk 2 insist that oppressive power is accountable. Verse 14 anticipates the earth filled with the knowledge of the Lord's glory; verse 20 calls the earth to silence before Him. Waiting does not mean injustice has ceased to matter.

[Read Habakkuk 2:6-20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/hab/2?lang=eng&id=p6-p20#p6)

## Yet I will rejoice

The final chapter is a prayer. Its closing verses name failed crops, empty fields, and missing flocks and herds. These are the loss of food and livelihood, not minor inconveniences.

> "Yet I will rejoice in the Lord, I will joy in the God of my salvation."

Habakkuk 3:18, KJV.

[Read Habakkuk 3:16-19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/hab/3?lang=eng&id=p16-p19#p16)

Notice what the ending does not say: it does not report that all the crops have recovered. The prophet's trust has changed without an account of every material problem being solved. Verse 19 names the Lord as his strength and pictures sure footing in difficult heights.

Do not demand that a grieving person sound cheerful to prove faith. Habakkuk's rejoicing grows within a prayer that also contains trembling and lament. Trust and sorrow can coexist.

## Questions to sit with

- What does Habakkuk's willingness to question God teach us about prayer?
- Why does the announcement of Babylon make his concern harder rather than easier?
- What could living by faith mean while an important answer remains incomplete?
- What changes when we read "yet I will rejoice" without first removing the losses in verse 17?
