# Mortality - Hope amid Opposition and Unfairness

Companion reading: 2 Nephi 2:11, 24-27; Moses 6:54-62.

## Can difficult conditions become a place of growth?

Some of the conditions that threaten to break us can become places where we receive help, develop compassion, and turn toward Christ. That possibility is not a claim that suffering automatically makes someone holy or that God approves of the harm people inflict.

Lehi teaches that opposition belongs to the setting in which agency and redemption have meaning. He also directs our attention to the wisdom of God:

> "But behold, all things have been done in the wisdom of him who knoweth all things."

2 Nephi 2:24.

[Read 2 Nephi 2:11, 24-27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/2?lang=eng&id=p11,p24-p27#p24)

Trust in that wisdom does not mean we can explain why a particular person became ill, lost someone, or was mistreated. We can acknowledge what we do not know and still seek the Lord's help.

## Brook P. Hales - Mortality Works!

**Verified teaching; thematic connection.** Elder Hales, of the Seventy, recounts a son's experience in the temple after his mother's death. He describes a message received through the Holy Ghost, not a vision or audible voice, conveying that she now understood her mortal experiences.

> "I want you to know that mortality works"

The account offers hope for a perspective beyond what we presently see. It is a reported spiritual experience, not a promise that every grieving person will receive a similar message or understand their loss immediately. Hales centers his testimony on the Savior's grace and Atonement making our eternal future possible.

["Mortality Works!," October 2024](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2024/10/43hales?lang=eng)

## Dale G. Renlund - Infuriating Unfairness

**Verified quotation; thematic connection.** Elder Renlund, of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, acknowledges that some unfairness cannot be explained. He identifies suffering connected with mortal bodies, unequal circumstances, and the harmful choices of others.

> "Jesus Christ both understands unfairness and has the power to provide a remedy."

He explains that the Savior voluntarily endured profound injustice because of His love for us and the Father. His testimony does not require us to call wrongdoing good. It invites us to trust the Redeemer who understands suffering and can ultimately make things right.

["Infuriating Unfairness," April 2021](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2021/04/25renlund?lang=eng)

## Hope should deepen our care

Faith in eventual redemption is not a reason to postpone helping someone now. Relief, protection, medical care, counseling, and practical support can be part of our response to suffering. We should not tell a person to remain in danger because it might teach them something.

Moses 6:54-62 connects moral agency and the experience of mortality with salvation through the Only Begotten. The center is Christ's redeeming work, not our ability to suffer without needing help.

[Read Moses 6:54-62](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/moses/6?lang=eng&id=p54-p62#p54)

## Questions to sit with

- How can we express trust in God without pretending to know the reason for someone else's pain?
- What does it mean that Christ understands unfairness rather than merely observes it?
- When has receiving help opened space for hope or growth?
- What is one way we can oppose unfairness or relieve a burden this week?
