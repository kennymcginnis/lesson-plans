# Walk with Me

Week 5 - Genesis 5; Moses 6

> What could change if we trusted the Lord's promise of help more than our first assessment of our limitations?

## Lesson notes

Enoch sees reasons he should not be the Lord's servant: he feels young, disliked, and slow of speech. The Lord answers with a command, a promise, and an invitation to walk with Him. Enoch then goes among the people and speaks.

The lesson begins with the larger questions of mortality, opposition, and unfairness. It then considers what seers help us see and how God works through people who know their own weaknesses. The answer is not that pain is unreal or that faithful people never feel inadequate. It is that Jesus Christ offers redemption and the Lord does not ask His servants to depend on themselves alone.

## A 25-minute class

1. **Name the question, 3 minutes:** Read Moses 6:31. Invite the class to notice Enoch's concerns without asking anyone to disclose personal struggles.
2. **Trust amid unfairness, 6 minutes:** Read 2 Nephi 2:24 and use the teaching from Dale G. Renlund about Christ understanding unfairness. Distinguish hope in redemption from claiming to know why a particular person suffers.
3. **Called despite weakness, 11 minutes:** Read Moses 6:32-37. Compare one other call account, such as Exodus 4:10-12 or Jeremiah 1:6-9. Ask what the Lord promises and what Enoch does next.
4. **Walk with Him, 5 minutes:** Read Moses 6:34 and briefly connect Enoch's message with being born again in verses 59-60. Invite one private next step of faith and close with your testimony of Jesus Christ.

The fuller discussion of seers and the study of family teaching and rebirth are available for preparation or follow-up. Protect the final five minutes rather than rushing through every comparison.

## Scriptures to emphasize

Prioritize Moses 6:31-37, 59-60 and Genesis 5:22-24. Companion readings include 2 Nephi 2:11, 24-27; Exodus 4:10-12; Judges 6:15-16; Jeremiah 1:6-9; and Isaiah 6:5-8.

## How the modern teachings connect

Modern teachings about mortality, unfairness, and prophetic perspective are paired thematically with these chapters. A related teaching is not automatically a direct citation of Enoch's call. Speaker roles and source details are identified on the topic pages, and any unresolved attribution is labeled rather than treated as verified.

Scripture excerpts use the King James Version and the Church's English editions of the Book of Mormon and Pearl of Great Price. Distinguish what the accounts explicitly say from devotional applications. Enoch's limitations do not make him worthless, and the Lord's promise is not a demand that every disability disappear before someone can serve.
