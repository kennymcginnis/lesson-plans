# Closing - Walk with Me

## Return to the invitation

> 34. "Thou shalt abide in me, and I in you; therefore walk with me."

Moses 6:34, excerpt.

[Read Moses 6:31-37](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/moses/6?lang=eng&id=p31-p37#p31)

Enoch's first description of himself is not the last word in his story. The Lord gives him a message, promises help, and invites him into an ongoing relationship. Enoch responds by going and speaking.

## Remember the movement

- **Mortality:** We can trust Christ's redeeming power without claiming to understand every hardship.
- **Perspective:** The Lord can help us see beyond our immediate view.
- **Weakness:** Recognizing a limitation does not make us worthless or unable to contribute.
- **Response:** We can prepare, accept help, and take the next faithful step.
- **Rebirth:** The purpose is not merely greater confidence; it is a life changed through Jesus Christ.

The message is not that we should never need rest, support, or accommodations. It is that we do not have to make our limitations the whole definition of who we are or what we can offer.

## Questions to sit with

- Which promise to Enoch would you like to remember this week?
- Is there a small act of service, a conversation, or a request for help you have been postponing?
- How could that step become an expression of walking with Christ rather than proving yourself?

Allow a private reflection. People do not need to name a disability, painful experience, or personal sin to participate.

## One response for the week

Choose one realistic action: prepare a few words of testimony, ask someone to help with a task, record a spiritual memory, or act on a clear invitation to serve. Let it be a next step, not a demand to solve your whole life at once.

Close with your own testimony of the Savior. His invitation is to walk with Him, not to become sufficient without Him.
