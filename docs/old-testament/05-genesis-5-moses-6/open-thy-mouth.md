# Moses 6:32-37 - Open Thy Mouth

## A command joined to a promise

> 32. "Open thy mouth, and it shall be filled, and I will give thee utterance, for all flesh is in my hands, and I will do as seemeth me good."

Moses 6:32, excerpt.

[Read Moses 6:32-34](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/moses/6?lang=eng&id=p32-p34#p32)

The Lord's answer directs Enoch outward. He is to call people to choose the God who made them. The message is larger than Enoch's personal confidence, yet the help is personal: the Lord's Spirit will be upon him.

> 34. "Thou shalt abide in me, and I in you; therefore walk with me."

Moses 6:34, excerpt.

The invitation is relational, not merely a technique for speaking well. Enoch is not sent away to prove that he can succeed independently. He is invited to walk with the One who calls him.

## Enoch goes

Verse 37 describes Enoch going among the people, standing on hills and high places, and crying with a loud voice. The person who had said he was slow of speech now bears witness publicly.

[Read Moses 6:35-39](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/moses/6?lang=eng&id=p35-p39#p35)

The text records action and utterance; it does not give a medical account of his speech or say that every difficulty has vanished. It also says people were offended. Faithful action is not measured only by how warmly others receive it.

At the same time, an offended audience is not proof that a speaker is right. Enoch's commission is established by the account, not by opposition alone. Our own speech still needs humility, care, and accountability.

## Begin with the next faithful act

For us, opening our mouths might mean sharing a brief testimony, apologizing, asking for help, or offering encouragement. It need not mean speaking at length or without preparation. We can study, listen, and choose a few honest words.

Where speech is difficult or inaccessible, writing, assistive communication, and quiet acts of service can also express faith. God's work is not reserved for polished public speakers.

## Questions to sit with

- What changes when we read "open thy mouth" alongside "walk with me"?
- Which actions in verses 35-37 show Enoch responding to the Lord?
- How can we distinguish dependence on God from neglecting preparation?
- What is one honest word or act of service you could offer without waiting to feel completely capable?
