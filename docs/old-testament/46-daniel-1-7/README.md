## A 25-minute class

1. **Resolve before pressure, 4 minutes:** Read Daniel 1:8, 12–16. Notice Daniel's conviction and respectful request.
2. **Seek the Lord together, 4 minutes:** Read Daniel 2:17–23, 44–45. Daniel asks his friends to pray, thanks God, and describes a kingdom that endures.
3. **Faith without a guaranteed rescue, 5 minutes:** Read Daniel 3:16–18, 24–25. Let “but if not” stand before discussing the outcome.
4. **Humility and prayer, 8 minutes:** Briefly compare Daniel 4:27, 34–37 with 5:22–23, then read Daniel 6:10, 21–23. Ask what repeated choices prepared Daniel.
5. **Christ's kingdom and response, 4 minutes:** Read Daniel 7:13–14. Invite a private choice for the week.

**What helps someone stay faithful before a moment of pressure arrives?**

<details><summary>Show a sample answer</summary><p>Daniel had resolved what mattered to him, asked for a respectful alternative, and kept praying as a regular practice (Daniel 1:8–13; 6:10).</p></details>

**What if the rescue we seek does not come in the way we hope?**

<details><summary>Show a sample answer</summary><p>The three friends say God can deliver them, but they will worship Him even if He does not rescue them from the furnace (Daniel 3:17–18). Their faith is not a promise that every faithful person avoids harm.</p></details>

## Reading carefully

The stories do not promise that every faithful person will escape danger. Daniel 3:18 explicitly leaves that outcome open. The fourth figure in Daniel 3:25 is described by Nebuchadnezzar; avoid presenting his description as a settled identification beyond the text. The stone in Daniel 2 points to a kingdom established by God; the [study guide](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-old-testament-2026/46?lang=eng) applies it to the latter-day Church. Daniel 7:13–14 points readers to the Son of Man and a kingdom that will not pass away. Chapters 8–12 are outside this week's assignment.
