# Jeremiah 1 - Called before Feeling Ready

## Known before the assignment

Jeremiah's call begins with the Lord's knowledge of him, not a list of qualifications he has assembled:

> 5. "Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee; and before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee, and I ordained thee a prophet unto the nations."

Jeremiah 1:5, KJV.

[Read Jeremiah 1:4-10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/1?lang=eng&id=p4-p10#p4)

The verse speaks specifically of Jeremiah's prophetic calling. As a devotional application, it invites us to consider the difference between being fully known by God and being fully confident in ourselves.

Jeremiah answers that he cannot speak and is a child. The Lord does not tell him to wait until every concern has disappeared. He gives direction, promises deliverance, and touches Jeremiah's mouth as a sign that His words are being entrusted to him.

## I am with thee

> 8. "Be not afraid of their faces: for I am with thee to deliver thee, saith the Lord."

Jeremiah 1:8, KJV.

The promise is the Lord's presence, not an easy audience. Jeremiah will speak among his own people, where familiar relationships and expectations can make a hard message especially difficult.

For us, a mission, a calling, or an ordinary opportunity to serve can expose real limits. God can help us grow through service, and that help can include preparation, mentoring, collaboration, and accommodations. Needing support does not make the contribution less faithful.

Do not assume that every person receives Jeremiah's specific prophetic authority or promise of protection. His experience can encourage trust without becoming a guarantee that our work will be free of danger or opposition.

## The almond branch

Jeremiah sees a rod or branch of an almond tree. The Lord interprets the vision by saying that He will hasten His word to perform it.

[Read Jeremiah 1:11-12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/1?lang=eng&id=p11-p12#p11)

The stated focus is the Lord carrying out His word. Jeremiah does not identify himself as the tree, and the passage does not compare the time needed to grow an almond tree with the time needed to grow an oak. Let the Lord's explanation govern our reading before adding a personal illustration.

## Courage without a promise of popularity

Verses 17-19 prepare Jeremiah to speak despite opposition from rulers, priests, and people. The images of a fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze walls express the strength the Lord gives for that assignment.

[Read Jeremiah 1:17-19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/1?lang=eng&id=p17-p19#p17)

The calling is not a test of whether Jeremiah can make everyone approve of him. Yet opposition by itself does not prove that any message is true. Our own words still need humility, care, and accountability to the Lord's teachings.

## Questions to sit with

- What does the Lord say before Jeremiah names his limitation?
- What help does Jeremiah receive besides an instruction to be brave?
- When has serving helped you develop something you did not have at the beginning?
- What preparation or support would help you take one faithful next step?
