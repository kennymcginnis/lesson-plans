# Jeremiah 3 - Return, Acknowledge, and Be Healed

## Is there still a way back?

Jeremiah 3 uses the painful image of an unfaithful marriage to describe covenant betrayal. Read the invitation to return within that setting. The wrongdoing is real, but the Lord does not make abandonment His final word.

> "Return, thou backsliding Israel, saith the Lord; and I will not cause mine anger to fall upon you: for I am merciful, saith the Lord, and I will not keep anger for ever."

Jeremiah 3:12, KJV excerpt.

[Read Jeremiah 3:12-15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/3?lang=eng&id=p12-p15#p12)

The verse names both anger and mercy. Its comfort is not that betrayal has no consequences or that God never judges. It is that He calls His people back and does not hold anger forever.

## Acknowledge the direction we have taken

Verse 13 asks the people to acknowledge their iniquity and disobedience. Returning involves honesty. We cannot change course while insisting that nothing needs to change.

That recognition is different from deciding that we are worthless. Repentance concerns turning toward God, making needed changes, and receiving His mercy. Remorse can move us toward repair; endless humiliation can keep us from believing return is possible.

Keep the focus on our own response rather than using the passage to pressure another person into a public confession.

## Gathered and fed

Verse 14 joins the call to turn with a promise of being brought to Zion. Verse 15 promises shepherds who will feed the people with knowledge and understanding. Restoration includes guidance and belonging, not merely cancellation of a penalty.

We can participate in that care through patient friendship, sound teaching, and invitations that respect agency. It is an application of the passage to imagine welcoming someone home; it is not a linguistic claim that every word for repentance and gathering means the same thing.

The marriage metaphor describes God's covenant with His people. It is not an instruction to remain in an unsafe human relationship or to ignore abuse because reconciliation is desirable.

## Come to the One who heals

> "Return, ye backsliding children, and I will heal your backslidings."

Jeremiah 3:22, KJV excerpt.

[Read Jeremiah 3:22-25](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/3?lang=eng&id=p22-p25#p22)

The response begins, "Behold, we come unto thee." The direction matters: toward the Lord whose mercy makes a new beginning possible. For Christian disciples, that hope rests in Jesus Christ, not in the belief that we can erase our history through effort alone.

## Questions to sit with

- How do mercy and acknowledgment belong together in verses 12-13?
- What is the difference between feeling sorrow for a wrong and giving up on ourselves?
- What kind of welcome helps a person return without excusing harm?
- What is one private change of direction you could begin this week?
