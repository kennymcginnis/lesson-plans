# Jeremiah 8; 11; 17 - Begin the Future Today

## A season has passed

> "The harvest is past, the summer is ended, and we are not saved."

Jeremiah 8:20, KJV.

[Read Jeremiah 8:18-22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/8?lang=eng&id=p18-p22#p18)

This is a lament over a people's unresolved distress, not simply a proverb about time management. Jeremiah grieves with them and asks whether there is healing available.

As a personal application, the passing season can prompt us to consider opportunities we keep postponing. A needed apology, an act of service, or a return to prayer will not happen merely because we imagine ourselves doing it someday.

Use the image to invite action, not to announce that a grieving or struggling person has missed their final chance. The surrounding message of Jeremiah continues to call people to return.

## My people, your God

Jeremiah 11 recalls the covenant associated with deliverance from Egypt:

> "Obey my voice, and do them, according to all which I command you: so shall ye be my people, and I will be your God."

Jeremiah 11:4, KJV excerpt.

[Read Jeremiah 11:1-5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/11?lang=eng&id=p1-p5#p1)

The invitation is relational and practical. Belonging to God includes listening and acting, not merely adopting a label. The person we hope to become is shaped through present choices, even while circumstances and limitations mean we cannot control everything about our future.

Choose a next step that is real and proportionate. Faithfulness need not begin with a sweeping promise to change every habit at once.

## What is being written on the heart?

Jeremiah 17:1 describes Judah's sin as engraved with an iron pen and a diamond point. The image communicates how deeply wrongdoing has marked the people and their worship.

[Read Jeremiah 17:1-8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/17?lang=eng&id=p1-p8#p1)

Later in the same chapter, a different image appears: someone who trusts the Lord is like a tree planted by water. Heat and drought are still present, but the roots reach a sustaining source.

> "Blessed is the man that trusteth in the Lord, and whose hope the Lord is."

Jeremiah 17:7, KJV.

Let the contrast raise a question about repeated choices. What are we reinforcing, and where are we rooted? Deep habits matter, but the image of engraved sin need not become our declaration that change is impossible.

## A prayer for healing

Jeremiah 17:13 returns to the image of the Lord as the fountain of living waters. Verse 14 answers with a prayer:

> "Heal me, O Lord, and I shall be healed; save me, and I shall be saved: for thou art my praise."

Jeremiah 17:14, KJV.

[Read Jeremiah 17:13-14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/17?lang=eng&id=p13-p14#p13)

Present action and dependence on God belong together. We act where we can, seek support where it is needed, and acknowledge that salvation is His gift. This prayer is not a timetable promising that every illness or difficulty ends immediately.

## Questions to sit with

- What is one good intention you have been waiting for an easier time to begin?
- How does covenant belonging differ from merely meeting a social expectation?
- Which habits help your roots reach toward the Lord when life is difficult?
- What small action could accompany your prayer for help this week?
