# Jeremiah 20 - Faith That Can Lament

## The calling has a cost

Jeremiah 20 begins with the prophet being struck and placed in stocks. After his release, the chapter records words of accusation, grief, resolve, praise, and deep anguish.

[Read Jeremiah 20:1-9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/20?lang=eng&id=p1-p9#p1)

Jeremiah's complaint in verse 7 is startling. The KJV uses "deceived" as he addresses the Lord and describes being mocked daily. Do not replace his words with a tidy statement that he never found his calling painful or confusing. This is a lament voiced from within his relationship with God, not a calm definition of God's character.

## A word like fire

Jeremiah considers no longer speaking in the Lord's name, but he describes the word within him as fire:

> 9. "His word was in mine heart as a burning fire shut up in my bones, and I was weary with forbearing, and I could not stay."

Jeremiah 20:9, KJV excerpt.

Read this alongside the words placed in his mouth in chapter 1. His conviction remains powerful even when the experience of serving is painful. That tension is part of the account, not evidence that one half of it must be false.

For us, a conviction can sustain a difficult responsibility. It does not mean we must refuse rest, ignore danger, or carry a burden without assistance.

## Praise and anguish in the same chapter

Verses 11-13 express trust in the Lord and praise for deliverance. Verses 14-18 then return to profound distress over life itself. The chapter does not end with every feeling neatly resolved.

[Read Jeremiah 20:10-18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/20?lang=eng&id=p10-p18#p10)

This gives us reason to listen carefully when a faithful person is hurting. A testimony and emotional distress can coexist. We do not need to diagnose Jeremiah from the chapter or ask class members to reveal similar experiences.

If someone expresses a wish to die or an intention to harm themselves, take it seriously and help them connect promptly with appropriate crisis or emergency support. A scripture discussion is not a substitute for that care.

## Questions to sit with

- How does chapter 20 deepen your understanding of the promise "I am with thee" in chapter 1?
- What can Jeremiah's honest prayer teach us about bringing painful feelings to God?
- Why is it important not to demand a cheerful ending from someone who is still struggling?
- How could we support a person whose service or circumstances have become difficult?
