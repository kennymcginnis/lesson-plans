# Jeremiah 18 - In the Potter's Hands

## Watch the work before explaining the image

The Lord sends Jeremiah to a potter's house. Jeremiah watches a vessel become marred in the potter's hand. The potter works the clay again into another vessel as seems good to him.

[Read Jeremiah 18:1-6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/18?lang=eng&id=p1-p6#p1)

> "Behold, as the clay is in the potter's hand, so are ye in mine hand, O house of Israel."

Jeremiah 18:6, KJV excerpt.

The Lord's first application concerns the house of Israel. Read the explanation that follows before turning the image into a personal lesson about our own lives.

## A future that responds to repentance

Verses 7-10 describe God's dealings with nations: a people can turn from evil, and the announced judgment can change; a people can also turn away from good and lose a promised benefit.

[Read Jeremiah 18:7-11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/18?lang=eng&id=p7-p11#p7)

The passage does not picture human beings as morally passive objects whose responses make no difference. Verse 11 explicitly calls each person to return and make their ways and actions good.

The KJV uses "evil" in this passage for the calamity or judgment announced against a nation. God's statement that He will "repent" describes a change in the announced course of action, not a confession that He has committed a moral wrong.

## Not finished does not mean discarded

As a devotional application, the potter's continued work can help us resist the assumption that a damaged past must be the whole future. A needed change may be substantial, but the image leaves room for renewed purpose.

Do not press the comparison beyond the text. It does not say that God causes every wound in order to improve us, that people should welcome abuse, or that all hardship has an explanation we can identify. Receiving His help can include leaving a harmful situation, seeking healing, and accepting the care of others.

Nor does change mean simply becoming whatever we personally prefer. The invitation is to respond to God's goodness and turn from wrongdoing. Christian disciples look to Jesus Christ for the forgiveness and transforming grace that make that response possible.

## One part of life available for change

Ask where you are willing to be taught. It might be a pattern of speech, a neglected responsibility, a mistaken assumption, or a relationship needing a more honest boundary or a sincere repair.

Willingness is more useful than announcing that we will never struggle again. Name one step and any help needed to take it.

## Questions to sit with

- What does the potter actually do when the vessel is marred?
- How do verses 7-11 prevent a reading in which our choices do not matter?
- What is the difference between receiving God's shaping and believing every harm comes from Him?
- Where could you make room for instruction, repentance, or healing now?
