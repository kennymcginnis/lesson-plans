# Jeremiah 2 - Return to the Living Water

## Remember who brought you here

The Lord asks what wrongdoing the people's ancestors found in Him that led them to go far away. Jeremiah recalls deliverance from Egypt, guidance through the wilderness, and the gift of a fruitful land. Leaders who should have helped people know the Lord have also turned aside.

[Read Jeremiah 2:5-9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/2?lang=eng&id=p5-p9#p5)

The passage describes a covenant relationship and the pain of unfaithfulness. It invites remembrance: who has sustained this people, and why are they now seeking life elsewhere?

When we feel abandoned, remembering past help can become a resource for prayer. It should not be used to dismiss someone's present loneliness or insist that they ought to feel God's nearness immediately.

## A fountain and a broken cistern

> 13. "For my people have committed two evils; they have forsaken me the fountain of living waters, and hewed them out cisterns, broken cisterns, that can hold no water."

Jeremiah 2:13, KJV.

[Read Jeremiah 2:11-13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/jer/2?lang=eng&id=p11-p13#p11)

A fountain supplies water. A cistern stores it, and a broken one cannot even do that reliably. The contrast is between the Lord, the source of life, and the inadequate substitutes His people have made.

The primary invitation is not to make the substitute impressive enough to replace the fountain. It is to return to the source. We can apply that question to approval, possessions, status, control, or any good thing asked to provide the security only God can give.

## Worship as returning, not checking a box

Why do we gather to worship? Social connection and habits have value, but worship invites us to seek the Lord, receive grace, renew covenants, and help one another follow Him.

We gather as people who need the Savior, not as people who have already made ourselves sufficient. Asking for help, repenting, praying, and receiving the sacrament can turn our attention from outward performance to a living relationship with Jesus Christ.

These are Christian applications of Jeremiah's image. The verse does not say that our souls are cisterns God must damage through suffering before He can fill them. God can meet us in painful experiences without our claiming to know that He caused each one for that purpose.

## A companion promise from Jesus

In John 4:13-14, Jesus speaks of water that becomes a well springing up into everlasting life. Read this as a thematic companion to Jeremiah: both direct attention toward God as the giver of life, though the Gospel passage does not explicitly cite Jeremiah 2:13.

[Read John 4:13-14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/4?lang=eng&id=p13-p14#p13)

## Questions to sit with

- What is the difference between a source of water and a container that depends on another source?
- What do we sometimes ask to sustain us that cannot carry that responsibility?
- How could worship become a more deliberate return to the Savior?
- What help could we offer someone who feels spiritually empty without blaming them for that feeling?
