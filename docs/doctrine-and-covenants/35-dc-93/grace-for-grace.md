# D&C 93:12-20 - Grace for Grace

## Notice the repeated words

> 13. "And he received not of the fulness at first, but continued from grace to grace, until he received a fulness."

D&C 93:13.

[Read D&C 93:11-20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/93?lang=eng&id=p11-p20#p11)

Verse 12 says "grace for grace"; verse 13 says "from grace to grace." Verse 20 extends the promise of receiving grace for grace to those who keep Christ's commandments. Notice both expressions before proposing an explanation.

The passage describes real growth in the Savior's mortal experience and the Father's fulness received by Him. It does not mean that Jesus had to repent of sin or that He was merely an ordinary teacher. The same section identifies Him as the Redeemer and Creator.

## Receive light and continue

D&C 50:24 connects receiving light, continuing in God, and receiving more light. Growth involves a continuing relationship, not simply accumulating information.

[Read D&C 50:24](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/50?lang=eng&id=p24#p24)

Obedience, righteousness, and sacrifice can be faithful responses to grace. They are not a price list through which we buy God's love or calculate our spiritual level. A rising line can illustrate growth, but lived discipleship includes learning, repentance, waiting, and help through difficulties.

## In due time

D&C 93:19 promises fulness "in due time." A companion revelation offers reassurance:

> 18. "Ye cannot bear all things now; nevertheless, be of good cheer, for I will lead you along."

D&C 78:18, excerpt.

[Read D&C 78:17-19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/78?lang=eng&id=p17-p19#p17)

Not bearing everything now is different from refusing the light we do have. We can accept limits and still respond. One honest step matters even when it does not feel like a dramatic spiritual advance.

## Questions to sit with

- What difference does "in due time" make to the way you hear the invitation to become like Christ?
- How does receiving grace differ from trying to prove that we need no help?
- What light have you already received that you could act on more consistently?
- What small practice would help you continue in God this week?
