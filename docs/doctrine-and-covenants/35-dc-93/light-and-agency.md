# D&C 93:21-39 - Identity, Truth, and Agency

## Find the definition

Before offering a definition of your own, find the word truth in section 93:

> "And truth is knowledge of things as they are, and as they were, and as they are to come;"

D&C 93:24.

[Read D&C 93:21-32](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/93?lang=eng&id=p21-p32#p21)

Truth is not whatever we would prefer to be real. Yet our understanding can remain incomplete. Seeking truth involves humility, honest questions, reliable learning, and willingness to correct an error.

Verse 36 defines God's glory as intelligence, or light and truth. This is not an IQ score or a ranking of people by education. The section joins knowing with receiving and living according to light.

## We were in the beginning with God

Verses 23 and 29 speak of our existence with God in the beginning and of intelligence not being created or made. Verse 38 affirms the innocence of spirits in the beginning and of children in their infant state through redemption from the Fall.

[Read D&C 93:29-38](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/93?lang=eng&id=p29-p38#p29)

These teachings give dignity to human life. They do not provide a detailed account of every stage of premortal existence or authorize us to explain someone's present circumstances by a supposed spiritual rank before birth.

If the question arises whether spirits differed, Abraham 3:18-23 describes differences of intelligence and calls some noble and great. It does not identify which present-day people occupied which position or explain disability, poverty, ancestry, or other conditions as evidence of premortal worthiness.

[Read Abraham 3:18-23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/abr/3?lang=eng&id=p18-p23#p18)

## Embodiment and joy

Verses 33-34 teach that spirit and element inseparably connected receive a fulness of joy. The body is not disposable packaging for the spiritual life. This teaching provides a foundation for the hope of resurrection discussed in D&C 138.

## Can light be refused or lost?

Verses 31-32 connect accountability with refusing light made manifest. Verse 39 speaks of light and truth being taken away through disobedience and inherited traditions. The warning concerns our response to truth, not a diagnosis of everyone who feels uncertain or spiritually tired.

Honest inquiry, grief, illness, and emotional numbness are not proof that a person has rejected God. We also cannot assume all traditions are bad: the question is whether a particular inherited practice helps us receive truth or keeps us from it.

D&C 88:40 pairs intelligence with intelligence, truth with truth, and mercy with compassion. Read it as a companion invitation to cultivate what is good, not a reason to withdraw kindness from those who think differently.

[Read D&C 88:40](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/88?lang=eng&id=p40#p40)

## Questions to sit with

- How does verse 24 challenge the habit of treating preference as truth?
- What does eternal identity invite us to respect in ourselves and others?
- What is the difference between an unanswered question and deliberately refusing something we know is right?
- What tradition could you examine thoughtfully, keeping what is good and changing what harms?
