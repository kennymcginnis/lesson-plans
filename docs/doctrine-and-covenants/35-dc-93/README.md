# Grow in Light and Truth

Week 35 - Doctrine and Covenants 93

> How does knowing whom we worship change the way we live?

## Lesson notes

Section 93 begins with Jesus Christ's invitation to come unto Him. The testimony of John describes the Savior receiving the Father's fulness, and the Lord explains why He gives this knowledge: that we may understand how and what we worship and come to the Father in His name.

The lesson follows that movement into growth through grace, our eternal identity and agency, and the responsibility to receive and teach light and truth. It closes with practical correction about life at home. Knowing more about God should change more than what we can explain.

## A 25-minute class

1. **Define truth, 3 minutes:** Invite everyone to find the definition in D&C 93:24, then read verse 19 to identify the purpose of the revelation.
2. **Whom we worship, 7 minutes:** Divide D&C 93:1-18 into the four reading groups on the worship page. Ask each group to share one thing it learns about Jesus Christ.
3. **Grace and a willing response, 9 minutes:** Read D&C 93:12-13, 19-20 and 50:24. Discuss how following the Savior differs from trying to earn His love or expecting instant perfection.
4. **Receive and share light, 6 minutes:** Read D&C 93:39-40 and 50. Choose one practical response in personal or family life, reflect privately, and close with your testimony.

The fuller discussion of eternal identity and agency is available for preparation or follow-up. Use one main question per segment to leave room for class responses.

## Scriptures to emphasize

Prioritize D&C 93:1, 12-13, 19-20, 24, 36, 39-40. Companion readings include D&C 50:24; 78:18; 88:40; and 3 Nephi 27:27.

## How the modern teachings connect

Distinguish the revelation's words from a teaching illustration. Growth through grace is not a numerical exchange rate between obedience and spiritual knowledge. Eternal identity is not a basis for ranking people's worth or explaining present disadvantage as a result of supposed premortal failure.

The pages cite scripture directly. Any modern quotation whose original wording has not been independently checked is labeled accordingly, rather than used as proof of an interpretation.
