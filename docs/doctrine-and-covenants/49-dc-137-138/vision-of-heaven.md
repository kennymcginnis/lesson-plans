# D&C 137 - Known by Works and Desires

## A vision and a surprise

Joseph Smith received this vision in the Kirtland Temple on January 21, 1836. He describes the celestial kingdom and sees Adam, Abraham, his parents, and his brother Alvin.

[Read D&C 137](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/137?lang=eng)

His parents were still living at the time. The vision is therefore not simply a report that everyone he sees has already died and occupies a completed final state.

Alvin had died before the restoration of the Church and had not been baptized for the remission of sins. Joseph marvels at his inheritance in the kingdom. The question joins love for a particular brother with a concern about God's plan for all who lacked the opportunity to receive the gospel.

## Opportunity and the heart

Verses 7-8 address people who died without knowledge of the gospel and would have received it wholeheartedly. The Lord knows more than the opportunities visible in a person's earthly biography.

> "For I, the Lord, will judge all men according to their works, according to the desire of their hearts."

D&C 137:9.

Works and desires are both named. The promise is not an excuse to dismiss the importance of action; nor does it reduce judgment to a checklist that ignores what a person genuinely could know and do.

This gives us reason for humility about people of other faiths or no faith. We do not know another person's full heart, circumstances, or response to opportunities still to come. God does.

## A promise concerning children

> "All children who die before they arrive at the years of accountability are saved in the celestial kingdom of heaven."

D&C 137:10, excerpt.

Let this assurance stand clearly. It is not a conditional promise requiring a bereaved parent to perform perfectly or secure an ordinance that the child missed.

The promise does not make a child's death desirable or remove the family's grief. Do not speculate about why the death occurred or tell a parent that knowledge of the plan should make sorrow disappear. Listening and practical care still matter.

## Read with the later revelation

D&C 138 explains the preaching of the gospel among the dead and their response through repentance and ordinances. The Lord's knowledge of desire does not make those teachings irrelevant. The sections complement one another rather than canceling the need for gospel opportunity.

[Read D&C 138:30-34, 58-59](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/138?lang=eng&id=p30-p34,p58-p59#p30)

## Questions to sit with

- What does Joseph's question help us understand about his concern for Alvin?
- How does judging works and desires differ from judging only visible circumstances?
- How should this teaching affect the way we speak of people outside our faith?
- How can we offer the promise about children without trying to explain away grief?
