# D&C 138:1-11 - Pondering and Revelation

## Another prophet, another time

Joseph F. Smith's vision was received on October 3, 1918, in Salt Lake City. This is Joseph F. Smith, not Joseph Smith, the recipient of section 137. His life included repeated bereavement, and 1918 brought further family losses amid war and a pandemic.

The historical setting can help us appreciate the questions he brought to scripture. It does not establish that loss automatically produces revelation or that grief is given to people so that they will have extraordinary experiences.

## What was he pondering?

The account begins with scripture and reflection on Christ's Atonement and the Father's and Son's love. His attention turns to Peter's words about preaching to the spirits in prison and the gospel being preached to the dead.

Verse 11 describes his understanding opening as he ponders. This is more than racing through a reading assignment. He considers the meaning of what he reads in relation to the Savior's saving work.

## D. Todd Christofferson - When Thou Art Converted

**Verified teaching; thematic connection.** Elder Christofferson, then of the Presidency of the Seventy, encourages study that includes rereading, pondering, praying, asking questions, and recording insights. He teaches:

> "For conversion you should care more about the amount of time you spend in the scriptures than about the amount you read in that time."

["When Thou Art Converted," April 2004](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2004/04/when-thou-art-converted?lang=eng)

This is not a quota for producing a vision. It is an invitation to give God's word room to work in us. Insight may come quietly, gradually, and with further study and counsel.

## Practice with a few verses

Read D&C 138:1-4 slowly. Notice what Joseph F. Smith is considering. Ask what the passage teaches about the Father's and Son's love, then write a question or response in your own words.

Personal impressions can guide our own discipleship without becoming new doctrine for everyone else. These recorded revelations came through prophets and were received as scripture; our private thoughts should not automatically be given the same status.

## Questions to sit with

- What helps you ponder rather than simply finish a passage?
- How does focusing on Christ's Atonement shape the questions in this section?
- Have you noticed a scripture opening a question beyond the one you began with?
- What small change would give your next study time more room for reflection?
