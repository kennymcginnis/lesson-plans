# Redemption through Jesus Christ

Week 49 - Doctrine and Covenants 137-138

> What do these visions teach us about God's knowledge, mercy, and care for people we cannot reach ourselves?

## Lesson notes

Joseph Smith's 1836 vision of the celestial kingdom raises a deeply personal question about his brother Alvin. Joseph F. Smith's 1918 vision explains Christ's ministry among the dead and the messengers He sends to those in darkness. Read the two revelations together: one speaks of God's judgment of works and desires; the other describes the continuing opportunity to receive the gospel through the Savior's redeeming work.

The visions were received more than eighty years apart. Their sequence invites thoughtful study of continuing revelation, not speculation that we know why God reveals each truth at a particular moment. Neither revelation makes the dead beyond His reach.

## A 25-minute class

1. **A personal question, 4 minutes:** Read D&C 137:5-9. Ask why Alvin's presence in the vision surprised Joseph and what the Lord's answer reveals about judgment.
2. **Mercy and hope, 6 minutes:** Read D&C 137:10 and 138:14-17. Discuss hope for children and the importance of resurrection without asking anyone to disclose a loss.
3. **Christ directs the work, 9 minutes:** Read D&C 138:1-11, 29-34. Notice the role of pondering and the messengers appointed by Christ.
4. **Our response, 6 minutes:** Read D&C 138:57-59. Connect the teaching with temple and family history service, invite one realistic action, and close with testimony.

Allow room for silence. When grief is present, a brief discussion and thoughtful care can be more appropriate than covering every question.

## Scriptures to emphasize

Prioritize D&C 137:7-10 and 138:1-11, 14-17, 29-34, 57-59. Companion readings include 1 Peter 3:18-20; 4:6; and D&C 93:33-34.

## How the modern teachings connect

The scriptural visions are the foundation. A verified teaching from D. Todd Christofferson about pondering is a thematic companion, not a direct quotation of these sections. His role at the time of the address is identified on the study page.

Read promises about mercy alongside the passages about repentance, ordinances, agency, and judgment. Do not infer that a temple ordinance forces someone to accept the gospel, that all outcomes are identical, or that we know the present state of a particular deceased person.
