# D&C 138:29-60 - Christ Sends Messengers

## The Redeemer directs the work

Joseph F. Smith wonders how the Savior's ministry could reach the vast world of spirits in the brief time between His death and Resurrection. The vision explains that Christ organizes and commissions messengers.

> 30. "From among the righteous, he organized his forces and appointed messengers, clothed with power and authority, and commissioned them to go forth and carry the light of the gospel to them that were in darkness."

D&C 138:30, excerpt.

[Read D&C 138:25-36](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/138?lang=eng&id=p25-p36#p25)

Verse 29 says He did not personally go among the wicked and disobedient to teach them. The account explains how He reached them through appointed servants; it does not explain every possible reason for this arrangement. Delegating the work does not mean withdrawing His concern.

## What is taught?

Verses 31-34 include repentance, faith in God, vicarious baptism, the gift of the Holy Ghost, and the other necessary principles of the gospel. Both those who lacked knowledge and those who rejected the prophets are included among those who hear.

The invitation is real, and so is the response. The passage does not say that everyone accepts or that ordinances performed by the living compel acceptance. Christ's sacrifice is the source of redemption, not the independent power of either mortal or spiritual messengers.

## Work continues beyond death

Verses 57-59 describe faithful servants continuing to preach after mortality and the redemption of those who repent through obedience to the ordinances of God's house.

[Read D&C 138:57-60](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/138?lang=eng&id=p57-p60#p57)

The vision also names prophets and people associated with the Restoration and speaks of preparation before birth in verses 38-56. This affirms continuity in God's work; it does not reveal every assignment in the spirit world or give us a way to rank living people's premortal worth.

The payment of penalties mentioned in verse 59 must be read with the section's repeated witness that redemption comes through the Son's sacrifice. It does not describe a person independently purchasing salvation without Christ.

## Temple work becomes personal service

Family history can turn an abstract discussion into attention to real people: their names, relationships, stories, and opportunities. Authorized temple ordinances are offered on their behalf in the hope that they may receive the gospel's blessings.

Participation can include research, preserving memories, helping another person prepare names appropriately, and temple service when available. Follow current Church policies for submitting names and obtaining required permissions. Do not treat completion of an ordinance as evidence that we know the deceased person's response.

## Questions to sit with

- How does the organization of messengers answer the question about reaching so many people?
- What does this reveal about Christ's concern for people beyond our reach?
- Why is agency important when discussing ordinances for the dead?
- What is one appropriate, manageable way you could contribute to family history or temple service?
