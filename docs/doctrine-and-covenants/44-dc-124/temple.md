# D&C 124:26-55 - A House for Ordinances

## More than completing a building

Verses 26-28 call for materials and the building of a house for the Most High. The stated purpose includes restoring what had been lost and receiving priesthood blessings. The building matters because of the Lord's purposes for His people.

[Read D&C 124:26-34](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/124?lang=eng&id=p26-p34#p26)

The revelation specifically addresses a baptismal font for baptisms on behalf of the dead. It allows an interval during the Saints' poverty and construction, then directs that the ordinance belongs in the Lord's house. This is an instruction about authorized ordinances, not permission to perform them wherever or however we prefer.

## Revelation and redemption

> "Let this house be built unto my name, that I may reveal mine ordinances therein unto my people."

D&C 124:40, excerpt.

[Read D&C 124:39-41](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/124?lang=eng&id=p39-p41#p39)

The temple connects the Saints' effort with blessings that come from God. We can discuss the publicly taught purposes of worship, covenants, and service for the dead without seeking to describe sacred ceremonial details.

Temple and family history service express concern for people beyond our immediate lifetime. These acts are offered through the Savior's saving work; they do not make us the source of another person's salvation or remove that person's agency.

## When diligent work is obstructed

Verse 49 describes people who labor with all their might but are prevented by enemies from completing a commanded work. The Lord says He accepts their offerings. The surrounding verses recall the Saints' difficulties in Missouri.

[Read D&C 124:49-55](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/124?lang=eng&id=p49-p55#p49)

The distinction is between failing to respond and having a faithful response obstructed. It is not a blanket exemption from every hard responsibility, nor does it allow us to judge another person's effort solely by the visible outcome.

The Lord can know both willingness and circumstances. For ourselves, ask what we can responsibly do and what help is available. For others, avoid assuming that an unfinished task proves indifference.

## Questions to sit with

- What purposes for the temple does the revelation name?
- How does serving for the dead enlarge the way we think about community?
- Why does verse 49 matter when we assess faithful effort?
- What is one realistic way to prepare for temple worship or contribute to family history work?
