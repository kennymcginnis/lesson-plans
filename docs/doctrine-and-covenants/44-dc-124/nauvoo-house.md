# D&C 124:22-24, 60-61 - A Resting Place for the Weary

## A house for travelers

> 23. "That the weary traveler may find health and safety while he shall contemplate the word of the Lord."

D&C 124:23, excerpt.

[Read D&C 124:22-24](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/124?lang=eng&id=p22-p24#p22)

This instruction concerns a boardinghouse. Verse 60 names it the Nauvoo House and again calls it a resting place for the weary traveler. It is distinct from the temple described elsewhere in the section.

[Read D&C 124:60-61](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/124?lang=eng&id=p60-p61#p60)

Notice how bodily and spiritual needs belong together. Health, safety, rest, and opportunity to consider the Lord's word are not presented as competing priorities.

## Hospitality as part of Zion

A person arriving from afar may first need a trustworthy welcome. What would the quality of that welcome communicate about the community's faith?

We are not all asked to operate a guesthouse, but the principle can shape a home, congregation, class, or act of service. Clear information, an accessible space, a meal, or patient conversation can make room for another person.

Do not make hospitality conditional on agreement with our beliefs. The opportunity to contemplate the gospel is an invitation, not a debt someone owes us in return for care.

## Stewardship behind a welcome

The section also gives financial and management instructions for this house. Practical planning matters: good intentions alone do not provide safe lodging or responsible care. Read those arrangements historically rather than treating them as a universal investment plan.

Boundaries, safeguarding, and realistic resources are part of responsible hospitality. Creating a place of welcome should not place a vulnerable person or host at avoidable risk.

## Questions to sit with

- Which words in verse 23 describe what a weary person might most need?
- How can practical care create space for reflection without pressuring someone?
- What barrier might make a newcomer feel unwelcome in our community?
- What could we change to make our welcome more trustworthy?
