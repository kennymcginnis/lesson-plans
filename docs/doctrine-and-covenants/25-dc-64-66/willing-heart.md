# D&C 64:20-25, 34 - The Heart and a Willing Mind

## Different instructions, shared devotion

D&C 64:20 instructs Isaac Morley to sell his farm. Verse 21 says Frederick G. Williams should retain his farm for the Lord's purposes in Kirtland. These neighboring instructions show why we should not assume that one person's particular sacrifice is the required pattern for everyone.

[Read D&C 64:20-25](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/64?lang=eng&id=p20-p25#p20)

The shared concern is a heart responsive to God. The revelation asks for open hearts, willing labor, and the use of resources in the work of Zion.

> "Behold, the Lord requireth the heart and a willing mind."

D&C 64:34, excerpt.

[Read D&C 64:33-34](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/64?lang=eng&id=p33-p34#p33)

## Neal A. Maxwell - Swallowed Up in the Will of the Father

**Verified quotation; thematic connection.** Elder Maxwell, of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, teaches:

> "The submission of one's will is really the only uniquely personal thing we have to place on God's altar."

He explains that the other things we give are things God has already given or loaned to us. The emphasis is not merely on surrendering an object, but on willingly yielding ourselves to God.

["Swallowed Up in the Will of the Father," October 1995](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/1995/10/swallowed-up-in-the-will-of-the-father?lang=eng)

Submission to God does not give another human being unlimited control over our choices. A willing mind includes thoughtful discernment, honesty about responsibilities, and a response rooted in faith rather than coercion.

## A day of sacrifice

Verse 23 describes a day of sacrifice and tithing before the coming of the Son of Man. Read this within the early Saints' efforts to establish Zion. The later revelation in D&C 119:1-4 gives more specific instruction about tithing; do not assume every use of the term in 1831 describes exactly the later administrative practice.

[Read D&C 119:1-4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/119?lang=eng&id=p1-p4#p1)

For present-day disciples, faithful stewardship can include tithing, generosity, time, skills, and care for people in need. These commitments should not become a competition over who can give the most. A person's income or visible sacrifice does not measure the worth of their heart.

The warning about burning at the Lord's coming is not a promise of immunity from house fires or other present-day disasters. Giving is not a transaction that purchases safety or a guaranteed financial return.

## Questions to sit with

> Can my heart sacrifice to bless others?

- What do the different instructions to Morley and Williams teach us about comparing sacrifices?
- What is the difference between giving something and giving it willingly?
- How might tithing and other forms of stewardship express a relationship with the Lord rather than a transaction?
- What could you offer sustainably, with attention to your responsibilities and the needs around you?
