# D&C 64:1-13 - Forgive One Another

## Mercy received and mercy offered

The Lord speaks of His own forgiveness before requiring the Saints to forgive one another. He knows the need for repentance within the community; forgiveness is not an instruction given only to people who have never needed mercy themselves.

> 10. "I, the Lord, will forgive whom I will forgive, but of you it is required to forgive all men."

D&C 64:10.

[Read D&C 64:1-11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/64?lang=eng&id=p1-p11#p1)

Verse 9's warning about the greater sin is deliberately serious. Use it first as an invitation to examine our own willingness to receive the Lord's help, not as a weapon for telling an injured person that their pain makes them worse than the person who harmed them.

## Let God judge

Verse 11 directs the heart toward God's judgment. Relinquishing personal revenge does not mean declaring that nothing wrong happened. Nor does it require us to control when painful memories or emotions return.

Forgiveness may involve a process of prayer, support, and repeated choices. The command does not supply a deadline by which grief must disappear. We can seek the Savior's help while being honest about where we are.

## Dieter F. Uchtdorf - The Merciful Obtain Mercy

**Direct quotation: D&C 64:10.** President Uchtdorf, then Second Counselor in the First Presidency, quotes the requirement to forgive all and challenges hatred, gossip, and destructive judgment.

His invitation to stop these patterns belongs beside his teaching about receiving God's love and allowing it to change our treatment of others. It is not permission to dismiss the complexity of recovering from serious harm.

["The Merciful Obtain Mercy," April 2012](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2012/04/the-merciful-obtain-mercy?lang=eng)

## Forgiveness and accountability can coexist

Read verses 12-13 rather than stopping at verse 10. The revelation includes a process for addressing unrepented wrongdoing and explicitly distinguishes that process from a lack of compassion.

[Read D&C 64:12-13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/64?lang=eng&id=p12-p13#p12)

This is an ecclesiastical instruction, not a complete modern safeguarding policy. It shows that mercy does not erase accountability. Forgiveness does not require renewed contact, restored access, immediate trust, or remaining in danger. Appropriate boundaries, reporting, and legal or professional help can remain necessary.

## Questions to sit with

> Is my heart willing to seek help in forgiving others?

- How does remembering the Lord's mercy toward us shape this commandment?
- What is the difference between releasing revenge and excusing wrongdoing?
- How do verses 12-13 broaden the way we discuss forgiveness?
- What is one prayer or next step you could take privately, without forcing yourself to pretend you are already healed?

Do not ask class members to identify an offender or disclose a painful experience publicly.
