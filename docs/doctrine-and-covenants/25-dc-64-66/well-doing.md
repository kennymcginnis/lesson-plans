# D&C 64:29-34 - Be Not Weary in Well-Doing

## Providing for people is the Lord's business

Verses 29-30 address agents charged with providing for the Saints. Practical arrangements and temporal needs are part of the Lord's work, not distractions from it.

[Read D&C 64:29-34](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/64?lang=eng&id=p29-p34#p29)

This can expand our picture of service. Preparing a meal, making a plan, keeping an agreement, or helping someone access what they need can matter alongside public teaching and visible leadership.

## Small things and the Lord's timing

> "But all things must come to pass in their time."

D&C 64:32.

> "Wherefore, be not weary in well-doing, for ye are laying the foundation of a great work. And out of small things proceedeth that which is great."

D&C 64:33.

Read the encouragement alongside the work already assigned. Patience does not mean doing nothing, and action does not mean controlling the timetable. We may be contributing to a foundation whose full result we will not see immediately.

The vision of Zion at the end of the section widens the horizon beyond one task. The Lord speaks of a community that flourishes and draws people from the nations.

[Read D&C 64:41-43](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/64?lang=eng&id=p41-p43#p41)

## Perseverance without pretending to be limitless

Becoming tired is not the same as abandoning goodness. Rest, medical care, shared responsibilities, and realistic boundaries can help us continue. The verse encourages faithful persistence; it does not condemn human limits.

Choose a small practice that can actually be sustained. Repairing a relationship, keeping a covenant, or helping a neighbor often involves repeated ordinary decisions rather than one dramatic gesture.

## Questions to sit with

> Can my heart persevere in well-doing?

- What small work has someone done consistently that made a difference to you?
- How does verse 32 change the way we hear verse 33?
- What would patience look like without becoming passivity?
- What support or adjustment would help you continue doing good without exhausting yourself?
