# D&C 64:6-8, 16 - A Heart That Seeks Good

## Start with the Lord's compassion

D&C 64 begins with the Lord's desire that His people overcome the world and His promise of compassion. Correction comes within that invitation, not as proof that the Saints are beyond His help.

[Read D&C 64:1-8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/64?lang=eng&id=p1-p8#p1)

Verse 6 describes people seeking occasion against Joseph Smith without cause. Verse 7 immediately acknowledges that he has sinned and speaks of confession and forgiveness. Read the two together: rejecting an unfounded accusation is not the same as claiming a leader cannot do wrong.

## Seeking occasion

Verse 8 recalls disciples who sought occasion against one another and did not forgive in their hearts. Verse 16 describes condemning as evil something in which there was no evil.

[Read D&C 64:15-17](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/64?lang=eng&id=p15-p17#p15)

The heart question is whether we are looking for a reason to condemn. Sometimes we treat uncertainty about another person's motives as permission to assume the worst. Repeating an unverified accusation, exaggerating a mistake, or enjoying someone's embarrassment can become habits of the heart.

This is different from identifying genuine harm. Reporting abuse, asking accountable questions, and correcting an error are not automatically fault-finding. Charity does not ask us to disregard evidence or silence someone who needs protection.

## Practice a different response

Before passing along a criticism, ask what you actually know, what you are assuming, and whether the conversation will help someone. Where appropriate and safe, seek clarification directly rather than building a case from fragments.

We can name an action honestly without claiming complete knowledge of a person's heart. We can also notice good without making praise a way to avoid a necessary difficult conversation.

## Questions to sit with

> Does my heart seek occasion or evil in others?

- Why is it important to read verses 6 and 7 together?
- What is the difference between discernment and looking for something to condemn?
- What could we do instead of rehearsing a story about someone's bad motives?
- What change in our conversations could make our community more truthful and merciful?
