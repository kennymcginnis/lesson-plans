## Scripture

> 105. Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path. (119:105)

**Not the whole road. Just the next step.**

### Living by Scriptural Guidance

President Russell M. Nelson shared this teaching in his general conference address, "Living by Scriptural Guidance."  He explained how turning to scriptural road maps helps people recognize errors, make corrections, and stay on the path leading to eternal life.

> If our journey through life is to be successful, we need to follow divine direction. The Lord said, 'Look unto me in every thought; doubt not, fear not' [Doctrine and Covenants 6:36]. And the Psalmist wrote, 'Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path' [Psalm 119:105].”


Psalm 119 is 176 verses long and keeps returning to one idea: **your life
is a journey back to Heavenly Father, and His word is what keeps you on
the path.**

## Built into the Hebrew alphabet

It's 176 verses because it's an acrostic: 22 Hebrew letters × 8 verses
each. Every eight-verse block opens with the same Hebrew letter — aleph,
then beth, and so on. Easy to miss in English, but it may have doubled
as a way to teach children both God's law and their ABCs at once.

### Seek Christ in Every Thought

Elder Ulisses Soares also cited Psalm 119:2 in "Seek Christ in Every
Thought" (Oct. 2020) — the same talk that draws on Psalm 139:1–3.

> Being aware of all that is necessary for us in this life, the
> Savior invites us to seek Him in every thought and to follow Him
> with all our heart.
>
> 2. Blessed are they that keep his testimonies, and that seek him with
> the whole heart. (119:2)

You don't need to know your whole future to walk with Him — you need to
know your next right step, and scripture is what lights it.
