# Psalms 134–136 — No Idol Compares

## Scripture

> They have mouths, but they speak not: eyes have they, but they see not;
> They have ears, but they hear not: neither is there any breath in their
> mouths.
> They that make them are like unto them: so is every one that trusteth
> in them. (135:16–18)

## Make a list — like the psalmists did

Make a list — like the psalmists did — of things the Lord has actually
done for you.

**Teaching notes**

Why is it foolish to trust in a false god? Because it can't see, hear,
act, or save. Ask: "What might you be tempted to trust in that functions
like an idol — looks powerful but can't actually do anything for you?"

Simple activity, no props: everyone lists a few real things the Lord has
done for them this year, out loud or on paper.

## A refrain meant to be sung, not just read

> O give thanks unto the Lord; for he is good: for his mercy endureth
> for ever. (136:1)

Psalm 136 repeats "for his mercy endureth for ever" after every single
line. Read silently, it feels repetitive. Sung congregationally — like a
hymn's recurring "Alleluia" — it becomes something else.

> They sang together by course... because he is good, for his mercy
> endureth for ever. (Ezra 3:11)

Quite possibly describing this very psalm being sung.

**Teaching notes**

Read a few verses flat first, then read them again with everyone
answering "for his mercy endureth for ever" out loud after each line.
Notice the difference.
