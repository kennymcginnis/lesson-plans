# Psalm 116 — I Love the Lord Because...

## Scripture

> 1. I love the Lord, because he hath heard my voice and my supplications. (116:1)
>
> 2. For thou hast delivered my soul from death, mine eyes from tears, and my feet from falling. (116:8)

## Writing a psalm is as simple as four words

"I love the Lord" — followed by "because," and then your list.

Take 60 seconds, quietly, to write their own list:

### "I love the Lord because \_\_\_."

## Addicted to appreciation

Not "I'll be grateful when something big happens." Grateful for legs. For an alarm clock. For eggs for breakfast. For a
window to look out of.

**I love the Lord because \_\_\_.**

The small, ordinary things count. If you want a praiseworthy day, write those four words and fill in the blank — today,
and see what you notice.

## Precious in the sight of the Lord

> Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints. (116:15)

**This verse isn't about death being sad — it's about death being safe. Because of the Resurrection, death is a doorway
home, not an ending. That's why prophets and apostles keep returning to this verse: it reframes death as something God
cares tenderly about, not something to dread.**

### President Russell M. Nelson - "Decisions for Eternity"

- President Russell M. Nelson quoted this verse in "Decisions for Eternity" (Oct. 2013), teaching that death is precious
  because it is a "homecoming" for the Saint. He'd just finished calling the human body one of God's priceless gifts,
  and framed death the same way:

  > The aging process is also a gift from God, as is death. The eventual death of your mortal body is essential to God's
  > great plan of happiness. Why? Because death will allow your spirit to return home to Him. From an eternal
  > perspective, death is only premature for those who are not prepared to meet God.
  >
  > The Psalmist wrote, "Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints." Death is precious because it is
  > a "homecoming" for the Saint with the Lord.

- He'd quoted the same verse in "Doors of Death" (Apr. 1992):
  > Such was the vision of the psalmist who wrote, "Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints."
- and in "We Are Children of God" (Oct. 1998):
  > The Psalmist expressed the viewpoint of Deity: "Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints."

### Elder Bruce R. McConkie - "The Dead Who Die in the Lord"

- Elder Bruce R. McConkie built an entire talk around this verse in "The Dead Who Die in the Lord" (Oct. 1976). He
  opened by naming the fear head-on, then turned it toward hope:

  > For a text I take these sweet and consoling words of biblical origin: "Precious in the sight of the Lord is the
  > death of his saints." ... Death can be comforting and sweet and precious, or it can thrust upon us all the agonies
  > and sulphurous burnings of an endless hell. And we — each of us individually — make the choice as to which it shall
  > be.

  He closed the thought this way:

  > Is it any wonder that the scriptures say: "Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints"? Truly such
  > is precious, wondrous, and glorious, for when the saints die, added souls have assured themselves of exaltation with
  > Him who provided the way for them to advance and progress and become like Him.

## What shall I render unto the Lord?

> What shall I render unto the Lord for all his benefits toward me? (116:12)

Elder Dieter F. Uchtdorf pointed to this verse in "O How Great the Plan of Our God!" (Oct. 2016) — the same question
this whole psalm is answering. He tied it to a motto he'd seen on a trip to Belfast: "Pro tanto quid retribuamus" —
"What shall we give in return for so much?"

> My beloved brothers and sisters, my dear friends, does it not fill our hearts and minds with wonder and awe to
> contemplate the great plan of happiness our Heavenly Father has prepared for us? Does it not fill us with unspeakable
> joy to know of the glorious future that is prepared for all who wait upon the Lord? ... I invite each of us to
> consider this question. What shall we give in return for the flood of light and truth God has poured out upon us?

His answer: "Our beloved Father simply asks that we live by the truth we have received and that we follow the path He
has provided." Same question the Psalmist asked 3,000 years earlier — same answer, too.
