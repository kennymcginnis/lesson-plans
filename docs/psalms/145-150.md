# Psalms 145–150 — Let Everything That Hath Breath Praise the Lord

## Scripture

> Every day will I bless thee, and I will praise thy name for ever and
> ever. (145:2)
>
> Happy is he... whose hope is in the Lord his God. (146:5)
>
> Let every thing that hath breath praise the Lord. (150:6)

## Before the praise: prayers against the natural man

> Deliver me, O Lord, from the evil man: preserve me from the violent
> man... which have purposed to overthrow my goings. (140:1, 4)
>
> Cause me to know the way wherein I should walk... Teach me to do thy
> will. (143:8, 10)

Right before these five hallelujah psalms, David prays to be delivered
from "the violent man" — his own natural man, purposed to overthrow his
walk. The praise of 146–150 doesn't come from nowhere; it comes after
asking to be delivered from yourself.

## That's a bold thing to ask for

Everything that has breath, every day, forever. Who is worthy of that
much praise, from that many people, for that long?

### The Tender Mercies of the Lord

> 9. The Lord is good to all: and his tender mercies are over all his works.
>
>
> 16. Thou openest thine hand, and satisfiest the desire of every living thing.
>
>
> 18. The Lord is nigh unto all them that call upon him, to all that call upon him in truth. (145:9, 16, 18)

Elder David A. Bednar quoted this exact verse — "the Lord is good to
all: and his tender mercies are over all his works" — in "The Tender
Mercies of the Lord" (Apr. 2005), making it central to his testimony of
God's tender mercies in the latter days.

Your own story goes here: a time you found God's goodness somewhere you
didn't expect it — a remote village, a hard season, an ordinary Tuesday.
Keep it to 90 seconds, specific, no moral tacked on at the end. The
point: anywhere you go, you can find His goodness there.

### Who is worthy of that much praise?

> 2. He gathereth together the outcasts... </br>
> 3. He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds... </br>
> 4. He telleth the number of the stars; he calleth them all by their names... </br>
> 5. Great is our Lord, and of great power: his understanding is infinite. (147:2–5)

### He will heal your wounded soul.

Every person alive has, at some point, experienced one of these: felt
like an outcast, felt broken, needed binding up, needed to be known and
called by name.

> He will heal your wounded soul. </br>
> As you yoke yourself to Him, your burdens will feel lighter.  </br>
> If you will make and keep covenants to follow Jesus Christ, you will find that the painful moments of your life are temporary. </br>
> Your afflictions will be swallowed up in the joy of Christ. </br>
> — President Russell M. Nelson, "The Lord Jesus Christ Will Come </br>
> Again" (Oct. 2024) — footnoted to this same verse, Psalm 147:3

### He Knows You by Name

> He not only knows the names of all the stars (see Psalm 147:4)...  </br>
> He knows your names and all your heartaches and your joys! </br>
> — Elder Neal A. Maxwell, quoted by Elaine S. Dalton in "He Knows You
> by Name" (Apr. 2005)

### Simple daily practice, no props: tonight, look back over today and try to find one of these </br>— a moment you felt welcomed, healed, known, or seen. That's the answer to "who is worthy"
