# Psalms 110 & 118 — Pointing to Christ

## Scripture

> The Lord said unto my Lord, Sit thou at my right hand. (110:1)
>
> Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek. (110:4)
>
> The stone which the builders refused is become the head stone of the
> corner. (118:22)

## Jesus quoted both of these psalms about Himself

Ps 110:1 is what Jesus used to stump the Pharisees about whose son the
Christ is (Matthew 22). Ps 110:4 is why Hebrews calls Him a priest after
the order of Melchizedek. Ps 118:22 is the rejected cornerstone Jesus
applied to Himself during His last week — and the crowd echoed 118:25–26
("Hosanna... blessed is he that cometh") at the triumphal entry.

These aren't just poetry. They're prophecy, and Jesus knew it.

> The Lord hath sworn, and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever
> after the order of Melchizedek. (110:4)

Elder Bruce R. McConkie quoted this verse in "The Ten Blessings of the
Priesthood" (Oct. 1977), applying it to the oath and covenant of the
Melchizedek Priesthood. Elder Dale G. Renlund cited Psalm 110:1, 4 in a
doctrinal discussion of that same covenant in "The Priesthood and the
Savior's Atoning Power" (Oct. 2017).

## This is the day

> This is the day which the Lord hath made; we will rejoice and be glad
> in it. (118:24)

A verse that stands fine on its own — no explanation needed. Elder
Quentin L. Cook closed his talk "Rejoice!" (Oct. 1996) by quoting this
same verse.

## Save now — the cry behind Hosanna

> Save now, I beseech thee, O Lord... send now prosperity. (118:25)

Elder Gerrit W. Gong called this "the full expression of the
traditional chiastic Messianic plea" in "Hosanna and Hallelujah — The
Living Jesus Christ" (Apr. 2020). That's the cry behind "Hosanna" at
the triumphal entry — still what we're pleading when we say it today.
