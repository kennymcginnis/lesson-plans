# Psalms 110 & 118 — Pointing to Christ

## Psalms 110

> 1. The Lord said unto my Lord, Sit thou at my right hand. (110:1)
>
> 2. Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek. (110:4)

## Psalms 118

> 22. **The stone which the builders refused is become the head stone of the corner. (118:22)**

## Jesus quoted both of these psalms about Himself

Ps 110:1 is what Jesus used to stump the Pharisees about whose son the Christ is (Matthew 22).

Ps 110:4 is why Hebrews calls Him a priest after the order of Melchizedek.

Ps 118:22 is the rejected cornerstone Jesus applied to Himself during His last week.

> 26. Blessed be he that cometh in the name of the Lord... Hosanna. (118:26)

The crowd echoed this at the triumphal entry.

These aren't just poetry. They're prophecy, and Jesus knew it.

> 4. The Lord hath sworn, and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek. (110:4)

### The Ten Blessings of the Priesthood

Elder Bruce R. McConkie quoted this verse in "The Ten Blessings of the Priesthood" (Oct. 1977), applying it to the oath
and covenant of the Melchizedek Priesthood.

> The Lord on his part covenants to give such faithful persons "all that my Father hath," which is eternal life in the
> kingdom of God... Then the Lord — to show the binding nature of his promise — swears with an oath that the promised
> reward shall be obtained. This oath, as it pertained to the Son of God himself, is spoken of in these words: "The Lord
> hath sworn, and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek."

### The Priesthood and the Savior's Atoning Power

Elder Dale G. Renlund cited Psalm 110:1, 4 in a doctrinal discussion of that same covenant in "The Priesthood and the
Savior's Atoning Power" (Oct. 2017).

> To each man who receives the Melchizedek Priesthood, God affirms His covenant promises with an oath... it is God who
> swears the oath, not the priesthood holder... an oath, employing the most forceful language He can, to assure us of
> the binding and irreversible nature of His promises.

The religious leaders rejected the cornerstone — not because they didn't believe in God, but because they didn't like
what He asked of them.

## This is the day

> 24. This is the day which the Lord hath made; we will rejoice and be glad in it. (118:24)

> A verse that stands fine on its own — no explanation needed. Elder Quentin L. Cook closed his talk "Rejoice!"
> (Oct. 1996) by quoting this same verse.

> Let us rejoice in the promise that is ours through the Atonement of the Savior and through Christlike living adhere to
> the counsel of the Psalmist: "This is the day which the Lord hath made; we will rejoice and be glad in it."

Fun aside: this verse was Eric Huntsman's signature sign-off on the old Mormon Channel podcast _Mormon Identities_

## Save now — the cry behind Hosanna

> 25. Save now, I beseech thee, O Lord... send now prosperity. (118:25)

Elder Gerrit W. Gong called this "the full expression of the traditional chiastic Messianic plea" in "Hosanna and
Hallelujah — The Living Jesus Christ" (Apr. 2020). That's the cry behind "Hosanna" at the triumphal entry — still what
we're pleading when we say it today.

> From Old Testament times, the waving of palm branches accompanied the chanting of "Save now, I beseech thee, O Lord."
> Psalm 118:25 is the full expression of the traditional chiastic Messianic plea: "Save now, I beseech thee, O Lord: O
> Lord, I beseech thee, send now prosperity."
